# 02 — Past Simple vs. Present Perfect

> **TOEIC parts:** Part 5, Part 6 · **Priority:** ★★★★★

## 1. The rule

| Tense | Form | Use | Signal words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Past simple** | V-ed / V2 | finished action at a **specific past time** | yesterday, last week/year, ago, in 2019, when…, at that time |
| **Present perfect** | has/have + V3 | past → now; result now; unspecified time | **since, for, recently, lately, already, yet, so far, just, over the past/last (+ time), ever, never** |
| **Present perfect continuous** | has/have been + V-ing | ongoing activity up to now, emphasizes duration | for, since, all day |

- *Sales **rose** by 10% **last quarter**.*
- *Sales **have risen** by 10% **since** January.*
- ***Over the past five years**, the firm **has opened** 12 branches.*
- *She **has been working** here **for** a decade.*

## 2. since vs. for

- **since** + point in time: *since 2020, since Monday, since she joined*
- **for** + period: *for three years, for a long time*

## 3. The key trap

❌ *We **have met** him **yesterday**.* → ✓ *We **met** him yesterday.*
A specific finished time (**ago, last, in + year**) **forbids** the present perfect.

## 4. TOEIC-style question

> Since its founding in 1998, Harlow Engineering ------- more than 40 bridges across the region.
> (A) designs  (B) designed  (C) has designed  (D) will design

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(C) has designed.** *Since + past point* → **present perfect** (from 1998 until now).
</details>

## 5. Practice

1. The board ------- the merger proposal two weeks ago.
   (A) has approved (B) approved (C) approves (D) has been approving
2. Mr. Kim has worked in the finance department ------- six years.
   (A) since (B) for (C) during (D) ago
3. The number of online orders ------- steadily over the last few months.
   (A) increase (B) have increased (C) has increased (D) increasing

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **(B)**: *ago* means past simple.
2. **(B)**: *for* + period.
3. **(C)**: *over the last few months* means present perfect; *the number of* takes a singular verb.
</details>
