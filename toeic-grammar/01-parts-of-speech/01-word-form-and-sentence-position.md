# 01 — Word Form: Choosing Noun, Verb, Adjective or Adverb by Position

> **TOEIC parts:** Part 5, Part 6 · **Priority:** ★★★★★ (about 25–30% of Part 5 questions)
> **Why it matters for 650–700:** These are the quickest points on the test. You can often answer
> **without reading the whole sentence**: just look at the words around the blank.

## 1. The rule

All four options come from the **same word family** (e.g. *success / succeed / successful / successfully*).
Pick the option by its **job in the sentence**, not by its meaning.

| Slot (what is around the blank) | Part of speech needed | Example |
|---|---|---|
| after **a / an / the / this / my / its**, before a verb | **Noun** | *the **approval** of the budget* |
| after an **adjective** | **Noun** | *a quick **response*** |
| after a **preposition** | **Noun** (or V-ing) | *for **consideration*** |
| between **article and noun** | **Adjective** | *a **reliable** supplier* |
| after **be / become / seem / remain / stay** | **Adjective** | *The results remain **impressive**.* |
| after a **subject**, with no other verb in the sentence | **Verb** | *The manager **approved** the plan.* |
| before / after a **verb**, or before an **adjective** | **Adverb** | *She **quickly** replied.* / ***extremely** busy* |
| between **auxiliary and main verb** (has ___ completed) | **Adverb** | *has **successfully** completed* |
| at the **start of a sentence + comma** | **Adverb** | ***Unfortunately**, the event was cancelled.* |

## 2. Suffixes: identify the part of speech fast

| Noun | Verb | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|---|
| -tion, -sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ance, -ence, -er/-or (person), -ist, -ship, -al (approval, arrival, proposal) | -ize, -ify, -ate, -en | -ful, -less, -ous, -ive, -able/-ible, -al, -ic, -ent/-ant, -y | -ly (almost always) |

⚠️ Traps: *-ly* adjectives: **friendly, costly, timely, lively, likely, orderly** (they are **not** adverbs).
⚠️ *-al* can be a noun (**approval, proposal, arrival, rental, renewal**) or an adjective (**annual, financial**).

## 3. Examples

- The **expansion** of the factory will create 200 jobs. *(noun: subject after "the")*
- Ms. Tanaka gave a **persuasive** presentation. *(adjective: between "a" and noun)*
- The software update was **successfully** installed. *(adverb: between "was" and participle)*
- All applicants must **submit** two references. *(verb: after modal)*
- The new policy seems **effective**. *(adjective: after linking verb "seems")*

## 4. Common traps

1. **Person noun vs. thing noun.** *an **applicant*** (person) vs. *an **application*** (thing). Check the meaning: *The **applicant** was interviewed* / *The **application** was rejected.*
2. **Countable noun needs an article or plural.** *We received ~~application~~ → **applications** / **an application**.*
3. **Adverb before an adjective, not an adjective.** *an ~~extreme~~ → **extremely** difficult task.*
4. **Adjective after be, not adverb.** *The instructions are ~~clearly~~ → **clear**.*

## 5. TOEIC-style question (a pattern that appears on almost every real test)

> The marketing team's ------- of the survey results took longer than expected.
> (A) analyze  (B) analytical  (C) analysis  (D) analytically

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(C) analysis.** After the possessive *team's*, and followed by *of*, the blank needs a **noun**.
Shortcut: *possessive + ___ + of* → noun, every time.
</details>

## 6. Practice

1. The new printer is ------- faster than the old model.
   (A) consider (B) considerable (C) considerably (D) consideration
2. Please read the safety instructions ------- before operating the machine.
   (A) care (B) careful (C) carefully (D) carefulness
3. Mr. Patel was praised for his ------- to the project.
   (A) dedicated (B) dedicate (C) dedicating (D) dedication

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **(C)**: an adverb modifies the comparative adjective *faster*.
2. **(C)**: an adverb modifies the verb *read*.
3. **(D)**: after the possessive *his* comes a noun (*dedication to*).
</details>
