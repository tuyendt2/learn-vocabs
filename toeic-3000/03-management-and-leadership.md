# 03 — Management & Leadership

### oversee
- **IPA:** /ˌəʊvəˈsiː/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To watch over and be responsible for work or a process.
  - *Example:* She **oversees** three teams across two sites.

### supervise
- **IPA:** /ˈsuːpəvaɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To direct and check the work of a person or group.
  - *Example:* A senior nurse **supervises** the night shift.

### coordinate
- **IPA:** /kəʊˈɔːdɪneɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To organise people or activities so they work together well.
  - *Example:* He **coordinates** the [rollout](05-business-strategy-and-planning.md#rollout) across all branches.

### prioritize
- **IPA:** /praɪˈɒrətaɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To decide which tasks are most important and deal with them first.
  - *Example:* We had to **prioritize** safety fixes over new [features](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#feature).

### empower
- **IPA:** /ɪmˈpaʊə/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To give someone the authority or confidence to act on their own.
  - *Example:* The policy **empowers** staff to refund small amounts without [approval](06-projects-and-deadlines.md#approval).

### motivate
- **IPA:** /ˈməʊtɪveɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To make someone want to work hard or achieve something.
  - *Example:* Clear goals **motivate** the team more than bonuses do.

### inspire
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈspaɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To fill someone with the desire or confidence to do something.
  - *Example:* Her calm [handling](../toeic-600/11-logistics-shipping-and-warehousing.md#17-handling) of the crisis **inspired** the whole [department](31-government-and-public-services.md#department).

### appraise
- **IPA:** /əˈpreɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To assess the quality or performance of someone or something.
  - *Example:* Managers **appraise** each report writer once a year.

### evaluate
- **IPA:** /ɪˈvæljueɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To judge the value, quality, or importance of something carefully.
  - *Example:* We **evaluate** every [supplier](../toeic-600/09-sales-and-purchasing.md#2-supplier) on price, speed, and quality.

### discipline
- **IPA:** /ˈdɪsəplɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To punish an employee formally for breaking rules.
  - *Example:* Two workers were **disciplined** for ignoring safety signs.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** Controlled, orderly behaviour and the training that produces it.
  - *Example:* [Meeting](../toeic-600/06-meetings-and-presentations.md#1-meeting) [deadlines](../toeic-600/01-contracts-and-agreements.md#13-deadline) takes **discipline**.

### reprimand
- **IPA:** /ˈreprɪmɑːnd/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To tell someone officially that they have done something wrong.
  - *Example:* He was **reprimanded** for missing the client call.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A formal expression of disapproval.
  - *Example:* She received a written **reprimand**.

### commend
- **IPA:** /kəˈmend/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To praise someone formally or publicly.
  - *Example:* The director **commended** the team for the quick [turnaround](../toeic-600/20-customer-service-and-complaints.md#24-turnaround).

### brief
- **IPA:** /briːf/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To give someone the information they need before a task.
  - *Example:* The pilot **briefed** the crew before [departure](../toeic-600/14-travel-and-transportation.md#3-departure).
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A set of instructions explaining a task or job.
  - *Example:* The design **brief** asked for a simple, low-cost package.
- **Meaning 3 (adj):** Lasting a short time; using few words.
  - *Example:* She gave a **brief** [summary](07-data-reports-and-analysis.md#summary) and took questions.

### debrief
- **IPA:** /ˌdiːˈbriːf/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To question someone after a task to gather information about it.
  - *Example:* The team was **debriefed** after the trade show.

### authorize
- **IPA:** /ˈɔːθəraɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To give official permission for something.
  - *Example:* Only a manager can **authorize** overtime.

### sign off
- **IPA:** /ˌsaɪn ˈɒf/
- **Meaning 1 (phr v):** To give final approval to something.
  - *Example:* Finance must **sign off** on the budget before work starts.

### approve
- **IPA:** /əˈpruːv/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To officially agree to or accept something.
  - *Example:* The board **approved** the plan without changes.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To think that someone or something is good or acceptable.
  - *Example:* Not everyone **approves** of the new dress code.

### endorse
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈdɔːs/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To publicly declare support for a person, plan, or product.
  - *Example:* The union **endorsed** the revised pay offer.

### ratify
- **IPA:** /ˈrætɪfaɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To make an agreement or decision official by formal approval.
  - *Example:* Members will **ratify** the new rules at the annual [meeting](../toeic-600/06-meetings-and-presentations.md#1-meeting).

### veto
- **IPA:** /ˈviːtəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To reject or block a decision using official power.
  - *Example:* The chair **vetoed** the [proposal](../toeic-600/06-meetings-and-presentations.md#13-proposal) to cut training.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The right to reject a decision.
  - *Example:* The [founder](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#founder) still holds a **veto** over major [spending](35-shopping-and-consumer-life.md#spending).

### direct
- **IPA:** /dəˈrekt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To manage or guide the work of a group or project.
  - *Example:* She **directs** the customer [support](04-teamwork-and-workplace-relationships.md#support) operation.
- **Meaning 2 (adj):** Going straight from one point to another without interruption.
  - *Example:* There is now a **direct** [reporting line](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#reporting-line) to the CEO.

### govern
- **IPA:** /ˈɡʌvn/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To officially control and run an organisation or country.
  - *Example:* A small [council](31-government-and-public-services.md#council) **governs** the trade association.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To control or strongly influence how something works.
  - *Example:* Strict rules **govern** how the data may be used.

### steer
- **IPA:** /stɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To guide the direction of a group, plan, or discussion.
  - *Example:* He **steered** the [meeting](../toeic-600/06-meetings-and-presentations.md#1-meeting) back to the main issue.

### chair
- **IPA:** /tʃeə/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To lead a meeting or committee.
  - *Example:* She will **chair** the safety review.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The person in charge of a meeting or organisation.
  - *Example:* The **chair** called for a vote.

### preside
- **IPA:** /prɪˈzaɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To be in the position of authority at a meeting or event.
  - *Example:* A judge **presided** over the [hearing](14-law-and-compliance.md#hearing).

### lead by example
- **IPA:** /ˌliːd baɪ ɪɡˈzɑːmpl/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** To behave in the way you want others to behave.
  - *Example:* She **leads by example**, arriving early and staying focused.

### hands-on
- **IPA:** /ˌhændz ˈɒn/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Involving direct, practical involvement in the work.
  - *Example:* He takes a **hands-on** approach on the [shop floor](17-manufacturing-and-engineering.md#shop-floor).

### hands-off
- **IPA:** /ˌhændz ˈɒf/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Not involving direct control; leaving others to work freely.
  - *Example:* Her **hands-off** style suits [experienced](../toeic-600/04-human-resources-and-recruitment.md#8-experience) staff.

### micromanage
- **IPA:** /ˈmaɪkrəʊmænɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To control every small part of a person's work too closely.
  - *Example:* Staff resent being **micromanaged** on routine tasks.

### accountability
- **IPA:** /əˌkaʊntəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The state of being responsible for decisions and having to explain them.
  - *Example:* Clear roles improve **accountability** when things go wrong.

### oversight
- **IPA:** /ˈəʊvəsaɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of watching over a process to make sure it is done properly.
  - *Example:* A [committee](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#committee) provides **oversight** of the budget.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A mistake caused by not noticing something.
  - *Example:* The missing [signature](../toeic-600/01-contracts-and-agreements.md#12-signature) was an **oversight**, not a refusal.

### initiative
- **IPA:** /ɪˈnɪʃətɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ability to act and make decisions without being told to.
  - *Example:* She showed **initiative** by fixing the process herself.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A new plan or action to solve a problem or improve something.
  - *Example:* The [recycling](28-environment-and-sustainability.md#recycling) **initiative** cut waste by a third.

### vision
- **IPA:** /ˈvɪʒn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A clear idea of what an organisation should become in the future.
  - *Example:* The founder's **vision** was affordable tools for small farms.

### foresight
- **IPA:** /ˈfɔːsaɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ability to predict and prepare for future needs or problems.
  - *Example:* It took **foresight** to buy the land before prices rose.

### decisiveness
- **IPA:** /dɪˈsaɪsɪvnəs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ability to make decisions quickly and confidently.
  - *Example:* The crisis called for **decisiveness**, not more [meetings](../toeic-600/06-meetings-and-presentations.md#1-meeting).

### integrity
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈteɡrəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The quality of being honest and having strong moral principles.
  - *Example:* Clients trust her because of her **integrity**.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The state of being whole and undamaged.
  - *Example:* Engineers checked the **integrity** of the bridge [supports](04-teamwork-and-workplace-relationships.md#support).

### credibility
- **IPA:** /ˌkredəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The quality of being believed and trusted.
  - *Example:* Missing two [deadlines](../toeic-600/01-contracts-and-agreements.md#13-deadline) damaged his **credibility**.

### charisma
- **IPA:** /kəˈrɪzmə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A natural ability to attract, charm, and influence people.
  - *Example:* Her **charisma** kept the [audience](33-arts-culture-and-entertainment.md#audience) listening for an hour.

### delegation
- **IPA:** /ˌdelɪˈɡeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of giving tasks and authority to other people.
  - *Example:* Good **delegation** frees a manager to focus on planning.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A group of people chosen to represent others.
  - *Example:* A **delegation** from [head office](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#head-office) visited the plant.

### stewardship
- **IPA:** /ˈstjuːədʃɪp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The careful and responsible management of something entrusted to you.
  - *Example:* The trust praised her **stewardship** of its funds.

### succession
- **IPA:** /səkˈseʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of one person taking over a role from another.
  - *Example:* The board has a **succession** plan for the CEO role.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A number of people or things following one after another.
  - *Example:* A **succession** of delays pushed the launch to autumn.

### figurehead
- **IPA:** /ˈfɪɡəhed/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A leader who has the title but little real power.
  - *Example:* The chairman is really a **figurehead**; the CEO runs everything.

### spokesperson
- **IPA:** /ˈspəʊkspɜːsn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who speaks officially on behalf of a group or organisation.
  - *Example:* A company **spokesperson** confirmed the store closures.

### deputy
- **IPA:** /ˈdepjəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person immediately below a leader, who acts for them when needed.
  - *Example:* Her **deputy** ran the office while she was on leave.

### interim
- **IPA:** /ˈɪntərɪm/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Holding a position temporarily until a permanent choice is made.
  - *Example:* An **interim** manager was appointed for six months.

### acting
- **IPA:** /ˈæktɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Doing the duties of a job temporarily for someone else.
  - *Example:* He is **acting** head of [department](31-government-and-public-services.md#department) this term.

### superior
- **IPA:** /suːˈpɪəriə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person of higher rank or position at work.
  - *Example:* Raise the concern with your immediate **superior**.
- **Meaning 2 (adj):** Better in quality than something else.
  - *Example:* The new supplier's parts are **superior**.

### peer
- **IPA:** /pɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person of the same rank, age, or ability as another.
  - *Example:* New ideas are reviewed by a group of **peers**.

### directive
- **IPA:** /dəˈrektɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official instruction telling people what to do.
  - *Example:* A **directive** from [head office](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#head-office) banned personal use of the printers.

### buy-in
- **IPA:** /ˈbaɪ ɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Agreement and support for a plan from the people it affects.
  - *Example:* The change failed because it lacked staff **buy-in**.

### empowerment
- **IPA:** /ɪmˈpaʊəmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The giving of authority and confidence to people to act for themselves.
  - *Example:* Staff **empowerment** shortened response times at the desk.

### competency framework
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒmpɪtənsi ˌfreɪmwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A defined set of skills and behaviours used to assess and develop staff.
  - *Example:* Promotions are mapped against the **competency framework**.

### performance review
- **IPA:** /pəˈfɔːməns rɪˌvjuː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A regular formal meeting to discuss how well an employee is doing.
  - *Example:* Goals for next year are set in the **performance review**.

### one-on-one
- **IPA:** /ˌwʌn ɒn ˈwʌn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A private meeting between two people, usually a manager and a team member.
  - *Example:* She holds a weekly **one-on-one** with each direct report.

### check-in
- **IPA:** /ˈtʃek ɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short meeting or message to review progress and address issues.
  - *Example:* A quick daily **check-in** keeps the project [on track](06-projects-and-deadlines.md#on-track).

### follow-through
- **IPA:** /ˈfɒləʊ θruː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of completing something that has been started or promised.
  - *Example:* His ideas are good, but the **follow-through** is weak.

### mentorship
- **IPA:** /ˈmentɔːʃɪp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The guidance and support given by an experienced person to a less experienced one.
  - *Example:* A formal **mentorship** scheme pairs juniors with a senior mentor.

### sponsorship
- **IPA:** /ˈspɒnsəʃɪp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Active support of a person's career by a senior figure who promotes them.
  - *Example:* Her **sponsorship** by a director opened doors to bigger projects.

### decision-maker
- **IPA:** /dɪˈsɪʒn ˌmeɪkə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person with the authority to make important choices.
  - *Example:* We need the real **decision-maker** in the room to close the deal.

### leadership style
- **IPA:** /ˈliːdəʃɪp staɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The typical way a person leads and manages others.
  - *Example:* His **leadership style** is calm and consultative.

### authority
- **IPA:** /ɔːˈθɒrəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The power or right to give orders and make decisions.
  - *Example:* Team leaders have the **authority** to [approve](03-management-and-leadership.md#approve) small [purchases](../toeic-600/09-sales-and-purchasing.md#1-purchase).
- **Meaning 2 (n):** An official organisation with power in a particular area.
  - *Example:* The local **authority** must [approve](03-management-and-leadership.md#approve) the sign.
- **Meaning 3 (n):** A person with expert knowledge on a subject.
  - *Example:* She is a leading **authority** on employment law.

### team lead
- **IPA:** /ˈtiːm liːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The person responsible for guiding a small group's daily work.
  - *Example:* Ask the **team lead** which task to start with.

### role model
- **IPA:** /ˈrəʊl ˌmɒdl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person whose behaviour is copied by others.
  - *Example:* [Experienced](../toeic-600/04-human-resources-and-recruitment.md#8-experience) staff act as **role models** for new hires.

### track record
- **IPA:** /ˈtræk ˌrekɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person's or organisation's past performance and achievements.
  - *Example:* She has a strong **track record** of turning around weak teams.

### accountable
- **IPA:** /əˈkaʊntəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Required to explain and take responsibility for your actions.
  - *Example:* Each manager is **accountable** for their own budget.

### empathetic
- **IPA:** /ˌempəˈθetɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Able to understand and share the feelings of others.
  - *Example:* An **empathetic** manager notices when someone is struggling.

### approachable
- **IPA:** /əˈprəʊtʃəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Friendly and easy to talk to.
  - *Example:* Staff raise [problems](10-problem-solving-and-decision-making.md#problem) early because she is so **approachable**.

### visionary
- **IPA:** /ˈvɪʒənri/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Having clear, original ideas about the future.
  - *Example:* His **visionary** plan for electric delivery vans was ahead of its time.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A person with such ideas.
  - *Example:* The company was built by a **visionary** with little money.

### pragmatic
- **IPA:** /præɡˈmætɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Dealing with problems in a practical, realistic way.
  - *Example:* She took a **pragmatic** view and kept the parts that worked.

### delegate tasks
- **IPA:** /ˈdelɪɡeɪt tɑːsks/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** To hand out pieces of work to other people to do.
  - *Example:* Learning to **delegate tasks** was the hardest part of the promotion.

### managerial
- **IPA:** /ˌmænəˈdʒɪəriəl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to the work of a manager.
  - *Example:* The role carries **managerial** responsibility for eight people.

### people management
- **IPA:** /ˈpiːpl ˌmænɪdʒmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The skill of leading, supporting, and developing staff.
  - *Example:* Strong **people management** keeps morale high during change.

### command
- **IPA:** /kəˈmɑːnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Authority and control over a group of people.
  - *Example:* She took **command** of the response team within minutes.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To have authority over someone or something.
  - *Example:* The role **commands** respect across the whole plant.

### authority figure
- **IPA:** /ɔːˈθɒrəti ˌfɪɡə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person seen as having power or the right to give orders.
  - *Example:* New employees look to the shift leader as the **authority figure**.

### reward system
- **IPA:** /rɪˈwɔːd ˌsɪstəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A structured way of giving pay, bonuses, or recognition for good work.
  - *Example:* The **reward system** now includes non-cash prizes and extra leave.
