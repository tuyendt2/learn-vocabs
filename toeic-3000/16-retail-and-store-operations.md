# 16 — Retail & Store Operations

### retailer
- **IPA:** /ˈriːteɪlə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A business that sells goods to the public in small quantities.
  - *Example:* The **retailer** operates 200 stores across the country.

### storefront
- **IPA:** /ˈstɔːfrʌnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The front of a shop that faces the street, including its window and entrance.
  - *Example:* A fresh **storefront** and new signage lifted walk-in trade.

### outlet
- **IPA:** /ˈaʊtlet/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A shop or place where goods are sold, especially one selling a particular brand.
  - *Example:* The factory **outlet** sells last season's stock at a discount.

### chain store
- **IPA:** /ˈtʃeɪn stɔː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** One of a group of shops owned by the same company and run in the same way.
  - *Example:* Small grocers struggle to match the prices of the **chain stores**.

### department store
- **IPA:** /dɪˈpɑːtmənt stɔː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large shop divided into sections selling many kinds of goods.
  - *Example:* The **department store** has everything from furniture to cosmetics.

### boutique
- **IPA:** /buːˈtiːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small shop selling fashionable clothes or specialised goods.
  - *Example:* She runs a **boutique** selling handmade jewellery.

### kiosk
- **IPA:** /ˈkiːɒsk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small stand or booth where goods or services are sold.
  - *Example:* A **kiosk** near the entrance sells phone accessories.

### retail concession
- **IPA:** /ˈriːteɪl kənˌseʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The right to run a small business inside a larger store, or the space used for it.
  - *Example:* The brand operates a **retail concession** within the department store.

### shopfitting
- **IPA:** /ˈʃɒpfɪtɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The fitting out of a shop with counters, shelving, lighting, and displays.
  - *Example:* **Shopfitting** the new branch took three weeks before opening.

### aisle
- **IPA:** /aɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A passage between shelves in a shop.
  - *Example:* Cleaning products are in **aisle** seven.

### shelf
- **IPA:** /ʃelf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A flat board fixed horizontally for goods to stand on.
  - *Example:* Products at eye level on the **shelf** sell fastest.

### display
- **IPA:** /dɪˈspleɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An arrangement of goods intended to attract customers.
  - *Example:* The new **display** near the till lifted impulse sales.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To put something where people can see it.
  - *Example:* Seasonal items are **displayed** at the front of the store.

### fixture
- **IPA:** /ˈfɪkstʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A piece of shop equipment, such as a shelving unit or rail, fixed in place.
  - *Example:* New **fixtures** made the aisles feel wider.

### planogram
- **IPA:** /ˈplænəɡræm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A diagram showing exactly where each product should be placed on a shelf.
  - *Example:* Staff reset the aisle to match the new **planogram**.

### endcap
- **IPA:** /ˈendkæp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A display at the end of an aisle, used to promote particular products.
  - *Example:* The drinks on the **endcap** were part of a weekend deal.

### checkout
- **IPA:** /ˈtʃekaʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The place in a shop where customers pay for goods.
  - *Example:* Long queues at the **checkout** drive shoppers away.

### till
- **IPA:** /tɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A machine or drawer that holds money in a shop; a cash register.
  - *Example:* She counted the **till** at the end of her shift.

### cash register
- **IPA:** /ˈkæʃ ˌredʒɪstə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A machine that records sales and holds cash.
  - *Example:* The **cash register** prints a receipt for every sale.

### point of sale
- **IPA:** /ˌpɔɪnt əv ˈseɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The place and system where a purchase is completed.
  - *Example:* The new **point of sale** system tracks stock in real time.

### barcode
- **IPA:** /ˈbɑːkəʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A pattern of lines on a product that a machine reads to identify it and its price.
  - *Example:* A damaged **barcode** has to be keyed in by hand.

### footfall
- **IPA:** /ˈfʊtfɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The number of people entering a shop or area in a given period.
  - *Example:* **Footfall** rose 8% after the street was pedestrianised.

### foot traffic
- **IPA:** /ˈfʊt ˌtræfɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The movement of people on foot past or into a place of business.
  - *Example:* A corner site gets far more **foot traffic** than a side street.

### conversion rate
- **IPA:** /kənˈvɜːʒn reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The percentage of visitors who make a purchase.
  - *Example:* Better displays raised the **conversion rate** from 18% to 24%.

### basket size
- **IPA:** /ˈbɑːskɪt saɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The average value or number of items in a single purchase.
  - *Example:* Meal deals increased the average **basket size**.

### upsell
- **IPA:** /ˌʌpˈsel/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To persuade a customer to buy a more expensive item or add-on.
  - *Example:* Staff are trained to **upsell** a warranty with each laptop.

### cross-sell
- **IPA:** /ˌkrɒsˈsel/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To sell a related product to a customer who is already buying something.
  - *Example:* The till screen prompts staff to **cross-sell** batteries with toys.

### impulse buy
- **IPA:** /ˈɪmpʌls baɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A purchase made suddenly, without planning.
  - *Example:* Sweets by the till are classic **impulse buys**.

### loss leader
- **IPA:** /ˈlɒs ˌliːdə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A product sold cheaply, even at a loss, to attract customers into a shop.
  - *Example:* Milk is often a **loss leader** in supermarkets.

### markdown
- **IPA:** /ˈmɑːkdaʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reduction in the marked price of an item.
  - *Example:* End-of-season **markdowns** cleared the winter range.

### loyalty scheme
- **IPA:** /ˈlɔɪəlti skiːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A programme that rewards customers for shopping with the same retailer.
  - *Example:* The **loyalty scheme** gives one point for every pound spent.

### gift card
- **IPA:** /ˈɡɪft kɑːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A card loaded with money that can be spent at a particular shop.
  - *Example:* Unspent **gift card** balances are a small source of extra income.

### layaway
- **IPA:** /ˈleɪəweɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A system where a shop keeps goods for a customer who pays in instalments.
  - *Example:* She put the sofa on **layaway** and paid it off over three months.

### restocking fee
- **IPA:** /ˌriːˈstɒkɪŋ fiː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A charge for returning goods, to cover the cost of putting them back into stock.
  - *Example:* A 15% **restocking fee** applies to opened electronics.

### shrinkage
- **IPA:** /ˈʃrɪŋkɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The loss of stock through theft, damage, or error.
  - *Example:* Tighter checks cut **shrinkage** by a third.

### shoplifting
- **IPA:** /ˈʃɒplɪftɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The crime of stealing goods from a shop.
  - *Example:* Security tags reduced **shoplifting** of the smaller items.

### stocktake
- **IPA:** /ˈstɒkteɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A count of all the goods a shop holds.
  - *Example:* The shop closes early once a year for a full **stocktake**.

### replenishment
- **IPA:** /rɪˈplenɪʃmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of refilling shelves and stock as goods are sold.
  - *Example:* Automatic **replenishment** orders more stock when levels fall.

### merchandiser
- **IPA:** /ˈmɜːtʃəndaɪzə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who decides how products are displayed and priced to boost sales.
  - *Example:* The **merchandiser** rearranged the store for the holiday season.

### floor staff
- **IPA:** /ˈflɔː stɑːf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Employees who work on the shop floor helping customers.
  - *Example:* Extra **floor staff** are added on Saturdays.

### sales associate
- **IPA:** /ˈseɪlz əˌsəʊʃiət/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A shop employee who serves customers and handles sales.
  - *Example:* A **sales associate** helped him choose the right size.

### store manager
- **IPA:** /ˈstɔː ˌmænɪdʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The person in charge of running a single shop.
  - *Example:* The **store manager** handles staffing, stock, and takings.

### opening hours
- **IPA:** /ˈəʊpənɪŋ ˌaʊəz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The times during which a shop or business is open to the public.
  - *Example:* **Opening hours** are extended in December.

### peak hours
- **IPA:** /ˈpiːk ˌaʊəz/

  - *Example:* Deliveries are avoided during **peak hours**.

### queue management
- **IPA:** /ˈkjuː ˌmænɪdʒmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The organising of waiting lines so customers are served quickly and fairly.
  - *Example:* A single-line system improved **queue management** at the tills.

### self-checkout
- **IPA:** /ˌself ˈtʃekaʊt/

### click and collect

### returns desk

### warranty card

  - *Example:* Keep the **warranty card** and the receipt together.

### window display

### mannequin
