# 40 — Travel, Commuting & Getting Around

### commuter
- **IPA:** /kəˈmjuːtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who travels regularly between home and work.
  - *Example:* **Commuters** packed the 8:05 train into the city.

### rush hour
- **IPA:** /ˈrʌʃ aʊə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The time of day when traffic is heaviest, as people travel to or from work.
  - *Example:* Avoid the ring road during **rush hour**.

### peak fare
- **IPA:** /ˈpiːk feə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The higher price charged for travel at the busiest times.
  - *Example:* Travelling ten minutes later avoids the **peak fare**.

### off-peak fare
- **IPA:** /ˌɒf piːk ˈfeə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The lower price charged for travel outside the busiest times.
  - *Example:* The **off-peak fare** is almost half the daytime price.

### travelcard
- **IPA:** /ˈtrævlkɑːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A ticket allowing unlimited travel on public transport within an area for a set period.
  - *Example:* A weekly **travelcard** works out cheaper than daily tickets.

### contactless
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒntæktləs/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Working by holding a card or phone near a reader, without inserting it.
  - *Example:* Just tap your card on the reader; the gates take **contactless**.

### top up
- **IPA:** /ˌtɒp ˈʌp/
- **Meaning 1 (phr v):** To add money or credit to a card or account.
  - *Example:* You can **top up** your travel card at any machine.

### smart card
- **IPA:** /ˈsmɑːt kɑːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A plastic card with a chip, used to store travel credit and pass through gates.
  - *Example:* Load a [season pass](33-arts-culture-and-entertainment.md#season-pass) onto the **smart card** and tap in each day.

### turnstile
- **IPA:** /ˈtɜːnstaɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A barrier with rotating arms that lets one person through at a time.
  - *Example:* The **turnstile** would not open until she tapped her card again.

### platform
- **IPA:** /ˈplætfɔːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The raised area beside a railway track where passengers get on and off.
  - *Example:* The train to the coast leaves from **platform** 4.

### concourse
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒŋkɔːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large open area in a station or airport where people move around and wait.
  - *Example:* Crowds gathered on the **concourse** watching the [departure board](40-travel-commuting-and-getting-around.md#departure-board).

### departure board
- **IPA:** /dɪˈpɑːtʃə bɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A screen showing the times, destinations, and platforms of departing services.
  - *Example:* The **departure board** showed the 9:15 as delayed.

### delay announcement
- **IPA:** /dɪˈleɪ əˌnaʊnsmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A public message telling travellers that a service is running late.
  - *Example:* A **delay announcement** blamed a signal fault further down the line.

### replacement bus
- **IPA:** /rɪˈpleɪsmənt bʌs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A bus used to carry passengers when a train service is not running.
  - *Example:* A **replacement bus** covered the last three stops during the works.

### engineering works
- **IPA:** /ˌendʒɪˈnɪərɪŋ wɜːks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Repair or upgrade work on a railway that closes part of the line.
  - *Example:* **Engineering works** close the line every weekend this month.

### park and ride
- **IPA:** /ˌpɑːk ən ˈraɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A car park on the edge of a town linked to the centre by frequent buses.
  - *Example:* They left the car at the **park and ride** and took the [shuttle](../toeic-600/14-travel-and-transportation.md#13-shuttle) in.

### carpool
- **IPA:** /ˈkɑːpuːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An arrangement where several people share a car for the journey to work.
  - *Example:* A four-person **carpool** cut everyone's fuel costs.

### ride-share
- **IPA:** /ˈraɪd ʃeə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A service, usually booked by app, where a driver takes passengers for a fare.
  - *Example:* She booked a **ride-share** from the airport rather than a taxi.

### hailing app
- **IPA:** /ˈheɪlɪŋ æp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A phone application used to call and pay for a car or taxi.
  - *Example:* The **hailing app** showed a car four minutes away.

### surge pricing
- **IPA:** /ˈsɜːdʒ ˌpraɪsɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Higher fares charged by a ride service when demand is high.
  - *Example:* **Surge pricing** doubled the fare on the night of the concert.

### taxi rank
- **IPA:** /ˈtæksi ræŋk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A place where taxis wait in line for passengers.
  - *Example:* There is a **taxi rank** just outside the main exit.

### meter fare
- **IPA:** /ˈmiːtə feə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A taxi fare calculated by a meter based on distance and time.
  - *Example:* Agree a price first, or insist on the **meter fare**.

### fare evasion
- **IPA:** /ˈfeər ɪˌveɪʒn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Travelling on public transport without paying.
  - *Example:* Barriers were [installed](../toeic-600/13-technology-and-computers.md#3-install) to reduce **fare evasion**.

### penalty fare
- **IPA:** /ˈpenəlti feə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A charge, higher than a normal ticket, for travelling without a valid one.
  - *Example:* He was issued a **penalty fare** for boarding without a ticket.

### ticket inspector
- **IPA:** /ˈtɪkɪt ɪnˌspektə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official who checks that passengers have valid tickets.
  - *Example:* A **ticket inspector** worked through the carriage after the last stop.

### train conductor
- **IPA:** /ˈtreɪn kənˌdʌktə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A member of train staff who checks tickets and helps passengers.
  - *Example:* Ask the **train conductor** which side the platform will be on.

### guard
- **IPA:** /ɡɑːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A railway employee responsible for the safety of a train and its passengers.
  - *Example:* The **guard** gave the signal and the doors closed.

### timetable
- **IPA:** /ˈtaɪmteɪbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A list of the times when buses, trains, or classes run.
  - *Example:* Check the weekend **timetable**, which has fewer services.

### connection
- **IPA:** /kəˈnekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A train, bus, or flight timed to let passengers continue their journey.
  - *Example:* A ten-minute delay made him miss his **connection**.

### interchange
- **IPA:** /ˈɪntətʃeɪndʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A station or point where passengers can change between lines or services.
  - *Example:* Change at the next **interchange** for the airport line.

### standby
- **IPA:** /ˈstændbaɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The state of waiting for a seat that becomes free on a full flight or train.
  - *Example:* They flew **standby** and got the last two seats.

### gate change
- **IPA:** /ˈɡeɪt tʃeɪndʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An announcement that a flight will now leave from a different gate.
  - *Example:* A late **gate change** sent everyone rushing across the [terminal](../toeic-600/14-travel-and-transportation.md#11-terminal).

### boarding group
- **IPA:** /ˈbɔːdɪŋ ɡruːp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** One of the sets in which passengers are called to get on a plane.
  - *Example:* Passengers in **boarding group** 3 were called last.

### overhead locker
- **IPA:** /ˌəʊvəhed ˈlɒkə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A closed compartment above the seats on a plane for hand luggage.
  - *Example:* The **overhead lockers** were full by the time he boarded.

### aisle seat
- **IPA:** /ˈaɪl siːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A seat next to the passage on a plane, train, or coach.
  - *Example:* She always books an **aisle seat** so she can stretch her legs.

### window seat
- **IPA:** /ˈwɪndəʊ siːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A seat next to the window on a plane, train, or coach.
  - *Example:* He took the **window seat** to watch the take-off.

### legroom
- **IPA:** /ˈleɡruːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of space for a passenger's legs.
  - *Example:* The exit row has extra **legroom**.

### jet lag
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒet læɡ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Tiredness and confusion after a long flight across time zones.
  - *Example:* **Jet lag** kept him awake at 3 a.m. for the first few nights.

### red-eye
- **IPA:** /ˈred aɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An overnight flight that arrives early in the morning.
  - *Example:* She took the **red-eye** to be in the office by nine.

### transit visa
- **IPA:** /ˈtrænzɪt ˌviːzə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Permission to pass through a country on the way to another.
  - *Example:* A long [stopover](../toeic-600/14-travel-and-transportation.md#24-stopover) meant they needed a **transit visa**.

### passport control
- **IPA:** /ˈpɑːspɔːt kənˌtrəʊl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The place at a border where officials check travellers' passports.
  - *Example:* The queue at **passport control** took over an hour.

### biometric
- **IPA:** /ˌbaɪəˈmetrɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Using physical features such as fingerprints or face scans to check identity.
  - *Example:* The **biometric** gates read your [passport](../toeic-600/14-travel-and-transportation.md#8-passport) photo and let you through.

### baggage carousel
- **IPA:** /ˈbæɡɪdʒ ˌkærəsel/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The moving belt at an airport where checked luggage is collected.
  - *Example:* Bags from the flight came out on **baggage carousel** 5.

### excess baggage
- **IPA:** /ˌekses ˈbæɡɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Luggage that weighs more than the allowance, for which a fee is charged.
  - *Example:* The extra suitcase cost £60 in **excess baggage**.

### hand luggage
- **IPA:** /ˈhænd ˌlʌɡɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Small bags a passenger keeps with them on a plane.
  - *Example:* Liquids in **hand luggage** must be under 100 ml.

### hold luggage
- **IPA:** /ˈhəʊld ˌlʌɡɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Luggage checked in and carried in the aircraft's hold.
  - *Example:* Put anything sharp in your **hold luggage**.

### customs channel
- **IPA:** /ˈkʌstəmz ˌtʃænl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** One of the routes travellers choose at customs depending on what they are carrying.
  - *Example:* With nothing to [declare](40-travel-commuting-and-getting-around.md#declare), they took the correct **customs channel**.

### green channel
- **IPA:** /ˌɡriːn ˈtʃænl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The customs route for travellers who have nothing to declare.
  - *Example:* He was stopped for a check even in the **green channel**.

### declare
- **IPA:** /dɪˈkleə/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To tell customs officials about goods you are bringing into a country.
  - *Example:* You must **declare** goods worth more than the [allowance](../toeic-600/18-employee-benefits-and-insurance.md#19-allowance).

### duty-free
- **IPA:** /ˌdjuːti ˈfriː/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Sold without the usual taxes, as at an airport for international travellers.
  - *Example:* She bought perfume in the **duty-free** shop before the flight.

### car hire
- **IPA:** /ˈkɑː haɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The renting of a car for a short period.
  - *Example:* **Car hire** at the airport was cheaper booked in advance.

### excess waiver
- **IPA:** /ˈekses ˌweɪvə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Extra insurance on a hire car that removes the amount you would otherwise pay toward damage.
  - *Example:* The **excess waiver** added £12 a day but gave peace of mind.

### satnav
- **IPA:** /ˈsætnæv/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that gives spoken driving directions using satellite positioning.
  - *Example:* The **satnav** sent them down a road that was closed.

### toll road
- **IPA:** /ˈtəʊl rəʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A road that drivers must pay a charge to use.
  - *Example:* The **toll road** is faster but costs €8.

### congestion charge
- **IPA:** /kənˈdʒestʃən tʃɑːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fee for driving into a busy city area, meant to reduce traffic.
  - *Example:* The daily **congestion charge** made him switch to the train.

### low-emission zone
- **IPA:** /ˌləʊ ɪˈmɪʃn zəʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An area where older, more polluting vehicles are charged or banned.
  - *Example:* Their old van cannot enter the **low-emission zone** without a fee.

### hard shoulder
- **IPA:** /ˌhɑːd ˈʃəʊldə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The strip at the edge of a motorway where vehicles can stop in an emergency.
  - *Example:* They pulled onto the **hard shoulder** with a flat tyre.

### tailback
- **IPA:** /ˈteɪlbæk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A long line of slow or stationary traffic behind a hold-up.
  - *Example:* An accident caused a five-mile **tailback**.

### diversion
- **IPA:** /daɪˈvɜːʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A route used when the usual road is closed.
  - *Example:* A **diversion** took traffic through three villages.

### roadworks
- **IPA:** /ˈrəʊdwɜːks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Repairs or building work on a road that slows or blocks traffic.
  - *Example:* **Roadworks** on the bridge cut it to one lane.

### service station
- **IPA:** /ˈsɜːvɪs ˌsteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A place beside a major road with fuel, food, and toilets.
  - *Example:* They stopped at the next **service station** for coffee.

### layby
- **IPA:** /ˈleɪbaɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short strip beside a road where vehicles can pull in and stop.
  - *Example:* He pulled into a **layby** to check the map.

### cycle lane
- **IPA:** /ˈsaɪkl leɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A part of the road marked off for bicycles.
  - *Example:* A protected **cycle lane** runs the length of the main road.

### pedestrian crossing
- **IPA:** /pəˌdestriən ˈkrɒsɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A marked place where people on foot can cross the road.
  - *Example:* Cars must stop at the **pedestrian crossing** when someone is waiting.

### level crossing
- **IPA:** /ˌlevl ˈkrɒsɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A place where a road crosses a railway line on the same level, with barriers.
  - *Example:* The **level crossing** barriers were down for a [freight train](21-transportation-and-shipping.md#freight-train).

### one-way system
- **IPA:** /ˌwʌn weɪ ˈsɪstəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A set of streets where traffic may travel in one direction only.
  - *Example:* The town's **one-way system** confuses first-time visitors.

### multi-storey car park
- **IPA:** /ˌmʌlti stɔːri ˈkɑː pɑːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A building with several floors for parking cars.
  - *Example:* The **multi-storey car park** was full by nine on Saturday.

### park-and-stride
- **IPA:** /ˌpɑːk ən ˈstraɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Parking a short distance from a destination and walking the rest of the way.
  - *Example:* The school [promotes](../toeic-600/08-marketing-and-advertising.md#7-promote) **park-and-stride** to cut traffic at the gates.

### commute
- **IPA:** /kəˈmjuːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The regular journey between home and work.
  - *Example:* Her **commute** is 40 minutes each way by train.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To travel regularly between home and work.
  - *Example:* He **commutes** into the city four days a week.

### ticket machine
- **IPA:** /ˈtɪkɪt məˌʃiːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A machine that sells travel tickets, usually taking cards or cash.
  - *Example:* Only one **ticket machine** was working, so the queue was long.

### bus route
- **IPA:** /ˈbʌs ruːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The fixed path a bus service follows, with set stops.
  - *Example:* A new **bus route** links the estate to the [hospital](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#hospital).

### train station
- **IPA:** /ˈtreɪn ˌsteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A place where trains stop for passengers to get on and off.
  - *Example:* The flat is a five-minute walk from the **train station**.

### road trip
- **IPA:** /ˈrəʊd trɪp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A long journey made by car for pleasure.
  - *Example:* They took a two-week **road trip** along the coast.

### fare zone
- **IPA:** /ˈfeə zəʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** One of the areas a transport network is divided into for pricing.
  - *Example:* Travelling across three **fare zones** costs more than staying in one.

### traffic jam
- **IPA:** /ˈtræfɪk dʒæm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A line of vehicles that can move only slowly or not at all.
  - *Example:* A **traffic jam** on the motorway made him an hour late.
