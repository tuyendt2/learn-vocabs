# 14 — Law & Compliance

### statute
- **IPA:** /ˈstætʃuːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A written law passed by a parliament or other law-making body.
  - *Example:* The right to paid leave is set out in **statute**.

### regulation
- **IPA:** /ˌreɡjuˈleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official rule made by a government or authority to control an activity.
  - *Example:* New safety **regulations** apply to every workplace from January.

### ordinance
- **IPA:** /ˈɔːdɪnəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A rule or law made by a city or local authority.
  - *Example:* A local **ordinance** limits delivery hours in the town centre.

### legislation
- **IPA:** /ˌledʒɪsˈleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Laws considered as a group; the act of making laws.
  - *Example:* **Legislation** on data privacy was tightened last year.

### sub-clause
- **IPA:** /ˈsʌb klɔːz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A smaller division within a clause of a legal document.
  - *Example:* The exception is buried in **sub-clause** 4(b).

### schedule
- **IPA:** /ˈʃedjuːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A separate section at the end of a contract or law giving detailed lists or terms.
  - *Example:* The prices are set out in **Schedule** 2.

### jurisdiction
- **IPA:** /ˌdʒʊərɪsˈdɪkʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The official power to make legal decisions, or the area in which that power applies.
  - *Example:* The dispute falls under the **jurisdiction** of the English courts.

### venue
- **IPA:** /ˈvenjuː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The place where a legal case is heard.
  - *Example:* The contract names London as the **venue** for any legal action.

### plaintiff
- **IPA:** /ˈpleɪntɪf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The person who brings a case against another in a civil court.
  - *Example:* The **plaintiff** claimed damages for lost earnings.

### defendant
- **IPA:** /dɪˈfendənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The person or organisation being sued or accused in court.
  - *Example:* The **defendant** denied breaking the agreement.

### litigant
- **IPA:** /ˈlɪtɪɡənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person involved in a lawsuit, either bringing or defending it.
  - *Example:* Both **litigants** were ordered to try mediation first.

### counsel
- **IPA:** /ˈkaʊnsl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A lawyer or group of lawyers giving legal advice and representing a party in court.
  - *Example:* The company's **counsel** advised settling out of court.

### attorney
- **IPA:** /əˈtɜːni/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A lawyer, especially one qualified to act for clients in court (mainly US).
  - *Example:* Her **attorney** filed the claim on Monday.

### solicitor
- **IPA:** /səˈlɪsɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A lawyer who advises clients and prepares legal documents (mainly UK).
  - *Example:* A **solicitor** drew up the lease and the sale contract.

### barrister
- **IPA:** /ˈbærɪstə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A lawyer who represents clients in the higher courts (mainly UK).
  - *Example:* The firm hired a **barrister** to argue the appeal.

### paralegal
- **IPA:** /ˌpærəˈliːɡl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A trained assistant who does legal work but is not a qualified lawyer.
  - *Example:* A **paralegal** gathered the documents for disclosure.

### affidavit
- **IPA:** /ˌæfɪˈdeɪvɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A written statement of facts sworn to be true, used as evidence in court.
  - *Example:* The manager signed an **affidavit** describing the meeting.

### deposition
- **IPA:** /ˌdepəˈzɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Sworn evidence given out of court and written down for later use.
  - *Example:* The witness gave a three-hour **deposition**.

### testimony
- **IPA:** /ˈtestɪməni/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal statement of what someone knows, given in court.
  - *Example:* Her **testimony** contradicted the earlier account.

### subpoena
- **IPA:** /səˈpiːnə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official order to attend court or produce documents.
  - *Example:* The firm received a **subpoena** for its email records.

### summons
- **IPA:** /ˈsʌmənz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official document ordering someone to appear in court.
  - *Example:* A **summons** was served on the company director.

### injunction
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈdʒʌŋkʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A court order requiring someone to do or stop doing something.
  - *Example:* The court granted an **injunction** stopping the sale.

### damages
- **IPA:** /ˈdæmɪdʒɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money claimed or awarded as compensation for loss or harm.
  - *Example:* The court awarded **damages** of £120,000.

### negligence
- **IPA:** /ˈneɡlɪdʒəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Failure to take reasonable care, causing loss or injury to another.
  - *Example:* The claim was based on **negligence** in maintaining the machine.

### covenant
- **IPA:** /ˈkʌvənənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal promise in a contract to do or not do something.
  - *Example:* A **covenant** in the lease bans any building on the land.

### precedent
- **IPA:** /ˈpresɪdənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An earlier legal decision that guides how similar cases are decided.
  - *Example:* The ruling set a **precedent** for gig-economy contracts.

### ruling
- **IPA:** /ˈruːlɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official decision made by a court or authority.
  - *Example:* The **ruling** went in the supplier's favour.

### verdict
- **IPA:** /ˈvɜːdɪkt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The formal decision reached at the end of a trial.
  - *Example:* The jury returned a **verdict** after two days.

### appeal
- **IPA:** /əˈpiːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal request to a higher court to change a decision.
  - *Example:* The company lodged an **appeal** against the fine.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To ask a higher court to review a decision.
  - *Example:* They plan to **appeal** the ruling.

### mediation
- **IPA:** /ˌmiːdiˈeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A process in which a neutral person helps two sides reach agreement without a trial.
  - *Example:* **Mediation** settled the dispute in a single day.

### tribunal
- **IPA:** /traɪˈbjuːnl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A body with authority to judge particular types of dispute, such as employment cases.
  - *Example:* The worker took the claim to an employment **tribunal**.

### litigation
- **IPA:** /ˌlɪtɪˈɡeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of taking legal action through the courts.
  - *Example:* **Litigation** would cost more than the amount in dispute.

### class action
- **IPA:** /ˌklɑːs ˈækʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A lawsuit brought by one person on behalf of a large group with the same claim.
  - *Example:* Customers filed a **class action** over the hidden fees.

### intellectual property
- **IPA:** /ˌɪntəlektʃuəl ˈprɒpəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Creations of the mind, such as inventions and designs, protected by law.
  - *Example:* The contract makes clear who owns the **intellectual property**.

### patent
- **IPA:** /ˈpeɪtnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A legal right giving an inventor sole use of an invention for a set period.
  - *Example:* The firm holds a **patent** on the locking mechanism.

### trademark
- **IPA:** /ˈtreɪdmɑːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A name, symbol, or design legally registered to identify a company's products.
  - *Example:* The logo is a registered **trademark**.

### copyright
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒpiraɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The legal right to control the copying and use of an original work.
  - *Example:* Using the photo without permission breached **copyright**.

### licensing
- **IPA:** /ˈlaɪsnsɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The granting of official permission to use property, a name, or a right, usually for a fee.
  - *Example:* **Licensing** the brand to overseas partners brought in steady income.

### infringement
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈfrɪndʒmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of breaking a law or another person's legal right.
  - *Example:* The lawsuit alleged patent **infringement**.

### non-disclosure
- **IPA:** /ˌnɒn dɪsˈkləʊʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An agreement or duty not to reveal certain information.
  - *Example:* Contractors sign a **non-disclosure** agreement before the briefing.

### confidentiality clause
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒnfɪdenʃiˈæləti klɔːz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A part of a contract requiring the parties to keep certain information secret.
  - *Example:* A **confidentiality clause** covers the settlement amount.

### non-compete
- **IPA:** /ˌnɒn kəmˈpiːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A contract term stopping someone from working for a rival for a period after leaving.
  - *Example:* Her **non-compete** runs for twelve months.

### governing law
- **IPA:** /ˌɡʌvənɪŋ ˈlɔː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The country's or state's law chosen to apply to a contract.
  - *Example:* The **governing law** of the agreement is that of Singapore.

### force majeure
- **IPA:** /ˌfɔːs mæˈʒɜː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A contract clause freeing parties from liability when an extraordinary event prevents performance.
  - *Example:* The supplier claimed **force majeure** after the port was closed by a storm.

### compliance officer
- **IPA:** /kəmˈplaɪəns ˌɒfɪsə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An employee responsible for making sure a company follows laws and rules.
  - *Example:* The **compliance officer** reviews every new marketing claim.

### regulatory body
- **IPA:** /ˈreɡjələtri ˌbɒdi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official organisation that supervises and enforces rules in an industry.
  - *Example:* The **regulatory body** can suspend a licence for serious breaches.

### sanction
- **IPA:** /ˈsæŋkʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A penalty imposed for breaking a rule or law.
  - *Example:* The firm faced **sanctions** for late reporting.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To give official permission or approval for something.
  - *Example:* The board **sanctioned** the extra spending.

### penalty
- **IPA:** /ˈpenəlti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A punishment, often a payment, for breaking a rule or contract.
  - *Example:* There is a **penalty** for cancelling the order after dispatch.

### fine
- **IPA:** /faɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sum of money that must be paid as punishment.
  - *Example:* The company was given a large **fine** for the data breach.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To make someone pay money as a punishment.
  - *Example:* The regulator **fined** the bank $50 million.

### whistleblower
- **IPA:** /ˈwɪslbləʊə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who reports illegal or unethical activity within an organisation.
  - *Example:* A **whistleblower** alerted the regulator to the false accounts.

### anti-bribery
- **IPA:** /ˌænti ˈbraɪbəri/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to laws and rules that forbid offering or taking bribes.
  - *Example:* All staff complete **anti-bribery** training each year.

### money laundering
- **IPA:** /ˈmʌni ˌlɔːndərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of hiding the illegal origin of money by passing it through legitimate businesses.
  - *Example:* Banks must report transactions that look like **money laundering**.

### data protection
- **IPA:** /ˈdeɪtə prəˌtekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Laws and practices that control how personal information is collected and used.
  - *Example:* **Data protection** rules limit how long customer records may be kept.

### consent
- **IPA:** /kənˈsent/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Permission for something to happen or agreement to do something.
  - *Example:* The company needs written **consent** before sharing your details.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To give permission or agree to something.
  - *Example:* She **consented** to the recording of the call.

### disclosure obligation
- **IPA:** /dɪsˈkləʊʒə ˌɒblɪˈɡeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A legal duty to reveal certain information to a court, regulator, or other party.
  - *Example:* The firm met its **disclosure obligation** by handing over all relevant emails.

### statute of limitations
- **IPA:** /ˌstætʃuːt əv ˌlɪmɪˈteɪʃnz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A law setting the maximum time after an event within which legal action can be started.
  - *Example:* The claim was too old, barred by the **statute of limitations**.

### binding precedent
- **IPA:** /ˌbaɪndɪŋ ˈpresɪdənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A past decision that a lower court must follow in similar cases.
  - *Example:* The higher court's ruling is a **binding precedent** for all the regional courts.

### cease and desist
- **IPA:** /ˌsiːs ən dɪˈzɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal demand that someone stop an activity said to be unlawful.
  - *Example:* The brand sent a **cease and desist** letter over the copied packaging.

### power of attorney
- **IPA:** /ˌpaʊər əv əˈtɜːni/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A legal document giving one person authority to act for another.
  - *Example:* She holds **power of attorney** for her elderly father's finances.

### by-law
- **IPA:** /ˈbaɪ lɔː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A rule made by a local authority or organisation for its own area or members.
  - *Example:* A parking **by-law** bans overnight lorries on residential streets.

### enactment
- **IPA:** /ɪˈnæktmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of making a bill into law, or the law that results.
  - *Example:* Since its **enactment**, the rule has cut workplace injuries.

### hearing
- **IPA:** /ˈhɪərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A meeting at which a court or official body listens to evidence and arguments.
  - *Example:* The **hearing** was adjourned until the following month.

### court trial
- **IPA:** /ˈkɔːt ˌtraɪəl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal examination of evidence in court to decide a legal case.
  - *Example:* The **court trial** is expected to last three weeks.

### judgment
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒʌdʒmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The official decision of a court in a legal case.
  - *Example:* The **judgment** ordered the firm to pay costs.

### plea
- **IPA:** /pliː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A defendant's formal answer of guilty or not guilty to a charge.
  - *Example:* The company entered a **plea** of not guilty.

### acquittal
- **IPA:** /əˈkwɪtl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A court decision that a person is not guilty.
  - *Example:* The **acquittal** ended two years of legal pressure on the director.

### conviction
- **IPA:** /kənˈvɪkʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal court decision that a person is guilty of a crime.
  - *Example:* A **conviction** for fraud would bar him from being a director.

### recourse
- **IPA:** /rɪˈkɔːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A source of help or a legal right to obtain a remedy.
  - *Example:* Customers have **recourse** to the ombudsman if the complaint is not settled.

### redress
- **IPA:** /rɪˈdres/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Compensation or correction for a wrong that has been done.
  - *Example:* The scheme offers **redress** to customers who were mis-sold the policy.

### non-compliance
- **IPA:** /ˌnɒn kəmˈplaɪəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Failure to obey a rule, law, or standard.
  - *Example:* **Non-compliance** with the safety code can shut a site down.

### regulatory framework
- **IPA:** /ˈreɡjələtri ˌfreɪmwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The complete set of laws and rules governing an industry.
  - *Example:* A new **regulatory framework** covers online lenders for the first time.

### statutory duty
- **IPA:** /ˌstætʃətri ˈdjuːti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An obligation placed on a person or body by law.
  - *Example:* Employers have a **statutory duty** to provide a safe workplace.

### tort
- **IPA:** /tɔːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A wrongful act, other than breach of contract, that causes harm and can be sued over.
  - *Example:* The claim was brought in **tort** for damage caused by the leak.

### legal proceedings
- **IPA:** /ˌliːɡl prəˈsiːdɪŋz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The formal steps taken to settle a dispute or enforce a right through a court.
  - *Example:* The bank started **legal proceedings** to recover the debt.

### out-of-court settlement
- **IPA:** /ˌaʊt əv kɔːt ˈsetlmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An agreement to resolve a dispute before it reaches a full trial.
  - *Example:* Both sides preferred an **out-of-court settlement** to a public trial.
