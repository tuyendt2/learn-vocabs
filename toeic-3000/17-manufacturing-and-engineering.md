### fabrication
- **IPA:** /ˌfæbrɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The making or building of a product, especially from prepared metal parts.
  - *Example:* Steel **fabrication** for the frame is done on site.

### machining
- **IPA:** /məˈʃiːnɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The shaping of metal or other material by cutting it with machine tools.
  - *Example:* Precise **machining** keeps every part within a fraction of a millimetre.

### casting
- **IPA:** /ˈkɑːstɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of shaping metal or plastic by pouring it into a mould.
  - *Example:* The engine block is made by **casting** molten aluminium.

### moulding
- **IPA:** /ˈməʊldɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The shaping of a soft or liquid material in a hollow form.
  - *Example:* Injection **moulding** produces thousands of identical covers an hour.

### forging
- **IPA:** /ˈfɔːdʒɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The shaping of hot metal by hammering or pressing.
  - *Example:* **Forging** makes the tool stronger than casting would.

### welding
- **IPA:** /ˈweldɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The joining of metal parts by heating them until they melt together.
  - *Example:* A robot handles the **welding** along the main seam.

### subassembly
- **IPA:** /ˈsʌbəˌsembli/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A unit built from several parts that is later fitted into a larger product.
  - *Example:* The pump is delivered as a finished **subassembly**.

### jig
- **IPA:** /dʒɪɡ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that holds a workpiece and guides the tools working on it.
  - *Example:* A **jig** keeps every hole in exactly the same place.

### tolerance
- **IPA:** /ˈtɒlərəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The allowed amount of variation in a measurement.
  - *Example:* The shaft must be made to a **tolerance** of 0.02 mm.

### calibration
- **IPA:** /ˌkælɪˈbreɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The setting or checking of an instrument so that its readings are accurate.
  - *Example:* Each gauge has a **calibration** sticker showing the last check.

### schematic
- **IPA:** /skɪˈmætɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A simplified drawing showing how the parts of a system connect.
  - *Example:* The wiring **schematic** helped the technician trace the fault.

### drafting
- **IPA:** /ˈdrɑːftɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The making of detailed technical drawings for a design.
  - *Example:* **Drafting** the new bracket took two days.

### CAD
- **IPA:** /kæd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Computer-aided design: software used to create technical drawings and models.
  - *Example:* The part was designed in **CAD** and sent straight to the machine.

### prototyping
- **IPA:** /ˈprəʊtətaɪpɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The building of early models to test and improve a design.
  - *Example:* 3D printing made **prototyping** far cheaper and faster.

### die
- **IPA:** /daɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A shaped tool used to cut or form metal, or to stamp a pattern.
  - *Example:* A worn **die** was leaving marks on every panel.

### mould
- **IPA:** /məʊld/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A hollow container into which material is poured to set into a shape.
  - *Example:* The **mould** cost more than the first year's production.

### lathe
- **IPA:** /leɪð/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A machine that spins a workpiece so tools can shape it evenly.
  - *Example:* He turned the metal rod on the **lathe** to the right diameter.

### milling machine
- **IPA:** /ˈmɪlɪŋ məˌʃiːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A machine that removes material with a rotating cutter to shape a workpiece.
  - *Example:* The slot was cut on a **milling machine**.

### press
- **IPA:** /pres/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A machine that shapes or cuts material using great force.
  - *Example:* The metal sheet is formed in a single stroke of the **press**.

### robotics
- **IPA:** /rəʊˈbɒtɪks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The design and use of robots, especially in manufacturing.
  - *Example:* Investment in **robotics** cut the assembly time in half.

### automation
- **IPA:** /ˌɔːtəˈmeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The use of machines and computers to do work previously done by people.
  - *Example:* **Automation** of the packing line removed three manual jobs.

### actuator
- **IPA:** /ˈæktʃueɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that produces movement in a machine in response to a control signal.
  - *Example:* A faulty **actuator** stopped the gate from closing.

### sensor
- **IPA:** /ˈsensə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that detects a physical condition, such as heat or movement, and reports it.
  - *Example:* A **sensor** halts the line if a guard is opened.

### servo
- **IPA:** /ˈsɜːvəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A motor system that controls position or speed very precisely.
  - *Example:* The **servo** holds the arm steady to within a degree.

### hydraulics
- **IPA:** /haɪˈdrɔːlɪks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The use of pressurised liquid to power or move machinery.
  - *Example:* The lift is raised by **hydraulics**.

### pneumatics
- **IPA:** /njuːˈmætɪks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The use of compressed air to power tools and machinery.
  - *Example:* The clamps are driven by **pneumatics**.

### conveyor
- **IPA:** /kənˈveɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A continuous moving system that carries items along a production line.
  - *Example:* Parts travel down the **conveyor** to the paint booth.

### cycle time
- **IPA:** /ˈsaɪkl taɪm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The time taken to complete one full operation or produce one unit.
  - *Example:* Reducing **cycle time** by ten seconds added 400 units a day.

### takt time
- **IPA:** /ˈtækt taɪm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The rate at which a product must be finished to meet customer demand.
  - *Example:* Demand rose, so the line's **takt time** was shortened.

### work-in-progress
- **IPA:** /ˌwɜːk ɪn ˈprəʊɡres/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Goods that have been started but are not yet finished.
  - *Example:* Too much **work-in-progress** clogged the floor between stations.

### finished goods
- **IPA:** /ˌfɪnɪʃt ˈɡʊdz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Products that are complete and ready to be sold or shipped.
  - *Example:* **Finished goods** are moved straight to the dispatch bay.

### bill of materials
- **IPA:** /ˌbɪl əv məˈtɪəriəlz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A complete list of the parts and quantities needed to make a product.
  - *Example:* A missing item on the **bill of materials** stopped the build.

### routing
- **IPA:** /ˈruːtɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The set sequence of operations a part goes through during manufacture.
  - *Example:* The **routing** sends the casting to machining, then to inspection.

### changeover
- **IPA:** /ˈtʃeɪndʒəʊvə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The switching of a machine or line from making one product to another.
  - *Example:* A faster **changeover** let them run smaller batches.

### retooling
- **IPA:** /ˌriːˈtuːlɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The fitting of new tools or equipment to make a different or improved product.
  - *Example:* **Retooling** the plant for the new model took the summer shutdown.

### preventive maintenance
- **IPA:** /prɪˌventɪv ˈmeɪntənəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Regular servicing done to stop equipment breaking down.
  - *Example:* **Preventive maintenance** every Friday cut unplanned stoppages.

### uptime
- **IPA:** /ˈʌptaɪm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The time during which a machine or system is working and available.
  - *Example:* The line now runs at 96% **uptime**.

### overall equipment effectiveness
- **IPA:** /ˌəʊvərɔːl ɪˈkwɪpmənt ɪˌfektɪvnəs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A combined measure of a machine's availability, speed, and quality output.
  - *Example:* **Overall equipment effectiveness** rose from 65% to 78%.

### scrap rate
- **IPA:** /ˈskræp reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The proportion of material or product that is thrown away as unusable.
  - *Example:* A better cutting pattern lowered the **scrap rate**.

### in-process inspection
- **IPA:** /ˌɪn prəʊses ɪnˈspekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Checking of quality while a product is still being made, not only at the end.
  - *Example:* **In-process inspection** caught the error before 200 more were made.

### nonconformity
- **IPA:** /ˌnɒnkənˈfɔːməti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A failure to meet a specified requirement or standard.
  - *Example:* Each **nonconformity** is logged with its cause and fix.

### root-cause analysis
- **IPA:** /ˌruːt kɔːz əˈnæləsɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A structured method for finding the underlying reason a problem occurred.
  - *Example:* **Root-cause analysis** traced the leaks to one supplier's seals.

### statistical process control
- **IPA:** /stəˌtɪstɪkl ˈprəʊses kənˌtrəʊl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The use of charts and data to monitor a process and catch problems early.
  - *Example:* **Statistical process control** showed the machine drifting out of tolerance.

### Six Sigma
- **IPA:** /ˌsɪks ˈsɪɡmə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A method for reducing defects and variation in a process to a very low level.
  - *Example:* A **Six Sigma** project cut billing errors by 90%.

### lean manufacturing
- **IPA:** /ˌliːn ˌmænjuˈfæktʃərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A system of production that aims to remove every kind of waste.
  - *Example:* **Lean manufacturing** cut the floor space needed by a third.

### kaizen
- **IPA:** /ˈkaɪzen/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A practice of continuous small improvements made by everyone in a workplace.
  - *Example:* Weekly **kaizen** meetings produced dozens of minor time savings.

### just-in-time
- **IPA:** /ˌdʒʌst ɪn ˈtaɪm/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Describing a system where materials arrive exactly when needed, keeping stock low.
  - *Example:* A **just-in-time** system leaves no room for late deliveries.

### kanban
- **IPA:** /ˈkænbæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A card or signal system that triggers the supply of parts only when they are used.
  - *Example:* An empty **kanban** bin tells the store to send more bolts.

### batch production
- **IPA:** /ˈbætʃ prəˌdʌkʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Making goods in groups of a set size rather than one at a time or continuously.
  - *Example:* **Batch production** suits products with frequent design changes.

### mass production
- **IPA:** /ˌmæs prəˈdʌkʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The making of large quantities of a standard product, usually on an assembly line.
  - *Example:* **Mass production** brought the unit cost down sharply.

### continuous flow
- **IPA:** /kənˌtɪnjuəs ˈfləʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Production in which items move steadily through each step without stopping in batches.
  - *Example:* Switching to **continuous flow** cut work-in-progress almost to zero.

### plant layout
- **IPA:** /ˈplɑːnt ˌleɪaʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The arrangement of machines, storage, and walkways within a factory.
  - *Example:* A new **plant layout** shortened the distance parts travel.

### shop floor
- **IPA:** /ˈʃɒp flɔː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The area of a factory where production work is done, and the workers there.
  - *Example:* The idea came from the **shop floor**, not from management.

### foreman
- **IPA:** /ˈfɔːmən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A worker who supervises and directs a team on the shop floor.
  - *Example:* The **foreman** reassigned two people to clear the backlog.

### machinist
- **IPA:** /məˈʃiːnɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A skilled worker who operates machine tools.
  - *Example:* An experienced **machinist** can hold a tighter tolerance.

### technician
- **IPA:** /tekˈnɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person skilled in the practical use and repair of equipment.
  - *Example:* A **technician** recalibrated the sensors after the fault.

### tolerancing
- **IPA:** /ˈtɒlərənsɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of deciding and specifying the allowable variation for each dimension.
  - *Example:* Careful **tolerancing** kept the parts interchangeable between plants.

### load-bearing
- **IPA:** /ˈləʊd ˌbeərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Designed to carry weight or force.
  - *Example:* The bracket is **load-bearing**, so it is made of forged steel.

### stress test
- **IPA:** /ˈstres test/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A test that applies extreme conditions to see when or how something fails.
  - *Example:* The frame passed a **stress test** at twice the rated load.

### fatigue
- **IPA:** /fəˈtiːɡ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Weakening of a material caused by repeated stress over time.
  - *Example:* Metal **fatigue** cracked the bracket after two years of vibration.

### durability
- **IPA:** /ˌdjʊərəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ability to withstand wear and last a long time.
  - *Example:* The casing was tested for **durability** against knocks and dust.

### specification sheet
- **IPA:** /ˌspesɪfɪˈkeɪʃn ʃiːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document listing the exact measurements, materials, and standards for a product.
  - *Example:* The **specification sheet** calls for stainless steel, not mild steel.

### quality gate
- **IPA:** /ˈkwɒləti ɡeɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A checkpoint that a product or project must pass before moving to the next stage.
  - *Example:* No batch leaves the line without passing the final **quality gate**.

### machine shop
- **IPA:** /məˈʃiːn ʃɒp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A workshop where parts are made and shaped with machine tools.
  - *Example:* The prototype was cut in the company's own **machine shop**.

### fitter
- **IPA:** /ˈfɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A skilled worker who assembles or installs machinery and parts.
  - *Example:* A **fitter** aligned the new motor with the gearbox.

### toolmaker
- **IPA:** /ˈtuːlmeɪkə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A highly skilled worker who makes the dies, moulds, and jigs used in production.
  - *Example:* The **toolmaker** spent a week building the new pressing die.

### production line
- **IPA:** /prəˈdʌkʃn laɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A line of workers and machines along which a product moves as it is built.
  - *Example:* A jam on the **production line** stopped output for an hour.

### production run
- **IPA:** /prəˈdʌkʃn rʌn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A single period of making a set quantity of one product.
  - *Example:* The first **production run** was limited to 500 units.

### precision engineering
- **IPA:** /prɪˌsɪʒn ˌendʒɪˈnɪərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The making of components to very exact measurements.
  - *Example:* The firm specialises in **precision engineering** for medical devices.

### numerical control
- **IPA:** /njuːˌmerɪkl kənˈtrəʊl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The operation of machine tools by coded instructions from a computer.
  - *Example:* **Numerical control** lets one program run the same job on any machine.

### equipment breakdown
- **IPA:** /ɪˈkwɪpmənt ˌbreɪkdaʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A failure that stops a machine from working.
  - *Example:* An **equipment breakdown** on the packer idled the whole line.

### heat treatment
- **IPA:** /ˈhiːt ˌtriːtmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Heating and cooling metal in a controlled way to change its hardness or strength.
  - *Example:* **Heat treatment** made the gear teeth far more wear-resistant.

### surface finish
- **IPA:** /ˈsɜːfɪs ˌfɪnɪʃ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The smoothness or texture of a machined or coated surface.
  - *Example:* The seal needs a fine **surface finish** to prevent leaks.

### line balancing
- **IPA:** /ˈlaɪn ˌbælənsɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Adjusting the work at each station so the line flows evenly with no idle time.
  - *Example:* **Line balancing** removed the pile-up at the third station.

### machine operator
- **IPA:** /məˈʃiːn ˌɒpəreɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A worker whose job is to run and monitor a particular machine.
  - *Example:* Each **machine operator** logs output and any stoppages per shift.
