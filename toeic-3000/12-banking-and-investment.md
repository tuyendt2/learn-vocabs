# 12 — Banking & Investment

### asset
- **IPA:** /ˈæset/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Something owned that has financial value, such as property or investments.
  - *Example:* The fund holds a wide range of **assets**, from shares to bonds.

### equity
- **IPA:** /ˈekwəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Ownership in a company, represented by its shares.
  - *Example:* The [founders](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#founder) kept 55% of the **equity** after the funding round.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The value of a property after any debt on it is subtracted.
  - *Example:* Rising prices increased the **equity** in their home.

### bond
- **IPA:** /bɒnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A loan to a government or company that pays fixed interest over a set period.
  - *Example:* The city issued **bonds** to pay for the new bridge.

### security
- **IPA:** /sɪˈkjʊərəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A financial asset that can be traded, such as a share or bond.
  - *Example:* The bank cannot hold that type of **security** for retail clients.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** Property promised to a lender in case a loan is not repaid.
  - *Example:* They offered the [warehouse](../toeic-600/11-logistics-shipping-and-warehousing.md#15-warehouse) as **security** for the loan.

### share
- **IPA:** /ʃeə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** One of the equal parts into which a company's capital is divided.
  - *Example:* She owns 2,000 **shares** in the company.

### stock
- **IPA:** /stɒk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The shares of a company, or shares in general.
  - *Example:* Tech **stocks** fell sharply on the news.

### share price
- **IPA:** /ˈʃeə praɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The current market value of one share in a company.
  - *Example:* The **share price** doubled in the year after the listing.

### dividend
- **IPA:** /ˈdɪvɪdend/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A portion of a company's profit paid out to shareholders.
  - *Example:* The board raised the **dividend** for the fifth year running.

### yield
- **IPA:** /jiːld/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The income from an investment, expressed as a percentage of its price.
  - *Example:* The bond offers a **yield** of 4%.

### rate of return
- **IPA:** /ˌreɪt əv rɪˈtɜːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The profit on an investment over a period, as a percentage of the amount invested.
  - *Example:* The [property](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#1-property) gave a **rate of return** of 7% a year.

### capital gain
- **IPA:** /ˌkæpɪtl ˈɡeɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The profit made when an asset is sold for more than its purchase price.
  - *Example:* Selling the shares produced a large **capital gain**.

### principal
- **IPA:** /ˈprɪnsəpl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The original sum of money borrowed or invested, not counting interest.
  - *Example:* Each payment covers interest plus part of the **principal**.

### maturity
- **IPA:** /məˈtʃʊərəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The time when a loan or bond must be repaid or an investment ends.
  - *Example:* The bond reaches **maturity** in ten years.

### brokerage
- **IPA:** /ˈbrəʊkərɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A firm that buys and sells shares and other investments for clients.
  - *Example:* She opened an account with an online **brokerage**.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The fee charged for arranging a purchase or sale.
  - *Example:* **Brokerage** on the trade came to $12.

### trader
- **IPA:** /ˈtreɪdə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who buys and sells shares, currencies, or goods, often frequently.
  - *Example:* A **trader** on the desk spotted the price gap within seconds.

### fund
- **IPA:** /fʌnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A pool of money from many investors, managed together.
  - *Example:* The **fund** invests mainly in Asian companies.

### mutual fund
- **IPA:** /ˌmjuːtʃuəl ˈfʌnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An investment fund that pools money from many people to buy a mix of assets.
  - *Example:* A **mutual fund** spreads risk across hundreds of companies.

### hedge fund
- **IPA:** /ˈhedʒ fʌnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A private fund that uses advanced strategies to aim for high returns.
  - *Example:* The **hedge fund** made money even as markets fell.

### index fund
- **IPA:** /ˈɪndeks fʌnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fund that simply tracks a market index rather than picking stocks.
  - *Example:* Low fees make the **index fund** popular with [long-term](05-business-strategy-and-planning.md#long-term) savers.

### portfolio manager
- **IPA:** /pɔːtˈfəʊliəʊ ˌmænɪdʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A professional who makes investment decisions for a fund or client.
  - *Example:* The **portfolio manager** shifted money out of energy stocks.

### exposure
- **IPA:** /ɪkˈspəʊʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of risk an investor faces from a particular asset or market.
  - *Example:* The fund cut its **exposure** to the [property](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#1-property) sector.

### hedge
- **IPA:** /hedʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To make an investment that reduces the risk of loss on another.
  - *Example:* The exporter **hedged** against a fall in the dollar.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** An investment used to offset possible losses.
  - *Example:* Gold is often used as a **hedge** against [inflation](11-economics-and-markets.md#inflation).

### margin trading
- **IPA:** /ˈmɑːdʒɪn ˌtreɪdɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Buying investments with borrowed money, which raises both potential gains and losses.
  - *Example:* **Margin trading** wiped out his account when prices dropped.

### short-selling
- **IPA:** /ˈʃɔːt ˌselɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Selling borrowed shares in the hope of buying them back later at a lower price.
  - *Example:* **Short-selling** rises when investors expect a stock to fall.

### bull market
- **IPA:** /ˈbʊl ˌmɑːkɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period when investment prices are rising and confidence is high.
  - *Example:* The **bull market** lasted almost a decade.

### bear market
- **IPA:** /ˈbeə ˌmɑːkɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period when investment prices are falling and confidence is low.
  - *Example:* In a **bear market**, investors move money into cash.

### correction
- **IPA:** /kəˈrekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fall of around 10% in a market after a period of rising prices.
  - *Example:* Analysts called the drop a healthy **correction**, not a crash.

### valuation
- **IPA:** /ˌvæljuˈeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An estimate of how much a company or asset is worth.
  - *Example:* The startup's **valuation** reached $1 billion after the round.

### market capitalization
- **IPA:** /ˌmɑːkɪt ˌkæpɪtəlaɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The total value of a company's shares on the market.
  - *Example:* The firm's **market capitalization** now exceeds that of its parent.

### blue chip
- **IPA:** /ˈbluː tʃɪp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A share in a large, well-established, financially sound company.
  - *Example:* The [pension fund](12-banking-and-investment.md#pension-fund) holds mainly **blue chip** stocks.

### IPO
- **IPA:** /ˌaɪ piː ˈəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Initial public offering: the first sale of a company's shares to the public.
  - *Example:* The **IPO** raised $400 million for [expansion](05-business-strategy-and-planning.md#expansion).

### flotation
- **IPA:** /fləʊˈteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of offering a company's shares for sale on a stock exchange for the first time.
  - *Example:* The **flotation** valued the [retailer](16-retail-and-store-operations.md#retailer) at £2 billion.

### underwriting
- **IPA:** /ˈʌndəraɪtɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process by which a bank guarantees to buy unsold shares in a new issue.
  - *Example:* Two banks handled the **underwriting** of the share sale.

### custodian
- **IPA:** /kʌˈstəʊdiən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A financial institution that holds and safeguards a client's securities.
  - *Example:* A global bank acts as **custodian** for the fund's assets.

### clearing
- **IPA:** /ˈklɪərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of settling payments and transfers between banks.
  - *Example:* The transfer went through the **clearing** system overnight.

### liquidity
- **IPA:** /lɪˈkwɪdəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ease with which an asset can be turned into cash without losing value.
  - *Example:* [Property](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#1-property) has low **liquidity** compared with shares.

### solvency
- **IPA:** /ˈsɒlvənsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ability of a person or company to pay all its debts.
  - *Example:* The audit confirmed the firm's **solvency**.

### creditworthiness
- **IPA:** /ˈkredɪtwɜːðinəs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How safe it is to lend money to a person or organisation.
  - *Example:* The bank checks the **creditworthiness** of every borrower.

### credit rating
- **IPA:** /ˈkredɪt ˌreɪtɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An assessment of how likely a borrower is to repay debt.
  - *Example:* A lower **credit rating** means the company pays more to borrow.

### default
- **IPA:** /dɪˈfɔːlt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A failure to repay a loan or meet a financial obligation.
  - *Example:* The bond fell in price as fears of **default** grew.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To fail to make a required payment.
  - *Example:* The company **defaulted** on its loan in March.

### foreclosure
- **IPA:** /fɔːˈkləʊʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The legal process by which a lender takes property when a mortgage is not paid.
  - *Example:* The bank began **foreclosure** after a year of missed payments.

### refinance
- **IPA:** /ˌriːˈfaɪnæns/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To replace an existing loan with a new one, usually on better terms.
  - *Example:* They **refinanced** the [mortgage](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#5-mortgage) at a lower rate.

### amortize
- **IPA:** /əˈmɔːtaɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To pay off a debt gradually through regular payments, or to spread a cost over time.
  - *Example:* The loan is **amortized** over 25 years.

### compound interest
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒmpaʊnd ˈɪntrəst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Interest calculated on both the original sum and the interest already added.
  - *Example:* Thanks to **compound interest**, small early [savings](36-personal-finance-and-household-budget.md#savings) grow a lot.

### annuity
- **IPA:** /əˈnjuːəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A financial product that pays a fixed sum each year, often for life.
  - *Example:* She used her [pension](../toeic-600/18-employee-benefits-and-insurance.md#7-pension) pot to buy an **annuity**.

### pension fund
- **IPA:** /ˈpenʃn fʌnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large fund that collects and invests money to pay people's pensions.
  - *Example:* The **pension fund** is a major [shareholder](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#shareholder) in the airline.

### wealth management
- **IPA:** /ˈwelθ ˌmænɪdʒmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Professional advice and services for managing a wealthy person's money.
  - *Example:* The bank's **wealth management** arm advises family businesses.

### private equity
- **IPA:** /ˌpraɪvət ˈekwəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Investment in companies that are not listed on a stock exchange.
  - *Example:* A **private equity** firm bought the chain and cut costs.

### venture capital
- **IPA:** /ˈventʃə ˌkæpɪtl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money invested in new, high-risk companies with strong growth potential.
  - *Example:* The app was built on two rounds of **venture capital**.

### angel investor
- **IPA:** /ˈeɪndʒl ɪnˌvestə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A wealthy individual who provides early funding to a startup.
  - *Example:* An **angel investor** put in the first $50,000.

### due diligence
- **IPA:** /ˌdjuː ˈdɪlɪdʒəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A careful investigation of a business or asset before a deal.
  - *Example:* **Due diligence** uncovered an unpaid tax bill.

### term sheet
- **IPA:** /ˈtɜːm ʃiːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document setting out the main terms of a proposed investment before contracts.
  - *Example:* The **term sheet** fixed the [valuation](12-banking-and-investment.md#valuation) and the investor's rights.

### dividend yield
- **IPA:** /ˈdɪvɪdend jiːld/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The annual dividend expressed as a percentage of the share price.
  - *Example:* A **dividend yield** of 5% attracts income investors.

### capital markets
- **IPA:** /ˈkæpɪtl ˌmɑːkɪts/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The financial markets where long-term debt and shares are bought and sold.
  - *Example:* The firm raised money directly in the **capital markets**.

### treasury
- **IPA:** /ˈtreʒəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The department of a company or government that manages its money and debt.
  - *Example:* The **treasury** team manages the group's cash across 20 [currencies](../toeic-600/02-finance-and-banking.md#12-currency).

### securities
- **IPA:** /sɪˈkjʊərətiz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Tradable financial assets such as shares, bonds, and options, taken together.
  - *Example:* The [regulator](31-government-and-public-services.md#regulator) [oversees](03-management-and-leadership.md#oversee) trading in **securities**.

### derivative
- **IPA:** /dɪˈrɪvətɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A financial contract whose value depends on the price of another asset.
  - *Example:* The bank uses **derivatives** to manage [currency](../toeic-600/02-finance-and-banking.md#12-currency) risk.

### futures
- **IPA:** /ˈfjuːtʃəz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Contracts to buy or sell an asset at a fixed price on a future date.
  - *Example:* Airlines buy oil **futures** to lock in fuel costs.

### options
- **IPA:** /ˈɒpʃnz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Contracts giving the right, but not the duty, to buy or sell an asset at a set price.
  - *Example:* Staff were granted share **options** as part of their pay.

### portfolio rebalancing
- **IPA:** /pɔːtˈfəʊliəʊ ˌriːˈbælənsɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Adjusting the mix of investments back to a target after prices have moved.
  - *Example:* Annual **portfolio rebalancing** keeps the risk level steady.

### benchmark index
- **IPA:** /ˈbentʃmɑːk ˌɪndeks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A standard market index against which a fund's performance is measured.
  - *Example:* The fund beat its **benchmark index** by two points.

### risk appetite
- **IPA:** /ˈrɪsk ˌæpɪtaɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of risk a person or organisation is willing to accept.
  - *Example:* A [pension fund](12-banking-and-investment.md#pension-fund) has a low **risk appetite**.

### nest egg
- **IPA:** /ˈnest eɡ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sum of money saved for the future, especially for retirement.
  - *Example:* They built a **nest egg** over 30 years of steady saving.

### shareholding
- **IPA:** /ˈʃeəhəʊldɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The number of shares a person or organisation owns in a company.
  - *Example:* The family reduced its **shareholding** to 20%.

### stockbroker
- **IPA:** /ˈstɒkbrəʊkə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person or firm that buys and sells shares on behalf of clients.
  - *Example:* Her **stockbroker** advised holding the shares for another year.

### bondholder
- **IPA:** /ˈbɒndhəʊldə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person or institution that owns bonds issued by a company or government.
  - *Example:* **Bondholders** are paid before [shareholders](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#shareholder) if a company fails.

### maturity date
- **IPA:** /məˈtʃʊərəti deɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The date on which a bond or loan must be fully repaid.
  - *Example:* The **maturity date** on the loan is 30 June 2031.

### bond coupon
- **IPA:** /ˈbɒnd ˌkuːpɒn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The fixed interest payment made to a bondholder, usually stated as a yearly percentage.
  - *Example:* The **bond coupon** of 3% is paid in two instalments a year.

### face value
- **IPA:** /ˌfeɪs ˈvæljuː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount printed on a bond or note, repaid at maturity.
  - *Example:* The bond trades below its **face value** because rates have risen.

### book value
- **IPA:** /ˌbʊk ˈvæljuː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The value of a company's assets minus its liabilities, as shown in its accounts.
  - *Example:* The shares trade at twice their **book value**.

### market maker
- **IPA:** /ˈmɑːkɪt ˌmeɪkə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A firm that always offers to buy and sell a security, providing liquidity.
  - *Example:* A **market maker** quotes prices even when trading is thin.

### fixed income
- **IPA:** /ˌfɪkst ˈɪnkʌm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Investments such as bonds that pay a set amount of interest.
  - *Example:* Older savers often shift money into **fixed income**.

### asset class
- **IPA:** /ˈæset klɑːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A group of investments with similar features, such as shares, bonds, or property.
  - *Example:* Spreading money across **asset classes** lowers overall risk.

### buy-and-hold
- **IPA:** /ˌbaɪ ən ˈhəʊld/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Describing a strategy of keeping investments for a long time regardless of short-term moves.
  - *Example:* Her **buy-and-hold** approach ignores daily price swings.

### capital preservation
- **IPA:** /ˌkæpɪtl ˌprezəˈveɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An investment aim of protecting the original sum rather than seeking high returns.
  - *Example:* Near retirement, the focus shifts to **capital preservation**.
