# 29 — Health, Medicine & Wellness

### prognosis
- **IPA:** /prɒɡˈnəʊsɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A doctor's prediction of how an illness will develop.
  - *Example:* With early [treatment](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#5-treatment), the **prognosis** is good.

### syndrome
- **IPA:** /ˈsɪndrəʊm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A group of symptoms that regularly occur together and indicate a condition.
  - *Example:* The **syndrome** combines [fatigue](17-manufacturing-and-engineering.md#fatigue), joint pain, and poor sleep.

### condition
- **IPA:** /kənˈdɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An illness or medical problem, especially a long-lasting one.
  - *Example:* She manages the **condition** with diet and [medication](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#18-medication).

### disorder
- **IPA:** /dɪsˈɔːdə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An illness that disrupts the normal function of the body or mind.
  - *Example:* A sleep **disorder** left him exhausted for months.

### ailment
- **IPA:** /ˈeɪlmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A minor illness.
  - *Example:* The clinic treats common **ailments** like coughs and sprains.

### chronic illness
- **IPA:** /ˌkrɒnɪk ˈɪlnəs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A disease that lasts a long time and is often not fully curable.
  - *Example:* [Support](04-teamwork-and-workplace-relationships.md#support) groups help people cope with **chronic illness**.

### acute
- **IPA:** /əˈkjuːt/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of an illness) Severe and sudden, but usually short.
  - *Example:* He was admitted with an **acute** [infection](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#infection).

### remission
- **IPA:** /rɪˈmɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period when the symptoms of a disease are reduced or absent.
  - *Example:* The cancer has been in **remission** for three years.

### relapse
- **IPA:** /rɪˈlæps/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A return of an illness after a period of improvement.
  - *Example:* Stopping the [medication](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#18-medication) early can cause a **relapse**.

### complication
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒmplɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An extra medical problem that develops during an illness or after treatment.
  - *Example:* The [surgery](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#surgery) went well, with no **complications**.

### comorbidity
- **IPA:** /ˌkəʊmɔːˈbɪdəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The presence of one or more additional conditions alongside a main one.
  - *Example:* Diabetes is a common **comorbidity** in older heart [patients](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#patient).

### screening
- **IPA:** /ˈskriːnɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Testing large numbers of people to find early signs of a disease.
  - *Example:* Regular **screening** catches the condition before [symptoms](10-problem-solving-and-decision-making.md#symptom) appear.

### vaccination
- **IPA:** /ˌvæksɪˈneɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The giving of a vaccine to protect against a disease.
  - *Example:* **Vaccination** rates rose after the mobile clinic visited.

### booster
- **IPA:** /ˈbuːstə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An extra dose of a vaccine given later to keep protection strong.
  - *Example:* Staff were offered a **booster** before the winter season.

### antibody
- **IPA:** /ˈæntibɒdi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A protein made by the body to fight a specific germ.
  - *Example:* A blood test showed high levels of **antibody** after [infection](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#infection).

### immunity
- **IPA:** /ɪˈmjuːnəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The body's ability to resist a particular infection.
  - *Example:* **Immunity** from the vaccine fades over time.

### pathogen
- **IPA:** /ˈpæθədʒən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A microorganism, such as a bacterium or virus, that causes disease.
  - *Example:* The **pathogen** spreads through contaminated water.

### infection
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈfekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The invasion of the body by germs that cause disease.
  - *Example:* The wound became red, a sign of **infection**.

### inflammation
- **IPA:** /ˌɪnfləˈmeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reaction in which part of the body becomes red, swollen, and painful.
  - *Example:* The cream reduces **inflammation** in the joint.

### contraindication
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒntrəˌɪndɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reason why a particular treatment should not be used for a patient.
  - *Example:* High [blood pressure](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#blood-pressure) is a **contraindication** for this medicine.

### adverse reaction
- **IPA:** /ˌædvɜːs riˈækʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A harmful and unwanted response to a drug or treatment.
  - *Example:* Any **adverse reaction** must be reported to the doctor.

### generic drug
- **IPA:** /dʒəˌnerɪk ˈdrʌɡ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A medicine sold under its chemical name rather than a brand, usually cheaper.
  - *Example:* The pharmacist offered a **generic drug** at a fraction of the price.

### over-the-counter
- **IPA:** /ˌəʊvə ðə ˈkaʊntə/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of medicine) Sold without a prescription.
  - *Example:* **Over-the-counter** painkillers were enough for the headache.

### therapy
- **IPA:** /ˈθerəpi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Treatment intended to relieve or cure an illness or difficulty.
  - *Example:* Speech **therapy** helped him [recover](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#12-recover) after the stroke.

### physiotherapy
- **IPA:** /ˌfɪziəʊˈθerəpi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Treatment of injury or weakness through exercise and massage.
  - *Example:* Six weeks of **physiotherapy** restored movement in the shoulder.

### surgery
- **IPA:** /ˈsɜːdʒəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Medical treatment in which the body is cut open to repair or remove something.
  - *Example:* She needs **surgery** to replace the worn joint.

### anaesthetic
- **IPA:** /ˌænəsˈθetɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A drug that removes feeling or causes sleep during surgery.
  - *Example:* The tooth was removed under a local **anaesthetic**.

### incision
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈsɪʒn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A cut made in the body during surgery.
  - *Example:* The **incision** was small and healed within a week.

### suture
- **IPA:** /ˈsuːtʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A stitch used to hold the edges of a wound together.
  - *Example:* The nurse removed the **sutures** after ten days.

### general practitioner
- **IPA:** /ˌdʒenrəl prækˈtɪʃənə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A doctor who treats common conditions and refers patients to specialists.
  - *Example:* See your **general practitioner** first for a [referral](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#21-referral).

### inpatient
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnpeɪʃnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A patient who stays in hospital overnight or longer for treatment.
  - *Example:* As an **inpatient**, she was [monitored](06-projects-and-deadlines.md#monitor) for three days.

### ward
- **IPA:** /wɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A room or section of a hospital for patients with similar needs.
  - *Example:* He was moved from [intensive care](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#intensive-care) to a general **ward**.

### intensive care
- **IPA:** /ɪnˌtensɪv ˈkeə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Hospital treatment for people who are very seriously ill, with constant monitoring.
  - *Example:* The [patient](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#patient) spent two nights in **intensive care**.

### triage
- **IPA:** /ˈtriːɑːʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The sorting of patients by how urgently they need treatment.
  - *Example:* **Triage** at the door sends the most serious cases straight through.

### ambulance
- **IPA:** /ˈæmbjələns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A vehicle for taking sick or injured people to hospital.
  - *Example:* An **ambulance** reached the site within eight minutes.

### paramedic
- **IPA:** /ˌpærəˈmedɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A trained person who gives emergency medical care, often in an ambulance.
  - *Example:* The **paramedic** stabilised the [patient](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#patient) on the way to [hospital](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#hospital).

### first responder
- **IPA:** /ˌfɜːst rɪˈspɒndə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A trained person who is first to arrive and give help at an emergency.
  - *Example:* Two staff act as **first responders** for the whole factory.

### defibrillator
- **IPA:** /diːˈfɪbrɪleɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that gives an electric shock to restart a stopped heart.
  - *Example:* A public **defibrillator** is fixed to the wall by the entrance.

### blood pressure
- **IPA:** /ˈblʌd ˌpreʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries.
  - *Example:* His **blood pressure** came down after he cut out salt.

### cholesterol
- **IPA:** /kəˈlestərɒl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fatty substance in the blood; too much raises the risk of heart disease.
  - *Example:* A blood test showed her **cholesterol** was slightly high.

### blood sugar
- **IPA:** /ˈblʌd ˌʃʊɡə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The level of glucose in the blood.
  - *Example:* People with diabetes check their **blood sugar** several times a day.

### obesity
- **IPA:** /əʊˈbiːsəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The condition of being very overweight in a way that harms health.
  - *Example:* The programme tackles childhood **obesity** through school meals.

### nutrition
- **IPA:** /njuˈtrɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of getting the food needed for health and growth; the study of this.
  - *Example:* Good **nutrition** speeds [recovery](11-economics-and-markets.md#recovery) after illness.

### dietitian
- **IPA:** /ˌdaɪəˈtɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An expert who advises people on what to eat for health or a medical condition.
  - *Example:* A **dietitian** drew up a meal plan for the diabetic [patients](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#patient).

### calorie
- **IPA:** /ˈkæləri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A unit for measuring the energy that food provides.
  - *Example:* The menu now shows the **calorie** count for each dish.

### supplement
- **IPA:** /ˈsʌplɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A vitamin, mineral, or other product taken to add to the diet.
  - *Example:* The doctor recommended an iron **supplement**.

### hydration
- **IPA:** /haɪˈdreɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The state of having enough water in the body.
  - *Example:* **Hydration** matters as much as food during long shifts in the heat.

### sedentary
- **IPA:** /ˈsedntri/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Involving a lot of sitting and little physical activity.
  - *Example:* A **sedentary** job raises the need for [exercise](39-leisure-hobbies-and-sports.md#exercise) outside work.

### aerobic
- **IPA:** /eəˈrəʊbɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of exercise) Increasing the heart rate and breathing to improve fitness.
  - *Example:* Thirty minutes of **aerobic** [exercise](39-leisure-hobbies-and-sports.md#exercise) a day was advised.

### strength training
- **IPA:** /ˈstreŋθ ˌtreɪnɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Exercise using weights or resistance to build muscle.
  - *Example:* **Strength training** twice a week helps protect the bones.

### flexibility
- **IPA:** /ˌfleksəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ability to bend and stretch the body easily.
  - *Example:* [Stretching](39-leisure-hobbies-and-sports.md#stretching) improved his **flexibility** and reduced back pain.

### mindfulness
- **IPA:** /ˈmaɪndflnəs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The practice of paying calm attention to the present moment.
  - *Example:* The company offers **mindfulness** [sessions](25-information-technology.md#session) at lunchtime.

### meditation
- **IPA:** /ˌmedɪˈteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The practice of focusing the mind quietly, often to reduce stress.
  - *Example:* Ten minutes of **meditation** each morning steadied her nerves.

### stress management
- **IPA:** /ˈstres ˌmænɪdʒmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Techniques and habits for keeping stress at a healthy level.
  - *Example:* The [workshop](../toeic-600/10-manufacturing-and-production.md#16-workshop) covered **stress management** for busy teams.

### sleep hygiene
- **IPA:** /ˈsliːp ˌhaɪdʒiːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Habits that help a person sleep well, such as a regular bedtime and no screens late.
  - *Example:* Better **sleep hygiene** cut the number of restless nights.

### wellbeing
- **IPA:** /welˈbiːɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The state of being comfortable, healthy, and happy.
  - *Example:* Staff **wellbeing** is now measured in the annual survey.

### preventive care
- **IPA:** /prɪˌventɪv ˈkeə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Medical care aimed at stopping illness before it starts, such as check-ups and vaccines.
  - *Example:* Insurers cover **preventive care** in full to reduce later costs.

### health insurance premium
- **IPA:** /ˈhelθ ɪnˌʃʊərəns ˌpriːmiəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The regular payment made for a health insurance policy.
  - *Example:* The **health insurance premium** rose 9% at [renewal](../toeic-600/01-contracts-and-agreements.md#14-renewal).

### co-pay
- **IPA:** /ˈkəʊ peɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fixed amount a patient pays for a service, with insurance covering the rest.
  - *Example:* There is a small **co-pay** for each visit to the clinic.

### patient
- **IPA:** /ˈpeɪʃnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person receiving medical treatment.
  - *Example:* Each nurse looks after up to eight **patients** on the ward.

### hospital
- **IPA:** /ˈhɒspɪtl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A place where sick or injured people are treated and cared for.
  - *Example:* The nearest **hospital** with an emergency unit is 20 minutes away.

### doctor
- **IPA:** /ˈdɒktə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person qualified to treat sick people.
  - *Example:* The **doctor** ordered blood tests and a scan.

### nurse
- **IPA:** /nɜːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person trained to care for sick or injured people, especially in a hospital.
  - *Example:* A **nurse** checked his [temperature](34-weather-climate-and-natural-events.md#temperature) every hour.

### healthcare
- **IPA:** /ˈhelθkeə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The services and system that provide medical treatment and prevention.
  - *Example:* The company added dental cover to its **healthcare** package.

### medical record
- **IPA:** /ˈmedɪkl ˌrekɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A written or electronic file of a patient's history and treatment.
  - *Example:* The [specialist](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#15-specialist) requested the patient's full **medical record**.

### medical history
- **IPA:** /ˈmedɪkl ˌhɪstri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A record of a person's past illnesses, operations, and treatments.
  - *Example:* The form asks for your family **medical history**.

### physical examination
- **IPA:** /ˌfɪzɪkl ɪɡˌzæmɪˈneɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A doctor's hands-on check of the body for signs of illness.
  - *Example:* A routine **physical examination** found the raised [blood pressure](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#blood-pressure).

### treatment plan
- **IPA:** /ˈtriːtmənt plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A written outline of the steps a patient will take to treat a condition.
  - *Example:* The **treatment plan** combines [medication](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#18-medication) with weekly [therapy](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#therapy).

### primary care
- **IPA:** /ˌpraɪməri ˈkeə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The first level of health care, given by GPs and local clinics.
  - *Example:* [Investment](../toeic-600/02-finance-and-banking.md#14-investment) in **primary care** eased pressure on [hospitals](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#hospital).

### health check
- **IPA:** /ˈhelθ tʃek/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A general examination to assess a person's overall health.
  - *Example:* Staff over 50 are offered a free annual **health check**.

### ward round
- **IPA:** /ˈwɔːd raʊnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A regular visit by doctors to each patient on a hospital ward.
  - *Example:* Decisions about [discharge](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#30-discharge) are made on the morning **ward round**.

### medical staff
- **IPA:** /ˈmedɪkl stɑːf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The doctors, nurses, and other trained people who provide care.
  - *Example:* Extra **medical staff** were brought in during the flu season.

### clinical trial
- **IPA:** /ˌklɪnɪkl ˈtraɪəl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A carefully controlled test of a new treatment on volunteers.
  - *Example:* The drug is in a large **clinical trial** across twelve [hospitals](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#hospital).

### symptom management
- **IPA:** /ˈsɪmptəm ˌmænɪdʒmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Treatment aimed at reducing discomfort rather than curing the disease.
  - *Example:* When a cure is not possible, care focuses on **symptom management**.

### care plan
- **IPA:** /ˈkeə plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document setting out the support and treatment a person needs and who provides it.
  - *Example:* The **care plan** is reviewed with the family every three months.
