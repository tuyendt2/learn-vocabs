# 22 — Real Estate & Property Development

> Cross-links jump to another entry in the 3000-word set. **Ctrl/Cmd + click** to follow.
> Words from `../toeic-600/` are left as plain text.

### development
- **IPA:** /dɪˈveləpmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An area of land with new buildings on it; the act of building on land.
  - *Example:* The **development** will have 120 flats and a small park.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The process of growing or improving.
  - *Example:* The **development** of the brand took a decade.

### plot
- **IPA:** /plɒt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small piece of land marked out for building or another use.
  - *Example:* They bought a corner **plot** with room for parking.

### parcel
- **IPA:** /ˈpɑːsl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A defined piece of land, especially one being bought or sold.
  - *Example:* The farm was split into three **parcels** for sale.

### site
- **IPA:** /saɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A piece of ground where something is located or will be built.
  - *Example:* The old depot **site** is being cleared for housing.

### acreage
- **IPA:** /ˈeɪkərɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An area of land measured in acres.
  - *Example:* The **acreage** is large enough for a [warehouse](../toeic-600/11-logistics-shipping-and-warehousing.md#15-warehouse) and a yard.

### rezoning
- **IPA:** /ˌriːˈzəʊnɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official change to the permitted use of a piece of land.
  - *Example:* **Rezoning** the site from industrial to [residential](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#9-residential) raised its value sharply.

### land use
- **IPA:** /ˈlænd juːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The purpose for which an area of land is used or allowed to be used.
  - *Example:* The plan sets out **land use** for the whole district.

### entitlement
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈtaɪtlmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The official rights to develop a piece of land in a particular way.
  - *Example:* The price includes full planning **entitlement** for 80 homes.

### easement
- **IPA:** /ˈiːzmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A legal right to use part of someone else's land for a specific purpose.
  - *Example:* The [utility](19-energy-and-utilities.md#utility) has an **easement** to reach the pipes under the garden.

### right of way
- **IPA:** /ˌraɪt əv ˈweɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A legal right to pass along a route through another person's land.
  - *Example:* A public **right of way** crosses the corner of the field.

### encumbrance
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈkʌmbrəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A claim or restriction on a property, such as a mortgage or right of way.
  - *Example:* The search revealed an **encumbrance** that limited building near the boundary.

### title deed
- **IPA:** /ˈtaɪtl diːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A legal document proving who owns a piece of property.
  - *Example:* The **title deed** is held by the lender until the [mortgage](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#5-mortgage) is repaid.

### conveyancing
- **IPA:** /kənˈveɪənsɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The legal work of transferring property from one owner to another.
  - *Example:* **Conveyancing** on the flat took ten weeks.

### closing
- **IPA:** /ˈkləʊzɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The final meeting at which a property sale is completed and money changes hands.
  - *Example:* Both lawyers attended the **closing** to [exchange](../toeic-600/09-sales-and-purchasing.md#23-exchange) documents.

### escrow account
- **IPA:** /ˈeskrəʊ əˌkaʊnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An account held by a third party where money is kept until a deal is complete.
  - *Example:* The [deposit](../toeic-600/02-finance-and-banking.md#2-deposit) sat in an **escrow account** until closing.

### comparable
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒmpərəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A recently sold property used to help estimate the value of another.
  - *Example:* The valuer based the figure on three local **comparables**.

### square metre
- **IPA:** /ˌskweə ˈmiːtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A unit of area used to measure and price floor space.
  - *Example:* Office rent in the district is €400 per **square metre** a year.

### floor area ratio
- **IPA:** /ˈflɔː ˌeəriə ˌreɪʃiəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ratio of a building's total floor space to the size of the plot it sits on.
  - *Example:* A higher **floor area ratio** lets the [developer](18-construction-and-building-trades.md#developer) build more storeys.

### building setback
- **IPA:** /ˈbɪldɪŋ ˌsetbæk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The minimum distance a building must be kept from the road or a boundary.
  - *Example:* The design was changed to meet the six-metre **building setback**.

### density
- **IPA:** /ˈdensəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The number of people or homes in a given area of land.
  - *Example:* Residents objected to the high **density** of the proposed scheme.

### mixed-use
- **IPA:** /ˌmɪkst ˈjuːs/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Combining homes, shops, offices, or leisure in one development.
  - *Example:* The **mixed-use** block has shops below and flats above.

### commercial property
- **IPA:** /kəˌmɜːʃl ˈprɒpəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Property used for business, such as shops, offices, or warehouses.
  - *Example:* **Commercial property** values fell as more staff worked from home.

### residential property
- **IPA:** /ˌrezɪˈdenʃl ˈprɒpəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Property used as housing.
  - *Example:* The fund invests only in **residential property**.

### industrial estate
- **IPA:** /ɪnˌdʌstriəl ɪˈsteɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An area set aside for factories, workshops, and warehouses.
  - *Example:* The unit is on an **industrial estate** near the motorway.

### retail unit
- **IPA:** /ˈriːteɪl ˌjuːnɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A single shop space within a larger building or development.
  - *Example:* Two **retail units** on the ground floor are still to let.

### office space
- **IPA:** /ˈɒfɪs speɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Floor area used or available for offices.
  - *Example:* The company sublet half its **office space** after [downsizing](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#downsizing).

### occupancy rate
- **IPA:** /ˈɒkjəpənsi reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The percentage of available space or rooms that is currently in use.
  - *Example:* The building's **occupancy rate** climbed back to 90%.

### vacancy rate
- **IPA:** /ˈveɪkənsi reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The percentage of available property that is empty and unlet.
  - *Example:* A rising **vacancy rate** forced [landlords](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#4-landlord) to cut rents.

### absorption rate
- **IPA:** /əbˈzɔːpʃn reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The speed at which available property in a market is rented or sold.
  - *Example:* A fast **absorption rate** meant the new flats sold within months.

### ground lease
- **IPA:** /ˈɡraʊnd liːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A long lease of land on which the tenant may build, with the building reverting to the owner at the end.
  - *Example:* The tower sits on a 99-year **ground lease**.

### leaseback
- **IPA:** /ˈliːsbæk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An arrangement where the seller of a property immediately rents it back from the buyer.
  - *Example:* The [retailer](16-retail-and-store-operations.md#retailer) sold its stores and took a **leaseback** to raise cash.

### anchor tenant
- **IPA:** /ˈæŋkə ˌtenənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large, well-known tenant that draws customers and other tenants to a development.
  - *Example:* A supermarket signed as the **anchor tenant** for the [retail park](16-retail-and-store-operations.md#retail-park).

### common area
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒmən ˌeəriə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Parts of a building shared by all occupants, such as halls, lifts, and gardens.
  - *Example:* The [service charge](../toeic-600/16-restaurants-and-dining.md#30-service-charge) covers cleaning of the **common areas**.

### dilapidations
- **IPA:** /dɪˌlæpɪˈdeɪʃnz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Repairs a tenant must pay for at the end of a lease to return a property to good condition.
  - *Example:* The **dilapidations** bill at lease-end came to £40,000.

### shell and core
- **IPA:** /ˌʃel ən ˈkɔː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A building completed only to its basic structure, leaving the interior for the tenant to finish.
  - *Example:* The office was handed over as **shell and core** for the tenant's own [fit-out](18-construction-and-building-trades.md#fit-out).

### off-plan
- **IPA:** /ˌɒf ˈplæn/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Bought before the building is finished, based on drawings and plans.
  - *Example:* They bought the apartment **off-plan** at a lower price.

### pre-sale
- **IPA:** /ˈpriː seɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The sale of units in a development before construction is complete.
  - *Example:* Strong **pre-sales** helped the [developer](18-construction-and-building-trades.md#developer) secure a construction loan.

### show home
- **IPA:** /ˈʃəʊ həʊm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A finished and furnished house on a new development, used to attract buyers.
  - *Example:* The **show home** is open to visitors at weekends.

### letting agent
- **IPA:** /ˈletɪŋ ˌeɪdʒənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that finds tenants and manages rented property for landlords.
  - *Example:* The **letting agent** takes 10% of the monthly rent.

### property manager
- **IPA:** /ˈprɒpəti ˌmænɪdʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person or firm responsible for the day-to-day running of a building.
  - *Example:* The **property manager** arranges repairs and collects the rent.

### portfolio landlord
- **IPA:** /pɔːtˈfəʊliəʊ ˌlændlɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A landlord who owns several rented properties.
  - *Example:* New rules made borrowing harder for **portfolio landlords**.

### buy-to-let
- **IPA:** /ˌbaɪ tə ˈlet/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Describing property bought as an investment to rent out.
  - *Example:* He took out a **buy-to-let** [mortgage](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#5-mortgage) on a two-bed flat.

### capital appreciation
- **IPA:** /ˌkæpɪtl əˌpriːʃiˈeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An increase in the value of a property or asset over time.
  - *Example:* The return came mostly from **capital appreciation**, not rent.

### rental yield
- **IPA:** /ˈrentl jiːld/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The annual rent from a property as a percentage of its value.
  - *Example:* A **rental yield** of 6% made the flat a solid [investment](../toeic-600/02-finance-and-banking.md#14-investment).

### gross yield
- **IPA:** /ˌɡrəʊs ˈjiːld/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Annual rent as a percentage of property value, before costs are deducted.
  - *Example:* The **gross yield** looks good until you subtract the [service charge](../toeic-600/16-restaurants-and-dining.md#30-service-charge).

### net yield
- **IPA:** /ˌnet ˈjiːld/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Annual rent as a percentage of property value, after costs such as management and repairs.
  - *Example:* Once fees were counted, the **net yield** was only 3.5%.

### void period
- **IPA:** /ˈvɔɪd ˌpɪəriəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A time when a rental property is empty and earning no rent.
  - *Example:* A two-month **void period** wiped out much of the year's profit.

### tenancy agreement
- **IPA:** /ˈtenənsi əˌɡriːmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The contract setting out the terms on which a property is rented.
  - *Example:* The **tenancy agreement** runs for twelve months with a break option.

### break clause
- **IPA:** /ˈbreɪk klɔːz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A term in a lease letting either side end it early on certain conditions.
  - *Example:* The **break clause** allows the tenant to leave after two years.

### rent review
- **IPA:** /ˈrent rɪˌvjuː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A point in a lease when the rent can be adjusted, usually upward.
  - *Example:* The five-year **rent review** raised the office rent by 18%.

### gearing
- **IPA:** /ˈɡɪərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The level of borrowing compared with the value of the assets it funds.
  - *Example:* High **gearing** boosts returns in a rising market but is risky in a fall.

### loan-to-value
- **IPA:** /ˌləʊn tə ˈvæljuː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The size of a loan as a percentage of the value of the property securing it.
  - *Example:* The bank would lend only up to 70% **loan-to-value**.

### mortgage broker
- **IPA:** /ˈmɔːɡɪdʒ ˌbrəʊkə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who arranges mortgages between borrowers and lenders for a fee.
  - *Example:* A **mortgage broker** found them a better rate than their own bank.

### chartered surveyor
- **IPA:** /ˌtʃɑːtəd səˈveɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A professionally qualified surveyor who values property and advises on its condition.
  - *Example:* A **chartered surveyor** valued the building for the loan.

### planning gain
- **IPA:** /ˈplænɪŋ ɡeɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A benefit, such as a park or affordable homes, that a developer provides in return for planning permission.
  - *Example:* The **planning gain** included a new footbridge over the railway.

### brownfield
- **IPA:** /ˈbraʊnfiːld/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of land) Previously built on and available for redevelopment.
  - *Example:* The [council](31-government-and-public-services.md#council) prefers housing on **brownfield** land near the town centre.

### greenfield
- **IPA:** /ˈɡriːnfiːld/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of land) Never built on before, often farmland or open countryside.
  - *Example:* Building on **greenfield** sites met strong local [opposition](31-government-and-public-services.md#opposition).

### infrastructure levy
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnfrəstrʌktʃə ˌlevi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A charge on new development to help pay for roads, schools, and services.
  - *Example:* The **infrastructure levy** on the scheme funded improvements to the junction.

### master plan
- **IPA:** /ˈmɑːstə plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A long-term plan setting out how a large area will be developed.
  - *Example:* The **master plan** phases 2,000 homes over fifteen years.

### real estate
- **IPA:** /ˈrɪəl ɪˌsteɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Land and the buildings on it, considered as property.
  - *Example:* The company put much of its cash into [commercial](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#8-commercial) **real estate**.

### planning application
- **IPA:** /ˈplænɪŋ ˌæplɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal request to a local authority for permission to build or change a property.
  - *Example:* The **planning application** attracted 200 [objections](09-negotiation-and-persuasion.md#objection).

### development site
- **IPA:** /dɪˈveləpmənt saɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A piece of land bought or held for building on.
  - *Example:* The firm holds three **development sites** near the new station.

### property portfolio
- **IPA:** /ˈprɒpəti pɔːtˌfəʊliəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The collection of properties owned by an investor or company.
  - *Example:* The [pension fund](12-banking-and-investment.md#pension-fund)'s **property portfolio** spans offices, shops, and [warehouses](../toeic-600/11-logistics-shipping-and-warehousing.md#15-warehouse).

### rental income
- **IPA:** /ˈrentl ˌɪnkʌm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The money received from letting out property.
  - *Example:* Steady **rental income** covers the loan repayments with room to spare.

### asking price
- **IPA:** /ˈɑːskɪŋ praɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The price at which a property is offered for sale.
  - *Example:* The flat sold for £15,000 below the **asking price**.

### land bank
- **IPA:** /ˈlænd bæŋk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A supply of land held by a developer for future building.
  - *Example:* The housebuilder's **land bank** holds enough sites for eight years.

### site acquisition
- **IPA:** /ˈsaɪt ˌækwɪˈzɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of finding and buying land for development.
  - *Example:* A dedicated team handles **site acquisition** across the region.

### ground-floor unit
- **IPA:** /ˌɡraʊnd flɔː ˈjuːnɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A space on the lowest floor of a building, often used for shops or reception.
  - *Example:* The **ground-floor unit** was fitted out as a café.

### site plan
- **IPA:** /ˈsaɪt plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A drawing showing the layout of buildings, roads, and spaces on a development.
  - *Example:* The **site plan** places parking behind the block, away from the street.

### property developer
- **IPA:** /ˈprɒpəti dɪˌveləpə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person or company that buys land and builds on it for profit.
  - *Example:* A local **property developer** turned the mill into loft apartments.

### landholding
- **IPA:** /ˈlændhəʊldɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An area of land owned by a person or organisation.
  - *Example:* The estate's **landholding** stretches for several miles along the coast.

### lettable area
- **IPA:** /ˈletəbl ˌeəriə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The floor space in a building that can actually be rented to tenants.
  - *Example:* Corridors and plant rooms are not counted in the **lettable area**.

### rent-free period
- **IPA:** /ˌrent friː ˈpɪəriəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A time at the start of a lease when the tenant pays no rent, offered as an incentive.
  - *Example:* The [landlord](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#4-landlord) offered a three-month **rent-free period** to attract a tenant.

### development plan
- **IPA:** /dɪˈveləpmənt plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official document setting out how land in an area should be used and built on.
  - *Example:* The [proposal](../toeic-600/06-meetings-and-presentations.md#13-proposal) did not fit the district's **development plan**.

### property listing
- **IPA:** /ˈprɒpəti ˌlɪstɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An advertisement offering a property for sale or rent, with details and photos.
  - *Example:* The **property listing** described the flat as "in need of modernisation".
