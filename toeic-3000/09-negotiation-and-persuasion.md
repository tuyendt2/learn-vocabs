# 09 — Negotiation & Persuasion

### bargain
- **IPA:** /ˈbɑːɡɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To discuss the terms or price of something in order to reach a deal.
  - *Example:* They **bargained** for an hour over the delivery date.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** An agreement reached after discussion of terms.
  - *Example:* We struck a **bargain**: a lower price for a longer contract.

### haggle
- **IPA:** /ˈhæɡl/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To argue persistently over the price or terms of something.
  - *Example:* He **haggled** the supplier down by five percent.

### mediate
- **IPA:** /ˈmiːdieɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To try to settle a dispute between two sides by helping them agree.
  - *Example:* HR was asked to **mediate** between the two departments.

### arbitrate
- **IPA:** /ˈɑːbɪtreɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To settle a dispute by making an official, binding judgement.
  - *Example:* An independent panel will **arbitrate** the pay claim.

### broker
- **IPA:** /ˈbrəʊkə/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To arrange an agreement between others.
  - *Example:* A trade body **brokered** a deal between the rival firms.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A person who buys and sells things for others.
  - *Example:* An insurance **broker** compared five policies for us.

### concede
- **IPA:** /kənˈsiːd/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To admit that something is true or to give something up in a negotiation.
  - *Example:* Management **conceded** an extra day of leave.

### compromise
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒmprəmaɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An agreement reached when each side gives up part of what it wanted.
  - *Example:* The final schedule was a **compromise** that suited neither side fully.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To reach agreement by each side making concessions.
  - *Example:* They **compromised** on a mid-range price.
- **Meaning 3 (v):** To put something at risk or weaken it.
  - *Example:* Cutting checks would **compromise** safety.

### counteroffer
- **IPA:** /ˈkaʊntərɒfə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An offer made in response to one that was not accepted.
  - *Example:* We rejected the first bid and made a **counteroffer**.

### offer
- **IPA:** /ˈɒfə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A statement of what one is willing to give or do.
  - *Example:* Their **offer** covered the goods but not the shipping.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To present something for someone to accept or refuse.
  - *Example:* We **offered** a discount for early payment.

### bid
- **IPA:** /bɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An offer to pay a particular price or to do work for a stated sum.
  - *Example:* The lowest **bid** was still above budget.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To offer to pay a price or do a job for a set amount.
  - *Example:* Four firms **bid** for the maintenance contract.

### conditions
- **IPA:** /kənˈdɪʃnz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The things that must be agreed or done as part of a deal.
  - *Example:* The loan came with strict **conditions** on spending.

### stipulation
- **IPA:** /ˌstɪpjuˈleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A specific demand or condition that is part of an agreement.
  - *Example:* One **stipulation** was that the work finish before the holidays.

### leverage
- **IPA:** /ˈliːvərɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Power or advantage that helps you influence a situation or negotiation.
  - *Example:* Being their only local supplier gave us real **leverage**.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To use something to maximum advantage.
  - *Example:* The firm **leveraged** its brand to enter a new market.

### bargaining power
- **IPA:** /ˈbɑːɡɪnɪŋ ˌpaʊə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The strength one side has to get favourable terms in a negotiation.
  - *Example:* A full order book increased the factory's **bargaining power**.

### upper hand
- **IPA:** /ˌʌpər ˈhænd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A position of advantage or control.
  - *Example:* With three other offers in hand, the seller had the **upper hand**.

### standoff
- **IPA:** /ˈstændɒf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A situation in which neither side can win or will give way.
  - *Example:* The talks ended in a **standoff** over pension terms.

### deadlock
- **IPA:** /ˈdedlɒk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A situation in which no progress can be made because neither side will move.
  - *Example:* An outside mediator broke the **deadlock**.

### stalemate
- **IPA:** /ˈsteɪlmeɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A situation in which further action by either side is impossible; a draw.
  - *Example:* After six meetings, the negotiation reached a **stalemate**.

### impasse
- **IPA:** /ˈæmpɑːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A point at which no agreement or progress is possible.
  - *Example:* We are at an **impasse** on price and need a new approach.

### concession
- **IPA:** /kənˈseʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Something given up in order to reach agreement.
  - *Example:* In exchange for a **concession** on price, they accepted faster payment.

### trade-off
- **IPA:** /ˈtreɪd ɒf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A balance between two things that cannot both be fully had.
  - *Example:* There is a **trade-off** between speed and cost.

### middle ground
- **IPA:** /ˌmɪdl ˈɡraʊnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A position between two opposing views that both sides can accept.
  - *Example:* A three-year term was the **middle ground** both sides took.

### walk away
- **IPA:** /ˌwɔːk əˈweɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (phr v):** To leave a negotiation without making a deal.
  - *Example:* Be ready to **walk away** if the terms are unfair.

### bottom line
- **IPA:** /ˌbɒtəm ˈlaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The lowest price or minimum terms someone will accept.
  - *Example:* Our **bottom line** is delivery within four weeks.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The most important fact or the final result.
  - *Example:* The **bottom line** is that we cannot afford both.

### ballpark
- **IPA:** /ˈbɔːlpɑːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An approximate range or figure.
  - *Example:* Give me a **ballpark** cost before we go further.

### sticking point
- **IPA:** /ˈstɪkɪŋ pɔɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An issue on which disagreement prevents an agreement.
  - *Example:* The warranty length was the main **sticking point**.

### deal-breaker
- **IPA:** /ˈdiːl ˌbreɪkə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An issue serious enough to stop an agreement completely.
  - *Example:* No local support would be a **deal-breaker** for us.

### ultimatum
- **IPA:** /ˌʌltɪˈmeɪtəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A final demand, with a threat of consequences if it is refused.
  - *Example:* The supplier issued an **ultimatum**: pay by Friday or the order is cancelled.

### sweetener
- **IPA:** /ˈswiːtnə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Something extra offered to make a deal more attractive.
  - *Example:* Free installation was added as a **sweetener**.

### persuade
- **IPA:** /pəˈsweɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To make someone agree to do or believe something by giving reasons.
  - *Example:* She **persuaded** the board to fund a small trial.

### convince
- **IPA:** /kənˈvɪns/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To make someone feel certain that something is true.
  - *Example:* The figures **convinced** him that the plan would work.

### influence
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnfluəns/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To affect the way someone thinks or acts.
  - *Example:* Reviews strongly **influence** what customers buy.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The power to affect people or events.
  - *Example:* She has a lot of **influence** over hiring decisions.

### sway
- **IPA:** /sweɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To influence someone's opinion or decision.
  - *Example:* A strong guarantee **swayed** the committee.

### coax
- **IPA:** /kəʊks/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To gently and patiently persuade someone to do something.
  - *Example:* He **coaxed** the client into a longer contract with small extras.

### lobby
- **IPA:** /ˈlɒbi/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To try to persuade those in power to support a cause.
  - *Example:* The industry **lobbied** hard against the new tax.

### advocate
- **IPA:** /ˈædvəkeɪt/ (v) · /ˈædvəkət/ (n)
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To publicly support or argue for a course of action.
  - *Example:* She **advocates** a slower, safer rollout.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A person who publicly supports a cause or another person.
  - *Example:* He became an **advocate** for flexible working.

### pitch
- **IPA:** /pɪtʃ/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To try to persuade someone to buy or accept something.
  - *Example:* They **pitched** the service to three large clients.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A speech or presentation aimed at persuading.
  - *Example:* The **pitch** lasted ten minutes and won the account.

### rebuttal
- **IPA:** /rɪˈbʌtl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An argument or statement that says the opposite of what someone has claimed.
  - *Example:* She had a clear **rebuttal** for every objection.

### objection
- **IPA:** /əbˈdʒekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reason or argument against something.
  - *Example:* The main **objection** was the up-front cost.

### pushback
- **IPA:** /ˈpʊʃbæk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Opposition or resistance to a plan or idea.
  - *Example:* There was strong **pushback** from staff on the new hours.

### win-win
- **IPA:** /ˌwɪn ˈwɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Giving an advantage to everyone involved.
  - *Example:* Shared transport was a **win-win**: lower cost and less traffic.

### zero-sum
- **IPA:** /ˌzɪərəʊ ˈsʌm/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Describing a situation in which one side's gain is the other's loss.
  - *Example:* They treated the talks as **zero-sum** and neither side moved.

### hard-line
- **IPA:** /ˈhɑːdlaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Firm and unwilling to change position.
  - *Example:* The union took a **hard-line** stance on overtime.

### flexible
- **IPA:** /ˈfleksəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Willing and able to change to suit new conditions.
  - *Example:* We can be **flexible** on the start date if the price is right.

### amicable
- **IPA:** /ˈæmɪkəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Friendly and without serious disagreement.
  - *Example:* The contract ended on **amicable** terms.

### tentative
- **IPA:** /ˈtentətɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Not certain or fixed; provisional.
  - *Example:* We have a **tentative** agreement, subject to legal review.

### provisional
- **IPA:** /prəˈvɪʒənl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Arranged for the present but likely to be changed later.
  - *Example:* The dates are **provisional** until the venue confirms.

### enforceable
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈfɔːsəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Able to be made to happen by law.
  - *Example:* A verbal promise is rarely **enforceable** in a dispute.

### non-binding
- **IPA:** /ˌnɒn ˈbaɪndɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Not creating a legal obligation.
  - *Example:* The early agreement was **non-binding** and either side could withdraw.

### memorandum of understanding
- **IPA:** /ˌmeməˈrændəm əv ˌʌndəˈstændɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document recording the intentions of two parties, usually not legally binding.
  - *Example:* The two universities signed a **memorandum of understanding** to share data.

### letter of intent
- **IPA:** /ˌletər əv ɪnˈtent/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document stating one party's plan to do business with another before a full contract.
  - *Example:* A **letter of intent** was signed while lawyers drafted the deal.

### counterparty
- **IPA:** /ˈkaʊntəpɑːti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The other party in a contract or financial transaction.
  - *Example:* Each **counterparty** must confirm the trade by the end of the day.

### mutual benefit
- **IPA:** /ˌmjuːtʃuəl ˈbenɪfɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An advantage gained by all sides in an arrangement.
  - *Example:* The partnership works because of clear **mutual benefit**.

### in principle
- **IPA:** /ɪn ˈprɪnsəpl/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** In general terms, before the details are settled.
  - *Example:* We agree **in principle**, but the price still needs work.

### subject to contract
- **IPA:** /ˌsʌbdʒɪkt tə ˈkɒntrækt/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** Depending on a formal written contract being signed later.
  - *Example:* The offer was accepted **subject to contract**.

### best and final
- **IPA:** /ˌbest ən ˈfaɪnl/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** Describing an offer that will not be improved further.
  - *Example:* They were asked to submit a **best and final** offer by noon.

### opening position
- **IPA:** /ˌəʊpənɪŋ pəˈzɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The demands a side states at the start of a negotiation.
  - *Example:* Our **opening position** asks for more than we expect to get.

### fallback
- **IPA:** /ˈfɔːlbæk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An alternative plan to use if the main one fails.
  - *Example:* If they refuse, our **fallback** is a shorter trial contract.

### reservation price
- **IPA:** /ˌrezəˈveɪʃn praɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The least favourable price a party will accept before walking away.
  - *Example:* Never reveal your **reservation price** early in the talks.

### negotiator
- **IPA:** /nɪˈɡəʊʃieɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who takes part in discussions aimed at reaching an agreement.
  - *Example:* A skilled **negotiator** listens more than she speaks.

### bargaining chip
- **IPA:** /ˈbɑːɡɪnɪŋ tʃɪp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Something one side can offer or withhold to gain an advantage.
  - *Example:* Early payment was our main **bargaining chip**.

### settlement
- **IPA:** /ˈsetlmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official agreement that ends a dispute.
  - *Example:* The two firms reached a **settlement** out of court.

### split the difference
- **IPA:** /ˌsplɪt ðə ˈdɪfrəns/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** To agree on an amount halfway between two offers.
  - *Example:* They were £400 apart, so they **split the difference**.

### meet halfway
- **IPA:** /ˌmiːt hɑːfˈweɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** To make concessions so that both sides give up something equal.
  - *Example:* We can **meet halfway** on the delivery schedule.

### non-negotiable
- **IPA:** /ˌnɒn nɪˈɡəʊʃiəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Not open to discussion or change.
  - *Example:* The safety clauses are **non-negotiable**.

### red line
- **IPA:** /ˌred ˈlaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A limit that a party will not cross under any circumstances.
  - *Example:* Losing control of the brand name is a **red line** for the founder.

### wiggle room
- **IPA:** /ˈwɪɡl ruːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small amount of flexibility in a position or plan.
  - *Example:* The budget leaves little **wiggle room** on price.

### quid pro quo
- **IPA:** /ˌkwɪd prəʊ ˈkwəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A favour or advantage given in return for something.
  - *Example:* The lower rent was a **quid pro quo** for a five-year lease.

### olive branch
- **IPA:** /ˈɒlɪv brɑːntʃ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An offer made to restore a friendly relationship after a dispute.
  - *Example:* Waiving the late fee was an **olive branch** to the client.

### climbdown
- **IPA:** /ˈklaɪmdaʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An admission that a firm position was wrong, and a retreat from it.
  - *Example:* Dropping the demand was an embarrassing **climbdown**.

### counterproposal
- **IPA:** /ˈkaʊntəprəˌpəʊzl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A different plan put forward in reply to one that was rejected.
  - *Example:* Their **counterproposal** kept the price but shortened the term.

### goodwill gesture
- **IPA:** /ˌɡʊdwɪl ˈdʒestʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An action done to show friendly intentions and build trust.
  - *Example:* A free month of service was offered as a **goodwill gesture**.

### hard bargain
- **IPA:** /ˌhɑːd ˈbɑːɡɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A deal in which one side pushes firmly for favourable terms.
  - *Example:* She drives a **hard bargain**, but she always delivers.

### concede a point
- **IPA:** /kənˌsiːd ə ˈpɔɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** To admit that the other side is right about one part of an argument.
  - *Example:* He **conceded a point** on timing to win on price.

### negotiating table
- **IPA:** /nɪˈɡəʊʃieɪtɪŋ ˌteɪbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The formal setting or process in which two sides discuss an agreement.
  - *Example:* The strike brought both sides back to the **negotiating table**.
