# 15 — Taxation & Regulation

### taxation
- **IPA:** /tækˈseɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The system by which a government collects money from people and businesses.
  - *Example:* Company **taxation** rules changed for the new financial year.

### levy
- **IPA:** /ˈlevi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An amount of money charged by a government or authority.
  - *Example:* A training **levy** on large employers funds apprenticeships.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To officially impose and collect a tax or charge.
  - *Example:* The council **levied** a small charge on single-use bags.

### excise
- **IPA:** /ˈeksaɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tax on certain goods produced or sold within a country, such as fuel or alcohol.
  - *Example:* **Excise** on tobacco rose again in the budget.

### income tax
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnkʌm tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tax charged on the money a person earns.
  - *Example:* **Income tax** is deducted from wages before they are paid.

### corporate tax
- **IPA:** /ˈkɔːpərət tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tax on the profits of a company.
  - *Example:* A lower **corporate tax** rate attracted new head offices.

### value-added tax
- **IPA:** /ˌvæljuː ædɪd ˈtæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tax added at each stage of production and paid finally by the consumer.
  - *Example:* The price shown includes **value-added tax**.

### sales tax
- **IPA:** /ˈseɪlz tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tax added to the price of goods and services at the point of sale.
  - *Example:* **Sales tax** varies from state to state.

### withholding tax
- **IPA:** /wɪðˈhəʊldɪŋ tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Tax deducted at source from a payment, such as interest or dividends, before it reaches the recipient.
  - *Example:* A 15% **withholding tax** applied to the dividend paid abroad.

### capital gains tax
- **IPA:** /ˌkæpɪtl ˈɡeɪnz tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tax on the profit made when an asset is sold for more than its cost.
  - *Example:* Selling the second property triggered **capital gains tax**.

### property tax
- **IPA:** /ˈprɒpəti tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tax based on the value of land and buildings owned.
  - *Example:* **Property tax** on the warehouse rose after the revaluation.

### payroll tax
- **IPA:** /ˈpeɪrəʊl tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tax an employer pays based on the wages it pays out.
  - *Example:* **Payroll tax** adds around 12% to the cost of each employee.

### tax bracket
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks ˌbrækɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A range of income taxed at a particular rate.
  - *Example:* The pay rise pushed her into a higher **tax bracket**.

### taxable income
- **IPA:** /ˌtæksəbl ˈɪnkʌm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The part of a person's or company's income on which tax must be paid.
  - *Example:* Pension contributions reduce your **taxable income**.

### tax return
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks rɪˌtɜːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official form on which income and tax due are reported.
  - *Example:* The **tax return** must be filed by 31 January.

### tax filing
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks ˌfaɪlɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of submitting tax documents to the authorities.
  - *Example:* Online **tax filing** cut the firm's paperwork sharply.

### tax year
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks jɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The 12-month period used to calculate a person's or company's tax.
  - *Example:* Bonuses paid after April fall into the new **tax year**.

### exemption
- **IPA:** /ɪɡˈzempʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Freedom from a duty or payment that others must make.
  - *Example:* Charities may qualify for an **exemption** from the tax.

### rebate
- **IPA:** /ˈriːbeɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A partial refund of money already paid, especially tax.
  - *Example:* She received a **rebate** after overpaying through the year.

### relief
- **IPA:** /rɪˈliːf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reduction in the amount of tax owed for a particular reason.
  - *Example:* Investment in equipment qualifies for tax **relief**.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A feeling of reassurance after worry or difficulty.
  - *Example:* It was a **relief** when the audit was over.

### arrears
- **IPA:** /əˈrɪəz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money that is owed and should already have been paid.
  - *Example:* The company faces a bill for two years of tax **arrears**.

### tax assessment
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks əˌsesmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official calculation of how much tax a person or company must pay.
  - *Example:* They disputed the **tax assessment** and asked for a review.

### tax evasion
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks ɪˌveɪʒn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The illegal act of not paying tax that is owed.
  - *Example:* Hiding cash sales is **tax evasion** and a criminal offence.

### tax avoidance
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks əˌvɔɪdəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The use of legal methods to reduce the amount of tax paid.
  - *Example:* The scheme was legal **tax avoidance**, though widely criticised.

### loophole
- **IPA:** /ˈluːphəʊl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A gap in the wording of a law that lets people avoid its intent.
  - *Example:* The budget closed a **loophole** used by property investors.

### shelter
- **IPA:** /ˈʃeltə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An arrangement that legally protects income or assets from tax.
  - *Example:* Pensions act as a **shelter** for long-term savings.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To protect income or money from taxation.
  - *Example:* The structure **shelters** overseas profits from local tax.

### haven
- **IPA:** /ˈheɪvn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A country or place with very low taxes, used to reduce tax bills.
  - *Example:* Profits were routed through a low-tax **haven**.

### double taxation
- **IPA:** /ˌdʌbl tækˈseɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The taxing of the same income by two different countries.
  - *Example:* A treaty prevents **double taxation** of cross-border workers.

### treaty
- **IPA:** /ˈtriːti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal agreement between countries, such as one setting tax rules.
  - *Example:* Under the **treaty**, tax paid abroad can be offset at home.

### transfer pricing
- **IPA:** /ˈtrænsfɜː ˌpraɪsɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The prices charged between parts of the same company in different countries, watched closely by tax authorities.
  - *Example:* Regulators questioned the group's **transfer pricing** on software licences.

### declaration
- **IPA:** /ˌdekləˈreɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official statement of income, goods, or facts to an authority.
  - *Example:* Travellers must make a customs **declaration** for goods over the limit.

### self-assessment
- **IPA:** /ˌself əˈsesmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A system in which taxpayers calculate and report their own tax.
  - *Example:* Freelancers pay tax through **self-assessment**.

### surcharge
- **IPA:** /ˈsɜːtʃɑːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An extra charge added to the normal amount, often as a penalty.
  - *Example:* A **surcharge** is added for returns filed after the deadline.

### interest charge
- **IPA:** /ˈɪntrəst tʃɑːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An amount added to a debt for the time it remains unpaid.
  - *Example:* Late tax attracts a daily **interest charge**.

### overpayment
- **IPA:** /ˌəʊvəˈpeɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An amount paid that is more than what was due.
  - *Example:* The **overpayment** was refunded within a month.

### underpayment
- **IPA:** /ˌʌndəˈpeɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An amount paid that is less than what was owed.
  - *Example:* An **underpayment** of tax must be made up with interest.

### taxpayer
- **IPA:** /ˈtækspeɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person or organisation that pays tax.
  - *Example:* The rules aim to make life simpler for small **taxpayers**.

### revenue authority
- **IPA:** /ˈrevənjuː ɔːˌθɒrəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The government body responsible for collecting taxes.
  - *Example:* The **revenue authority** opened an inquiry into the accounts.

### inspector
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈspektə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official who examines records or premises to check that rules are followed.
  - *Example:* A tax **inspector** visited to review three years of invoices.

### holiday
- **IPA:** /ˈhɒlədeɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period during which a normal payment or tax is not required (*tax holiday*, *payment holiday*).
  - *Example:* New factories in the zone get a five-year tax **holiday**.

### zone
- **IPA:** /zəʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An area given special rules, such as reduced tax or fewer regulations.
  - *Example:* Firms in the enterprise **zone** pay lower business rates.

### flat rate
- **IPA:** /ˌflæt ˈreɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A single fixed rate that applies regardless of amount or income.
  - *Example:* Some countries tax all income at a **flat rate**.

### progressive
- **IPA:** /prəˈɡresɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of a tax) Taking a larger percentage from higher incomes.
  - *Example:* A **progressive** tax system asks more of top earners.

### regressive
- **IPA:** /rɪˈɡresɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of a tax) Taking a larger percentage from lower incomes.
  - *Example:* Sales taxes are often called **regressive**.

### gross
- **IPA:** /ɡrəʊs/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Total, before any deductions such as tax.
  - *Example:* Her **gross** salary is £48,000.

### net
- **IPA:** /net/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Remaining after deductions such as tax and costs.
  - *Example:* **Net** pay is what actually reaches your account.

### deductible
- **IPA:** /dɪˈdʌktəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Able to be subtracted from income before tax is calculated.
  - *Example:* Business travel is **deductible**, but commuting is not.

### non-deductible
- **IPA:** /ˌnɒn dɪˈdʌktəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Not allowed to be subtracted from income for tax purposes.
  - *Example:* Client entertainment is treated as **non-deductible**.

### remit tax
- **IPA:** /rɪˌmɪt ˈtæks/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** To send collected or owed tax to the authorities.
  - *Example:* Employers must **remit tax** deducted from wages each month.

### tax withholding
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks wɪðˌhəʊldɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The holding back of tax from a payment so it goes straight to the authorities.
  - *Example:* **Tax withholding** on salaries means most employees never file a return.

### file
- **IPA:** /faɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To submit a document officially to an authority.
  - *Example:* The company **files** its return electronically.

### offset
- **IPA:** /ˈɒfset/ (n) · /ˌɒfˈset/ (v)
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To use one amount to reduce or cancel another.
  - *Example:* Losses in one year can **offset** profits in the next.

### carry forward
- **IPA:** /ˌkæri ˈfɔːwəd/
- **Meaning 1 (phr v):** To move an unused amount, such as a loss or allowance, into a later period.
  - *Example:* The firm can **carry forward** the tax loss for up to five years.

### taxable event
- **IPA:** /ˌtæksəbl ɪˈvent/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An occurrence, such as a sale or gift, that creates a liability to tax.
  - *Example:* Transferring the shares to a trust was a **taxable event**.

### fiscal drag
- **IPA:** /ˌfɪskl ˈdræɡ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The effect of inflation pushing incomes into higher tax bands when thresholds do not rise.
  - *Example:* **Fiscal drag** raised the average tax bill without any change in rates.

### regulatory burden
- **IPA:** /ˈreɡjələtri ˌbɜːdn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The total cost and effort a business spends on meeting rules and reporting.
  - *Example:* Small firms complain that the **regulatory burden** is too heavy.

### tax code
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks kəʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A set of letters and numbers telling an employer how much tax to deduct from pay.
  - *Example:* An out-of-date **tax code** meant too much was taken from her wages.

### tax base
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks beɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The total amount of income, property, or activity on which a government can levy tax.
  - *Example:* A shrinking population narrows the **tax base**.

### tax break
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks breɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reduction in tax given to encourage a particular activity.
  - *Example:* A **tax break** for research spending helped fund the lab.

### tax burden
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks ˌbɜːdn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The total amount of tax paid by a person, business, or country, relative to income.
  - *Example:* The reforms shifted the **tax burden** from earnings to property.

### tax cut
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks kʌt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reduction in the rate or amount of a tax.
  - *Example:* The **tax cut** left households with more to spend.

### tax hike
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks haɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sharp increase in a tax.
  - *Example:* Businesses warned that the **tax hike** would cost jobs.

### tax audit
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks ˌɔːdɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official examination of a taxpayer's records to check that the right tax was paid.
  - *Example:* The **tax audit** covered the last four years of returns.

### tax rate
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The percentage at which income or a transaction is taxed.
  - *Example:* The standard **tax rate** on services is 20%.

### marginal rate
- **IPA:** /ˌmɑːdʒɪnl ˈreɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The rate of tax paid on the next unit of income earned.
  - *Example:* Above that threshold, the **marginal rate** jumps to 40%.

### effective rate
- **IPA:** /ɪˌfektɪv ˈreɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The average percentage of total income actually paid in tax.
  - *Example:* Deductions brought the company's **effective rate** down to 14%.

### taxable
- **IPA:** /ˈtæksəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Subject to tax.
  - *Example:* Interest on the savings account is **taxable**.

### tax-exempt
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks ɪɡˌzempt/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Not required to be taxed.
  - *Example:* Certain government bonds are **tax-exempt**.

### input tax
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnpʊt tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Value-added tax a business pays on its purchases, which it can usually reclaim.
  - *Example:* The firm reclaimed the **input tax** on the new machinery.

### output tax
- **IPA:** /ˈaʊtpʊt tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Value-added tax a business charges on its sales and must pass to the authorities.
  - *Example:* **Output tax** collected from customers is paid over each quarter.

### tax invoice
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks ˌɪnvɔɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An invoice that shows the tax charged and meets legal requirements for reclaiming it.
  - *Example:* Keep the **tax invoice** to support the reclaim.

### tax residency
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks ˌrezɪdənsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The country whose tax rules apply to a person, based on where they live or work.
  - *Example:* Spending more than 183 days there changed his **tax residency**.

### tax liability
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks ˌlaɪəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The total amount of tax that a person or company owes.
  - *Example:* Pension contributions cut her **tax liability** for the year.

### compliance cost
- **IPA:** /kəmˈplaɪəns kɒst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The money and time a business spends to meet legal and tax requirements.
  - *Example:* Digital filing lowered the **compliance cost** for small firms.

### tax haven
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks ˌheɪvn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A country or territory where taxes are very low and used to reduce tax bills.
  - *Example:* The company was criticised for holding cash in a **tax haven**.

### tax relief
- **IPA:** /ˈtæks rɪˌliːf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reduction in tax allowed for a specific type of spending or situation.
  - *Example:* Donations to charity qualify for **tax relief**.
