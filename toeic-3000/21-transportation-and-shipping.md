# 21 — Transportation & Shipping

### haulier
- **IPA:** /ˈhɔːliə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company or person whose business is transporting goods by road.
  - *Example:* A regional **haulier** handles all our short-distance deliveries.

### fleet
- **IPA:** /fliːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A group of vehicles, ships, or aircraft operated by one organisation.
  - *Example:* The company is switching its delivery **fleet** to electric vans.

### freight forwarder
- **IPA:** /ˈfreɪt ˌfɔːwədə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A firm that arranges the shipping of goods on behalf of others.
  - *Example:* The **freight forwarder** booked space on the next sailing.

### consignor
- **IPA:** /kənˈsaɪnə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The person or company sending a shipment of goods.
  - *Example:* The **consignor** is responsible for packing the goods correctly.

### consignee
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒnsaɪˈniː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The person or company to whom a shipment is sent.
  - *Example:* Delivery cannot be made without the **consignee**'s signature.

### dispatcher
- **IPA:** /dɪˈspætʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who assigns vehicles and drivers to jobs and tracks their progress.
  - *Example:* The **dispatcher** rerouted three trucks around the accident.

### logistics provider
- **IPA:** /ləˈdʒɪstɪks prəˌvaɪdə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that manages transport, storage, and distribution for clients.
  - *Example:* An outside **logistics provider** runs the whole warehouse operation.

### third-party logistics
- **IPA:** /ˌθɜːd pɑːti ləˈdʒɪstɪks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The outsourcing of warehousing and distribution to a specialist company.
  - *Example:* Moving to **third-party logistics** freed up capital tied in warehouses.

### container
- **IPA:** /kənˈteɪnə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large standard metal box used to move goods by ship, train, or truck.
  - *Example:* The order filled two 40-foot **containers**.

### twenty-foot equivalent
- **IPA:** /ˌtwentɪ fʊt ɪˈkwɪvələnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A unit for counting container traffic, equal to one standard 20-foot container.
  - *Example:* The port handled 12 million **twenty-foot equivalent** units last year.

### bulk carrier
- **IPA:** /ˈbʌlk ˌkæriə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A ship designed to carry loose cargo such as grain, coal, or ore.
  - *Example:* A **bulk carrier** waited off the coast to load wheat.

### tanker
- **IPA:** /ˈtæŋkə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A ship or truck built to carry liquids such as oil or chemicals.
  - *Example:* The **tanker** discharged its crude oil at the refinery berth.

### barge
- **IPA:** /bɑːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A long flat boat for carrying goods on rivers and canals.
  - *Example:* Gravel is moved cheaply by **barge** along the river.

### port
- **IPA:** /pɔːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A town or area with a harbour where ships load and unload.
  - *Example:* Congestion at the **port** delayed every container by days.

### harbour
- **IPA:** /ˈhɑːbə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sheltered area of water where ships can anchor safely.
  - *Example:* Fishing boats returned to the **harbour** before the storm.

### berth
- **IPA:** /bɜːθ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A place in a port where a ship stops to load or unload.
  - *Example:* The ship waited two days for a free **berth**.

### quay
- **IPA:** /kiː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A solid structure along the water's edge where ships are loaded and unloaded.
  - *Example:* Cranes line the **quay** at the container terminal.

### wharf
- **IPA:** /wɔːf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A platform built along the shore where ships tie up.
  - *Example:* Timber was stacked on the **wharf** ready for loading.

### dock
- **IPA:** /dɒk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An enclosed area of water where ships are loaded, unloaded, or repaired.
  - *Example:* The ship went into **dock** for a hull inspection.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** (of a ship) To come into a dock and stop.
  - *Example:* The ferry **docks** at 6 a.m.

### stevedore
- **IPA:** /ˈstiːvədɔː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A worker who loads and unloads ships at a port.
  - *Example:* A team of **stevedores** worked through the night to clear the hold.

### gantry crane
- **IPA:** /ˈɡæntri kreɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large crane on a raised frame that lifts containers on and off ships.
  - *Example:* Each **gantry crane** can move 30 containers an hour.

### containerization
- **IPA:** /kənˌteɪnəraɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The system of moving goods in standard containers across ship, rail, and road.
  - *Example:* **Containerization** cut the cost of ocean shipping dramatically.

### break-bulk
- **IPA:** /ˈbreɪk bʌlk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Cargo that is loaded piece by piece rather than in containers.
  - *Example:* Heavy machinery is shipped as **break-bulk** on special vessels.

### palletization
- **IPA:** /ˌpælətaɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The stacking of goods on pallets so they can be moved by forklift.
  - *Example:* **Palletization** made loading the trucks three times faster.

### unload
- **IPA:** /ˌʌnˈləʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To remove goods from a vehicle, ship, or container.
  - *Example:* It took an hour to **unload** the delivery.

### transship
- **IPA:** /trænsˈʃɪp/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To transfer cargo from one ship or vehicle to another during a journey.
  - *Example:* Goods for the islands are **transshipped** at the main hub port.

### drayage
- **IPA:** /ˈdreɪɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The short-distance transport of goods, especially containers to and from a port.
  - *Example:* **Drayage** from the terminal to the warehouse added $150 per container.

### last mile
- **IPA:** /ˌlɑːst ˈmaɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The final stage of delivery, from a local hub to the customer's door.
  - *Example:* The **last mile** is the most expensive part of parcel delivery.

### hub-and-spoke
- **IPA:** /ˌhʌb ən ˈspəʊk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Describing a network where traffic flows through a central hub to reach many points.
  - *Example:* A **hub-and-spoke** model lets one sorting centre serve the whole region.

### distribution centre
- **IPA:** /ˌdɪstrɪˈbjuːʃn ˌsentə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large warehouse where goods are received, stored briefly, and sent out to shops or customers.
  - *Example:* The new **distribution centre** ships to 200 stores.

### cross-docking
- **IPA:** /ˈkrɒs ˌdɒkɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Moving goods straight from incoming to outgoing trucks with little or no storage.
  - *Example:* **Cross-docking** keeps fresh produce moving within hours.

### warehouse slot
- **IPA:** /ˈweəhaʊs slɒt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A booked time for a vehicle to arrive at a warehouse to load or unload.
  - *Example:* The truck missed its **warehouse slot** and waited three hours.

### waybill
- **IPA:** /ˈweɪbɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document listing the goods carried and the route of a shipment.
  - *Example:* The air **waybill** travels with the cargo.

### manifest
- **IPA:** /ˈmænɪfest/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A list of all the cargo or passengers carried by a ship, plane, or truck.
  - *Example:* Customs checked the goods against the **manifest**.

### customs declaration
- **IPA:** /ˈkʌstəmz ˌdekləreɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official statement of goods being imported or exported, for tax and control.
  - *Example:* An error on the **customs declaration** held the shipment for a week.

### tariff code
- **IPA:** /ˈtærɪf kəʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A number that classifies a product for customs duty purposes.
  - *Example:* Using the wrong **tariff code** meant the firm overpaid duty for years.

### incoterms
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnkəʊtɜːmz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Standard international trade terms that define who pays for and risks each part of shipping.
  - *Example:* The contract uses **incoterms** to fix where the buyer takes responsibility.

### free on board
- **IPA:** /ˌfriː ɒn ˈbɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A shipping term meaning the seller pays until goods are loaded onto the ship; the buyer pays from then.
  - *Example:* The price was quoted **free on board** the port of Rotterdam.

### cost insurance freight
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒst ɪnˌʃʊərəns ˈfreɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A shipping term where the seller pays cost, insurance, and freight to the destination port.
  - *Example:* Under **cost insurance freight**, the seller arranges and pays for the sea transport.

### ex works
- **IPA:** /ˌeks ˈwɜːks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A shipping term where the buyer collects the goods from the seller's premises and bears all onward costs.
  - *Example:* Buying **ex works** was cheaper but left us to arrange every step.

### demurrage
- **IPA:** /dɪˈmʌrɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A charge for keeping a ship or container beyond the agreed free time.
  - *Example:* Port delays ran up thousands in **demurrage**.

### detention
- **IPA:** /dɪˈtenʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A charge for holding a carrier's equipment, such as a container, too long outside the port.
  - *Example:* Slow unloading at our yard led to **detention** fees.

### laytime
- **IPA:** /ˈleɪtaɪm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The time allowed in a charter for loading or unloading a ship before extra charges apply.
  - *Example:* The **laytime** was 72 hours, and loading finished with an hour to spare.

### chartering
- **IPA:** /ˈtʃɑːtərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The hiring of a ship or aircraft for a voyage or a period.
  - *Example:* **Chartering** a whole vessel made sense for the large one-off shipment.

### freight rate
- **IPA:** /ˈfreɪt reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The price charged for transporting goods.
  - *Example:* **Freight rates** on the Asia route tripled during the shortage.

### fuel surcharge
- **IPA:** /ˈfjuːəl ˌsɜːtʃɑːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An extra charge added to freight costs to cover changes in fuel prices.
  - *Example:* A rising **fuel surcharge** pushed up delivery costs every month.

### telematics
- **IPA:** /ˌteliˈmætɪks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Technology that sends data from vehicles, such as location, speed, and fuel use.
  - *Example:* **Telematics** showed which drivers were braking too hard.

### route optimization
- **IPA:** /ˈruːt ˌɒptɪmaɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The use of software to plan the most efficient set of stops for a vehicle.
  - *Example:* **Route optimization** cut the daily mileage by 12%.

### backhaul
- **IPA:** /ˈbækhɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The return trip of a vehicle, ideally carrying cargo rather than travelling empty.
  - *Example:* Finding **backhaul** loads keeps the trucks earning both ways.

### deadhead
- **IPA:** /ˈdedhed/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A trip made by a vehicle with no cargo or passengers.
  - *Example:* Cutting **deadhead** miles was the biggest saving of the year.

### weigh station
- **IPA:** /ˈweɪ ˌsteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A roadside point where trucks are checked for weight and safety.
  - *Example:* The truck was pulled in at the **weigh station** for a random check.

### axle load
- **IPA:** /ˈæksl ləʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The total weight carried by one axle of a vehicle, limited by law.
  - *Example:* The load was rearranged to keep each **axle load** legal.

### road haulage
- **IPA:** /ˈrəʊd ˌhɔːlɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The business of transporting goods by truck.
  - *Example:* **Road haulage** still carries most freight within the country.

### rail freight
- **IPA:** /ˈreɪl freɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The transport of goods by train.
  - *Example:* Shifting bulk cargo to **rail freight** cut both cost and emissions.

### air cargo
- **IPA:** /ˈeə ˌkɑːɡəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Goods transported by aircraft.
  - *Example:* Only urgent, high-value items go by **air cargo**.

### courier network
- **IPA:** /ˈkʊriə ˌnetwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A connected system of depots and vehicles used to deliver parcels quickly.
  - *Example:* The retailer joined a national **courier network** for next-day delivery.

### express delivery
- **IPA:** /ɪkˌspres dɪˈlɪvəri/

  - *Example:* **Express delivery** guarantees the parcel arrives by noon the next day.

### perishable cargo
- **IPA:** /ˈperɪʃəbl ˌkɑːɡəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Goods that decay quickly and must be moved fast, often refrigerated.
  - *Example:* **Perishable cargo** is loaded last and unloaded first.

### dangerous goods
- **IPA:** /ˌdeɪndʒərəs ˈɡʊdz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Items that are a risk to health or safety in transport, such as chemicals or batteries.
  - *Example:* **Dangerous goods** must be labelled and packed to strict rules.

### cargo insurance
- **IPA:** /ˈkɑːɡəʊ ɪnˌʃʊərəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Insurance covering loss of or damage to goods while they are being transported.
  - *Example:* **Cargo insurance** paid out when the container went overboard.

### transloading
- **IPA:** /trænsˈləʊdɪŋ/

  - *Example:* **Transloading** at the border switches the cargo to local trucks.

### intermodal
- **IPA:** /ˌɪntəˈməʊdl/

### supply chain visibility

### supply chain

### container terminal

### shipment tracking

### freight charge

- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount billed for transporting goods.
  - *Example:* The **freight charge** is added to the invoice separately.

### cargo ship

- **Meaning 1 (n):** A ship built to carry goods rather than passengers.
  - *Example:* The **cargo ship** carried 8,000 containers on the Asia–Europe run.

### container port

### logistics network

### freight train

### delivery route

### transit warehouse
