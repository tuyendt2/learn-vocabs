# 13 — Accounting & Auditing

### journal entry
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒɜːnl ˌentri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A record of a single financial transaction in the accounting books.
  - *Example:* Each sale creates a **journal entry** for revenue and for cash.

### accounts payable
- **IPA:** /əˌkaʊnts ˈpeɪəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money a company owes to its suppliers for goods or services received.
  - *Example:* **Accounts payable** rose as the firm delayed paying invoices.

### accounts receivable
- **IPA:** /əˌkaʊnts rɪˈsiːvəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money owed to a company by its customers for goods or services supplied.
  - *Example:* Chasing **accounts receivable** improved the firm's cash position.

### accrual
- **IPA:** /əˈkruːəl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An amount recorded as earned or owed before cash actually changes hands.
  - *Example:* An **accrual** was made for the electricity used but not yet billed.

### prepayment
- **IPA:** /ˌpriːˈpeɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A payment made in advance for goods or services to be received later.
  - *Example:* The annual insurance is treated as a **prepayment** and spread over 12 months.

### provision
- **IPA:** /prəˈvɪʒn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An amount set aside in the accounts for a likely future cost or loss.
  - *Example:* The company made a **provision** for warranty claims.

### write-down
- **IPA:** /ˈraɪt daʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reduction in the recorded value of an asset.
  - *Example:* A **write-down** of the old machinery cut reported profit.

### impairment
- **IPA:** /ɪmˈpeəmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A permanent fall in the value of an asset below its recorded amount.
  - *Example:* The firm took a $30 million **impairment** on the failed acquisition.

### amortization
- **IPA:** /əˌmɔːtaɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The spreading of the cost of an intangible asset over its useful life.
  - *Example:* **Amortization** of the software licence runs over five years.

### capital expenditure
- **IPA:** /ˌkæpɪtl ɪkˈspendɪtʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money spent on long-term assets such as buildings or equipment.
  - *Example:* **Capital expenditure** on the new line was $12 million.

### operating expense
- **IPA:** /ˈɒpəreɪtɪŋ ɪkˌspens/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A day-to-day cost of running a business, such as rent, wages, or utilities.
  - *Example:* Rising fuel prices pushed up **operating expenses**.

### cost of goods sold
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒst əv ɡʊdz ˈsəʊld/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The direct cost of producing the goods a company has sold.
  - *Example:* A cheaper supplier reduced the **cost of goods sold**.

### gross margin
- **IPA:** /ˌɡrəʊs ˈmɑːdʒɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Sales revenue minus the cost of goods sold, as a percentage of sales.
  - *Example:* The **gross margin** on accessories is far higher than on hardware.

### net margin
- **IPA:** /ˌnet ˈmɑːdʒɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Profit after all costs, as a percentage of sales.
  - *Example:* A **net margin** of 4% leaves little room for error.

### EBITDA
- **IPA:** /ɪˈbɪtdɑː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization.
  - *Example:* Investors focused on **EBITDA** rather than the reported loss.

### retained earnings
- **IPA:** /rɪˌteɪnd ˈɜːnɪŋz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Profits kept in the business rather than paid out as dividends.
  - *Example:* The expansion was funded from **retained earnings**.

### working capital
- **IPA:** /ˌwɜːkɪŋ ˈkæpɪtl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The money available for day-to-day operations: current assets minus current liabilities.
  - *Example:* Slow-paying customers tied up too much **working capital**.

### current asset
- **IPA:** /ˌkʌrənt ˈæset/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An asset expected to be turned into cash within a year, such as stock or debtors.
  - *Example:* Cash and inventory are the firm's main **current assets**.

### fixed asset
- **IPA:** /ˌfɪkst ˈæset/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A long-term asset used in the business, such as property or machinery.
  - *Example:* The factory building is the company's largest **fixed asset**.

### intangible asset
- **IPA:** /ɪnˌtændʒəbl ˈæset/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An asset with no physical form, such as a patent, trademark, or brand.
  - *Example:* The brand name is a valuable **intangible asset**.

### provision for tax
- **IPA:** /prəˌvɪʒn fə ˈtæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An amount set aside in the accounts to cover expected tax owed.
  - *Example:* The **provision for tax** was based on the year's estimated profit.

### reconciliation
- **IPA:** /ˌrekənsɪliˈeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of checking that two sets of records agree.
  - *Example:* The monthly bank **reconciliation** found a missing deposit.

### general ledger
- **IPA:** /ˌdʒenrəl ˈledʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The main accounting record containing all of a company's accounts.
  - *Example:* Every transaction ends up posted to the **general ledger**.

### chart of accounts
- **IPA:** /ˌtʃɑːt əv əˈkaʊnts/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The full list of accounts a company uses to record transactions.
  - *Example:* A new **chart of accounts** made department reporting easier.

### fiscal period
- **IPA:** /ˈfɪskl ˌpɪəriəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A set span of time, such as a month or quarter, used for financial reporting.
  - *Example:* Results are locked at the end of each **fiscal period**.

### closing entry
- **IPA:** /ˈkləʊzɪŋ ˌentri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A journal entry made at the end of a period to transfer balances and reset accounts.
  - *Example:* **Closing entries** move the year's profit into retained earnings.

### audit trail
- **IPA:** /ˈɔːdɪt treɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A record that shows the history of each transaction from start to finish.
  - *Example:* A clear **audit trail** let the auditors trace every payment.

### internal control
- **IPA:** /ɪnˌtɜːnl kənˈtrəʊl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A procedure inside a company designed to prevent errors and fraud.
  - *Example:* Requiring two signatures is a basic **internal control**.

### external auditor
- **IPA:** /ɪkˌstɜːnl ˈɔːdɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An independent accountant from outside the company who checks its accounts.
  - *Example:* The **external auditor** confirmed the figures were fairly stated.

### statutory audit
- **IPA:** /ˌstætʃətri ˈɔːdɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An audit required by law for certain companies each year.
  - *Example:* The **statutory audit** must be completed within six months of year-end.

### materiality
- **IPA:** /məˌtɪəriˈæləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The idea that an error matters only if it could affect users' decisions.
  - *Example:* A $50 mistake is below the **materiality** threshold.

### going concern
- **IPA:** /ˌɡəʊɪŋ kənˈsɜːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The assumption that a business will keep operating for the foreseeable future.
  - *Example:* The auditors raised doubts about the firm as a **going concern**.

### qualified opinion
- **IPA:** /ˌkwɒlɪfaɪd əˈpɪnjən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An audit conclusion that the accounts are fair except for a specific issue.
  - *Example:* The auditors gave a **qualified opinion** because stock could not be verified.

### unqualified opinion
- **IPA:** /ˌʌnkwɒlɪfaɪd əˈpɪnjən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A clean audit conclusion that the accounts give a true and fair view.
  - *Example:* For the tenth year, the company received an **unqualified opinion**.

### discrepancy
- **IPA:** /dɪsˈkrepənsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A difference between two things that should be the same.
  - *Example:* A **discrepancy** of $2,000 between the ledger and the bank was investigated.

### variance analysis
- **IPA:** /ˈveəriəns əˌnæləsɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The study of differences between planned (budgeted) figures and actual results.
  - *Example:* **Variance analysis** showed materials cost far more than budgeted.

### cost centre
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒst ˌsentə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A part of a business to which costs can be charged but which does not earn revenue.
  - *Example:* The IT department is run as a **cost centre**.

### profit centre
- **IPA:** /ˈprɒfɪt ˌsentə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A part of a business whose costs and revenues are both measured, so its profit is known.
  - *Example:* Each store is treated as a separate **profit centre**.

### allocation base
- **IPA:** /ˌæləˈkeɪʃn beɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The measure used to spread shared costs across departments or products.
  - *Example:* Floor space is the **allocation base** for the rent charge.

### overhead absorption
- **IPA:** /ˌəʊvəhed əbˈzɔːpʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The method of adding a share of indirect costs to each unit produced.
  - *Example:* **Overhead absorption** is based on machine hours.

### standard costing
- **IPA:** /ˌstændəd ˈkɒstɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A system that sets expected costs for materials and labour and compares them with actual costs.
  - *Example:* **Standard costing** flagged a rise in the price of steel at once.

### marginal cost
- **IPA:** /ˌmɑːdʒɪnl ˈkɒst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The cost of producing one more unit of a good or service.
  - *Example:* Once the software is built, the **marginal cost** of another copy is tiny.

### contribution margin
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒntrɪˈbjuːʃn ˌmɑːdʒɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Sales price minus variable cost per unit; what each sale contributes to fixed costs and profit.
  - *Example:* A higher **contribution margin** means fewer sales are needed to break even.

### break-even point
- **IPA:** /ˌbreɪk ˈiːvn pɔɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The level of sales at which total revenue equals total cost.
  - *Example:* The shop passes its **break-even point** around the 20th of each month.

### cash equivalent
- **IPA:** /ˈkæʃ ɪˌkwɪvələnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A very safe, short-term investment that can quickly be turned into a known amount of cash.
  - *Example:* Money-market funds are treated as **cash equivalents** on the balance sheet.

### petty cash
- **IPA:** /ˌpeti ˈkæʃ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small amount of cash kept for minor everyday expenses.
  - *Example:* She took the taxi fare from **petty cash** and kept the receipt.

### expense claim
- **IPA:** /ɪkˈspens kleɪm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A request by an employee to be repaid money spent on the company's behalf.
  - *Example:* Submit the **expense claim** with all receipts attached.

### invoice matching
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnvɔɪs ˌmætʃɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Checking that an invoice agrees with the purchase order and the goods received.
  - *Example:* **Invoice matching** caught a supplier overcharge.

### aged debtors
- **IPA:** /ˌeɪdʒd ˈdetəz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A report listing money owed by customers, grouped by how long it has been outstanding.
  - *Example:* The **aged debtors** report showed several accounts over 90 days.

### bad debt
- **IPA:** /ˌbæd ˈdet/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money owed to a company that is unlikely ever to be paid.
  - *Example:* The failed customer left a **bad debt** of $8,000.

### loss provisioning
- **IPA:** /ˈlɒs prəˌvɪʒənɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of setting aside amounts in the accounts for expected future losses.
  - *Example:* Higher **loss provisioning** for bad debts cut the bank's profit.

### capitalize
- **IPA:** /ˈkæpɪtəlaɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To record a cost as a long-term asset rather than an immediate expense.
  - *Example:* The company **capitalized** the cost of building the new website.

### restate
- **IPA:** /ˌriːˈsteɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To publish corrected versions of financial figures already reported.
  - *Example:* The firm had to **restate** last year's profit after an error was found.

### financial statement
- **IPA:** /faɪˌnænʃl ˈsteɪtmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal record of a company's financial activities, such as the balance sheet or income statement.
  - *Example:* The annual report contains three main **financial statements**.

### balance carried forward
- **IPA:** /ˌbæləns ˌkærid ˈfɔːwəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The closing amount in an account that becomes the opening amount for the next period.
  - *Example:* The **balance carried forward** was £4,120.

### opening balance
- **IPA:** /ˌəʊpənɪŋ ˈbæləns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount in an account at the start of a period.
  - *Example:* The **opening balance** must match last period's closing figure.

### sundry
- **IPA:** /ˈsʌndri/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Various small items not important enough to be listed separately.
  - *Example:* Postage and small fees are recorded under **sundry** expenses.

### remittance advice
- **IPA:** /rɪˈmɪtəns ədˌvaɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A note sent with or before a payment saying which invoices it covers.
  - *Example:* The **remittance advice** listed the six invoices being paid.

### credit note
- **IPA:** /ˈkredɪt nəʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document reducing the amount a customer owes, issued after an overcharge or return.
  - *Example:* A **credit note** was raised for the damaged items.

### debit note
- **IPA:** /ˈdebɪt nəʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document showing that an additional amount is owed, often for an undercharge.
  - *Example:* The supplier sent a **debit note** for the freight left off the invoice.

### double-entry
- **IPA:** /ˌdʌbl ˈentri/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Describing the bookkeeping system in which every transaction is recorded in two accounts.
  - *Example:* In **double-entry** bookkeeping, debits must always equal credits.

### deferred revenue
- **IPA:** /dɪˌfɜːd ˈrevənjuː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money received for goods or services not yet delivered, shown as a liability.
  - *Example:* Annual subscriptions are booked as **deferred revenue** and released monthly.

### prepaid expense
- **IPA:** /ˌpriːpeɪd ɪkˈspens/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A cost paid in advance and recorded as an asset until it is used.
  - *Example:* Rent paid for next quarter sits as a **prepaid expense**.

### cost accounting
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒst əˌkaʊntɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The recording and analysis of a company's costs to help managers control them.
  - *Example:* **Cost accounting** showed which products were actually losing money.

### management accounts
- **IPA:** /ˈmænɪdʒmənt əˌkaʊnts/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Internal financial reports prepared for managers, usually monthly.
  - *Example:* The **management accounts** are reviewed at the first meeting of each month.

### year-end
- **IPA:** /ˌjɪər ˈend/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The date on which a company's financial year finishes.
  - *Example:* Staff work late around **year-end** to close the books.

### audit opinion
- **IPA:** /ˈɔːdɪt əˌpɪnjən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The auditor's formal conclusion about whether the accounts are true and fair.
  - *Example:* The **audit opinion** appears at the front of the financial statements.

### test of controls
- **IPA:** /ˌtest əv kənˈtrəʊlz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An audit check to see whether a company's internal controls are working.
  - *Example:* A **test of controls** confirmed that every payment had two approvers.

### financial year
- **IPA:** /faɪˌnænʃl ˈjɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The 12-month period a company uses for its accounts and tax.
  - *Example:* Their **financial year** runs from April to March.

### accounting period
- **IPA:** /əˈkaʊntɪŋ ˌpɪəriəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The span of time covered by a set of financial statements.
  - *Example:* Revenue must be recorded in the **accounting period** in which it is earned.

### accrued expense
- **IPA:** /əˌkruːd ɪkˈspens/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A cost that has been incurred but not yet paid or invoiced.
  - *Example:* Unpaid wages at month-end are recorded as an **accrued expense**.

### income statement
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnkʌm ˌsteɪtmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A financial statement showing revenue, costs, and profit over a period.
  - *Example:* The **income statement** shows a small profit despite lower sales.

### profit and loss
- **IPA:** /ˌprɒfɪt ən ˈlɒs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A statement summarising income and expenses to show whether a business made a profit.
  - *Example:* The **profit and loss** account is reviewed line by line each month.

### accounting standard
- **IPA:** /əˈkaʊntɪŋ ˌstændəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official rule governing how particular transactions must be recorded and reported.
  - *Example:* A new **accounting standard** changed how leases appear on the balance sheet.

### audit finding
- **IPA:** /ˈɔːdɪt ˌfaɪndɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An issue or weakness identified during an audit.
  - *Example:* Each **audit finding** came with a recommendation and a deadline.
