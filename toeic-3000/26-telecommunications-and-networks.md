# 26 — Telecommunications & Networks

### telecommunication
- **IPA:** /ˌtelɪkəˌmjuːnɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The sending of information over distance by phone, radio, or cable.
  - *Example:* The country invested heavily in **telecommunication** in rural areas.

### operator
- **IPA:** /ˈɒpəreɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that provides telephone or internet services.
  - *Example:* Three **operators** compete for mobile customers in the region.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A person who works a machine or a switchboard.
  - *Example:* The **operator** connected the call to the right [department](31-government-and-public-services.md#department).

### subscriber
- **IPA:** /səbˈskraɪbə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who pays regularly for a service such as phone, internet, or streaming.
  - *Example:* The [network](../toeic-600/13-technology-and-computers.md#5-network) added two million **subscribers** last year.

### handset
- **IPA:** /ˈhændset/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A mobile phone, or the part of a phone you hold to your ear.
  - *Example:* The deal includes a new **handset** every two years.

### base station
- **IPA:** /ˈbeɪs ˌsteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Fixed radio equipment that connects mobile phones to the network.
  - *Example:* A new **base station** improved coverage in the valley.

### cell tower
- **IPA:** /ˈsel ˌtaʊə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tall structure carrying antennas for a mobile phone network.
  - *Example:* Residents objected to a **cell tower** near the school.

### coverage
- **IPA:** /ˈkʌvərɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The geographic area within which a network provides service.
  - *Example:* **Coverage** is patchy once you leave the main roads.

### signal strength
- **IPA:** /ˈsɪɡnl streŋθ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How strong a network signal is at a particular place.
  - *Example:* **Signal strength** dropped to one bar inside the basement.

### roaming
- **IPA:** /ˈrəʊmɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Using your mobile phone on another network, usually abroad.
  - *Example:* **Roaming** charges made the overseas trip expensive.

### spectrum
- **IPA:** /ˈspektrəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The range of radio frequencies available for wireless communication.
  - *Example:* [Operators](26-telecommunications-and-networks.md#operator) paid billions for a slice of the **spectrum**.

### frequency band
- **IPA:** /ˈfriːkwənsi bænd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A defined range of radio frequencies used for a particular purpose.
  - *Example:* The new phones [support](04-teamwork-and-workplace-relationships.md#support) an extra **frequency band** for faster data.

### broadband
- **IPA:** /ˈbrɔːdbænd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A high-speed internet connection that is always on.
  - *Example:* **Broadband** finally reached the village last year.

### fibre-optic
- **IPA:** /ˌfaɪbər ˈɒptɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Using thin glass strands to carry data as light, allowing very high speeds.
  - *Example:* The office was [upgraded](../toeic-600/13-technology-and-computers.md#4-upgrade) to a **fibre-optic** line.

### copper line
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒpə laɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A traditional phone line made of copper wire, slower than fibre.
  - *Example:* The old **copper line** could not [deliver](06-projects-and-deadlines.md#deliver) the speeds they needed.

### leased line
- **IPA:** /ˌliːst ˈlaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A private, dedicated connection rented from a provider, with guaranteed speed.
  - *Example:* The company pays for a **leased line** so its speed never drops at peak times.

### dial-up
- **IPA:** /ˈdaɪəl ʌp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An old, slow internet connection made over a normal phone line.
  - *Example:* Before [broadband](26-telecommunications-and-networks.md#broadband), they struggled with **dial-up**.

### jitter
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Variation in the delay of data packets, which harms voice and video calls.
  - *Example:* High **jitter** made the [video call](26-telecommunications-and-networks.md#video-call) freeze and stutter.

### packet
- **IPA:** /ˈpækɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small unit of data sent across a network.
  - *Example:* A file is split into thousands of **packets** for sending.

### packet loss
- **IPA:** /ˈpækɪt lɒs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The failure of some data packets to reach their destination.
  - *Example:* **Packet loss** on the link caused gaps in the audio.

### router
- **IPA:** /ˈruːtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that directs data between networks, such as between a home and the internet.
  - *Example:* Restarting the **router** restored the [connection](40-travel-commuting-and-getting-around.md#connection).

### switch
- **IPA:** /swɪtʃ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that connects computers within a local network and directs traffic between them.
  - *Example:* A faulty **switch** cut off half the office from the server.

### modem
- **IPA:** /ˈməʊdem/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that connects a local network to an internet service over phone or cable lines.
  - *Example:* The provider sent a new **modem** for the fibre [connection](40-travel-commuting-and-getting-around.md#connection).

### gateway
- **IPA:** /ˈɡeɪtweɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A point that connects one network to another, often controlling traffic between them.
  - *Example:* All traffic to the internet passes through a single **gateway**.

### hub
- **IPA:** /hʌb/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A central point where connections or traffic come together.
  - *Example:* The city is a major **hub** for undersea data cables.

### topology
- **IPA:** /təˈpɒlədʒi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The arrangement of the parts of a network and how they connect.
  - *Example:* A ring **topology** keeps working if one link breaks.

### mesh network
- **IPA:** /ˈmeʃ ˌnetwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A network in which each node relays data, so there are many possible paths.
  - *Example:* A **mesh network** of small units gave wifi to the whole [warehouse](../toeic-600/11-logistics-shipping-and-warehousing.md#15-warehouse).

### backbone
- **IPA:** /ˈbækbəʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The main high-capacity links that carry most of a network's traffic.
  - *Example:* A cut in the national **backbone** slowed the internet countrywide.

### uplink
- **IPA:** /ˈʌplɪŋk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The connection used to send data from a user or device to the network.
  - *Example:* [Video calls](26-telecommunications-and-networks.md#video-call) need a strong **uplink**, not just a fast [download](../toeic-600/13-technology-and-computers.md#17-download).

### downlink
- **IPA:** /ˈdaʊnlɪŋk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The connection used to receive data from the network to a user or device.
  - *Example:* The satellite **downlink** carries the TV signal to homes.

### upload speed
- **IPA:** /ˈʌpləʊd spiːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How fast data can be sent from a device to the internet.
  - *Example:* A slow **upload speed** made sending large files painful.

### download speed
- **IPA:** /ˈdaʊnləʊd spiːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How fast data can be received from the internet to a device.
  - *Example:* The new line tripled the office **download speed**.

### data plan
- **IPA:** /ˈdeɪtə plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A mobile contract that includes a set amount of internet data each month.
  - *Example:* [Streaming](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#streaming) video used up her **data plan** in a week.

### data cap
- **IPA:** /ˈdeɪtə kæp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A limit on how much data can be used before charges or slowdowns apply.
  - *Example:* Going over the **data cap** [triggered](10-problem-solving-and-decision-making.md#trigger) an extra fee.

### throttling
- **IPA:** /ˈθrɒtlɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The deliberate slowing of an internet connection by a provider.
  - *Example:* Users noticed **throttling** of video streams in the evenings.

### tethering
- **IPA:** /ˈteðərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Sharing a phone's mobile internet connection with a computer or other device.
  - *Example:* He worked from the train by **tethering** his laptop to his phone.

### hotspot
- **IPA:** /ˈhɒtspɒt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A place or device that provides wireless internet access.
  - *Example:* The café offers a free wifi **hotspot**.

### SIM card
- **IPA:** /ˈsɪm kɑːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small card in a mobile phone that identifies the subscriber to the network.
  - *Example:* He bought a local **SIM card** on [arrival](../toeic-600/14-travel-and-transportation.md#4-arrival) to avoid [roaming](26-telecommunications-and-networks.md#roaming) fees.

### prepaid
- **IPA:** /ˌpriːˈpeɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Paid for in advance, with credit used up as you go.
  - *Example:* A **prepaid** phone plan avoids a monthly bill.

### postpaid
- **IPA:** /ˌpəʊstˈpeɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Billed after use, usually with a monthly contract.
  - *Example:* **Postpaid** customers get a cheaper rate per minute.

### call charge
- **IPA:** /ˈkɔːl tʃɑːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount billed for making a telephone call.
  - *Example:* International **call charges** dropped sharply with internet calling.

### line rental
- **IPA:** /ˈlaɪn ˌrentl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fixed monthly fee for having a phone or broadband line, separate from usage.
  - *Example:* The bill is mostly **line rental**, with only a few calls on top.

### landline
- **IPA:** /ˈlændlaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A telephone connection that uses a physical wire, not a mobile signal.
  - *Example:* The office kept one **landline** for the alarm system.

### VoIP
- **IPA:** /ˌviː əʊ aɪ ˈpiː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Voice over Internet Protocol: making phone calls over the internet instead of phone lines.
  - *Example:* Switching to **VoIP** cut the company's phone bill in half.

### teleconference
- **IPA:** /ˈtelikɒnfərəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A meeting held by phone or video link between people in different places.
  - *Example:* The weekly **teleconference** connects all three offices.

### video call
- **IPA:** /ˈvɪdiəʊ kɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A phone call with live moving pictures of the people talking.
  - *Example:* A quick **video call** replaced the trip to the client.

### dropped call
- **IPA:** /ˌdrɒpt ˈkɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A phone call that is cut off before it is finished.
  - *Example:* Poor coverage meant a **dropped call** every few minutes on that road.

### busy signal
- **IPA:** /ˈbɪzi ˌsɪɡnl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A repeated tone telling a caller that the line is already in use.
  - *Example:* She kept getting a **busy signal** all afternoon.

### dial tone
- **IPA:** /ˈdaɪəl təʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The steady sound that tells you a phone line is ready for you to dial.
  - *Example:* There was no **dial tone**, so the line was dead.

### extension
- **IPA:** /ɪkˈstenʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An internal phone number within an organisation's system.
  - *Example:* Dial zero for reception, then ask for **extension** 214.

### switchboard
- **IPA:** /ˈswɪtʃbɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The system that connects incoming calls to the right internal phone.
  - *Example:* The **switchboard** was jammed with calls after the [announcement](08-business-communication.md#announcement).

### PBX
- **IPA:** /ˌpiː biː ˈeks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A private branch exchange: a company's internal telephone network.
  - *Example:* The old **PBX** was replaced with an internet phone system.

### call centre
- **IPA:** /ˈkɔːl ˌsentə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An office where staff handle large numbers of phone calls, for sales or support.
  - *Example:* The **call centre** answers 3,000 customer calls a day.

### interconnection
- **IPA:** /ˌɪntəkəˈnekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The linking of two networks so their users can communicate.
  - *Example:* **Interconnection** fees are paid when a call crosses from one [network](../toeic-600/13-technology-and-computers.md#5-network) to another.

### peering
- **IPA:** /ˈpɪərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An arrangement where two networks exchange traffic directly, usually without charge.
  - *Example:* A new **peering** deal cut the delay for local users.

### service restoration
- **IPA:** /ˈsɜːvɪs ˌrestəˈreɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The bringing back of a service after an interruption.
  - *Example:* **Service restoration** after the cable cut took eleven hours.

### network congestion
- **IPA:** /ˈnetwɜːk kənˌdʒestʃən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A slowdown caused by too much traffic for the network to carry.
  - *Example:* Evening **network congestion** slowed [downloads](../toeic-600/13-technology-and-computers.md#17-download) to a crawl.

### quality of service
- **IPA:** /ˌkwɒləti əv ˈsɜːvɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The overall performance of a connection, measured by speed, delay, and reliability.
  - *Example:* The contract sets a minimum **quality of service** for business lines.

### fifth generation
- **IPA:** /ˌfɪfθ dʒenəˈreɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The fifth generation of mobile network technology, offering higher speed and lower delay.
  - *Example:* **Fifth generation** coverage reached the city centre first.

### satellite link
- **IPA:** /ˈsætəlaɪt lɪŋk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A communication connection made through a satellite in orbit.
  - *Example:* The remote site relies on a **satellite link** for internet.

### undersea cable
- **IPA:** /ˌʌndəˈsiː ˌkeɪbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A cable laid on the sea floor to carry data between continents.
  - *Example:* Most international internet traffic travels through **undersea cables**.

### spectrum licence
- **IPA:** /ˈspektrəm ˌlaɪsns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Official permission to use a particular range of radio frequencies.
  - *Example:* The operator's **spectrum licence** runs for another fifteen years.

### base tariff
- **IPA:** /ˈbeɪs ˌtærɪf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The standard rate for a service before any add-ons or discounts.
  - *Example:* The **base tariff** covers 500 minutes and unlimited texts.

### network carrier
- **IPA:** /ˈnetwɜːk ˌkæriə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that owns and operates a telecommunications network.
  - *Example:* Smaller providers rent [capacity](06-projects-and-deadlines.md#capacity) from a large **network carrier**.

### telecoms
- **IPA:** /ˈtelikɒmz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The telecommunications industry, or its technology, for short.
  - *Example:* She works in **telecoms**, selling [network](../toeic-600/13-technology-and-computers.md#5-network) [equipment](../toeic-600/05-office-procedures-and-equipment.md#26-equipment).

### mobile network
- **IPA:** /ˈməʊbaɪl ˌnetwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The system of towers and equipment that provides service to mobile phones.
  - *Example:* The whole **mobile network** went down for two hours after the [power cut](19-energy-and-utilities.md#power-cut).

### signal
- **IPA:** /ˈsɪɡnl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The radio transmission that carries a phone or data connection.
  - *Example:* There is no **signal** at all in the tunnel.

### wireless
- **IPA:** /ˈwaɪələs/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Working without connecting cables, using radio waves instead.
  - *Example:* The whole building is covered by a **wireless** [network](../toeic-600/13-technology-and-computers.md#5-network).

### connectivity
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒnekˈtɪvəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ability to connect to a network or to other systems.
  - *Example:* Poor **connectivity** in the region holds back local businesses.

### fixed line
- **IPA:** /ˌfɪkst ˈlaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A telephone or internet connection through a cable to a fixed location.
  - *Example:* Many homes have dropped their **fixed line** and use only mobile.

### data usage
- **IPA:** /ˈdeɪtə ˌjuːsɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of internet data consumed over a period.
  - *Example:* The app shows your **data usage** day by day.

### network coverage
- **IPA:** /ˈnetwɜːk ˌkʌvərɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The area over which a network provides a usable signal.
  - *Example:* **Network coverage** maps show a gap along the coast road.

### call plan
- **IPA:** /ˈkɔːl plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A package setting how many minutes, texts, and data a customer gets for a monthly fee.
  - *Example:* He switched to a cheaper **call plan** with fewer minutes.

### network outage
- **IPA:** /ˈnetwɜːk ˌaʊtɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period when a network stops working and users lose service.
  - *Example:* A [software](../toeic-600/13-technology-and-computers.md#1-software) fault caused a nationwide **network outage**.

### bit rate
- **IPA:** /ˈbɪt reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The number of bits of data transferred per second.
  - *Example:* A higher **bit rate** gives clearer sound but uses more data.

### network provider
- **IPA:** /ˈnetwɜːk prəˌvaɪdə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that supplies phone or internet network services to customers.
  - *Example:* [Complaints](../toeic-600/20-customer-service-and-complaints.md#1-complaint) about the **network provider** rose after the price increase.
