# 28 — Environment & Sustainability

### sustainability
- **IPA:** /səˌsteɪnəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The use of resources in a way that can continue without harming the future.
  - *Example:* **Sustainability** is now part of every purchasing decision.

### conservation
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒnsəˈveɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The protection of nature, wildlife, and natural resources.
  - *Example:* Part of the land was set aside for **conservation**.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** Careful use of something to avoid waste.
  - *Example:* Water **conservation** measures came in during the dry summer.

### biodiversity
- **IPA:** /ˌbaɪəʊdaɪˈvɜːsəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The variety of plant and animal life in a place.
  - *Example:* Clearing the forest sharply reduced local **biodiversity**.

### ecosystem
- **IPA:** /ˈiːkəʊsɪstəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A community of living things and their environment, working as a unit.
  - *Example:* The wetland **ecosystem** filters water for the whole region.

### habitat
- **IPA:** /ˈhæbɪtæt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The natural home of an animal or plant.
  - *Example:* Road building destroyed the birds' nesting **habitat**.

### wildlife
- **IPA:** /ˈwaɪldlaɪf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Wild animals and plants living in their natural environment.
  - *Example:* The reserve is home to rare **wildlife**.

### deforestation
- **IPA:** /diːˌfɒrɪˈsteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The clearing of forests, usually for farming or building.
  - *Example:* **Deforestation** in the region has slowed but not stopped.

### reforestation
- **IPA:** /ˌriːfɒrɪˈsteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The replanting of trees on land where forest has been lost.
  - *Example:* A **reforestation** project put back a million trees in five years.

### afforestation
- **IPA:** /əˌfɒrɪˈsteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The planting of trees on land that was not previously forest.
  - *Example:* **Afforestation** of the old mine site created a new woodland.

### desertification
- **IPA:** /dɪˌzɜːtɪfɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process by which fertile land becomes desert.
  - *Example:* Overgrazing has driven **desertification** along the region's edge.

### degradation
- **IPA:** /ˌdeɡrəˈdeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The lowering of the quality or condition of the environment.
  - *Example:* Years of pollution led to serious **degradation** of the river.

### pollution
- **IPA:** /pəˈluːʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Harmful substances released into the air, water, or land.
  - *Example:* Traffic is the main source of air **pollution** in the city.

### contaminant
- **IPA:** /kənˈtæmɪnənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A substance that makes something impure or unsafe.
  - *Example:* Tests found several **contaminants** in the groundwater.

### emissions
- **IPA:** /ɪˈmɪʃnz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Gases or other substances sent out into the air.
  - *Example:* The plant cut its carbon **emissions** by a third.

### greenhouse gas
- **IPA:** /ˈɡriːnhaʊs ɡæs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A gas that traps heat in the atmosphere, such as carbon dioxide or methane.
  - *Example:* Agriculture is a major source of **greenhouse gas**.

### carbon dioxide
- **IPA:** /ˌkɑːbən daɪˈɒksaɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A gas produced by burning fuel and by breathing, a main cause of global warming.
  - *Example:* Trees absorb **carbon dioxide** as they grow.

### methane
- **IPA:** /ˈmiːθeɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A greenhouse gas released by livestock, landfill, and gas leaks.
  - *Example:* Capturing **methane** from the landfill provides fuel for the site.

### carbon footprint
- **IPA:** /ˈkɑːbən ˌfʊtprɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The total amount of greenhouse gas caused by a person, product, or organisation.
  - *Example:* Switching suppliers cut the product's **carbon footprint** in half.

### carbon offset
- **IPA:** /ˈkɑːbən ˌɒfset/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reduction in emissions elsewhere, bought to make up for one's own emissions.
  - *Example:* The airline lets passengers buy a **carbon offset** for their flight.

### carbon neutral
- **IPA:** /ˌkɑːbən ˈnjuːtrəl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Producing no net addition of carbon to the atmosphere.
  - *Example:* The company aims to be **carbon neutral** by 2030.

### net zero
- **IPA:** /ˌnet ˈzɪərəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A state where the greenhouse gas added to the atmosphere equals the amount removed.
  - *Example:* The country set a legal target of **net zero** by mid-century.

### climate change
- **IPA:** /ˈklaɪmət tʃeɪndʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Long-term changes in temperature and weather patterns, largely caused by human activity.
  - *Example:* **Climate change** is making extreme weather more common.

### global warming
- **IPA:** /ˌɡləʊbl ˈwɔːmɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The gradual rise in the earth's average temperature.
  - *Example:* **Global warming** has pushed the growing season later into autumn.

### sea level
- **IPA:** /ˈsiː levl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The average height of the ocean's surface, used as a base for measuring land height.
  - *Example:* Rising **sea level** threatens low-lying farmland.

### glacier
- **IPA:** /ˈɡlæsiə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large mass of slow-moving ice on land.
  - *Example:* The **glacier** has retreated by two kilometres in fifty years.

### wildfire
- **IPA:** /ˈwaɪldfaɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large, fast-spreading fire in a wild area such as forest or grassland.
  - *Example:* A dry summer raised the risk of **wildfire** across the hills.

### renewable energy
- **IPA:** /rɪˌnjuːəbl ˈenədʒi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Energy from sources that are naturally replaced, such as sun, wind, and water.
  - *Example:* **Renewable energy** now supplies most of the grid at midday.

### recycling
- **IPA:** /riːˈsaɪklɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The processing of used materials so they can be made into new products.
  - *Example:* The office set up separate bins for paper **recycling**.

### upcycling
- **IPA:** /ˈʌpsaɪklɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Turning waste into products of higher quality or value than the original.
  - *Example:* The workshop makes bags by **upcycling** old advertising banners.

### compost
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒmpɒst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Decayed plant material used to enrich soil.
  - *Example:* Kitchen scraps go into the **compost** rather than the bin.

### landfill
- **IPA:** /ˈlændfɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A site where waste is buried in the ground.
  - *Example:* The city wants to send nothing to **landfill** within ten years.

### incineration
- **IPA:** /ɪnˌsɪnəˈreɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The burning of waste, sometimes to produce energy.
  - *Example:* **Incineration** reduces the volume of waste but releases emissions.

### waste stream
- **IPA:** /ˈweɪst striːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The flow of a particular type of waste from where it is produced to disposal.
  - *Example:* Packaging is the fastest-growing part of the household **waste stream**.

### single-use
- **IPA:** /ˌsɪŋɡl ˈjuːs/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Designed to be used once and then thrown away.
  - *Example:* The café replaced **single-use** cups with a deposit scheme.

### biodegradable
- **IPA:** /ˌbaɪəʊdɪˈɡreɪdəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Able to be broken down naturally by bacteria and other living things.
  - *Example:* The new wrapping is fully **biodegradable**.

### compostable
- **IPA:** /kəmˈpɒstəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Able to break down into compost under the right conditions.
  - *Example:* The **compostable** cutlery needs an industrial composting plant.

### effluent
- **IPA:** /ˈefluənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Liquid waste flowing out of a factory or sewage works.
  - *Example:* The factory must treat its **effluent** before releasing it.

### runoff
- **IPA:** /ˈrʌnɒf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Rainwater that flows off land, often carrying soil or chemicals into rivers.
  - *Example:* Fertiliser in the **runoff** caused algae to bloom in the lake.

### watershed
- **IPA:** /ˈwɔːtəʃed/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An area of land from which all water drains into the same river or lake.
  - *Example:* Protecting the **watershed** keeps the city's water clean.

### aquifer
- **IPA:** /ˈækwɪfə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A layer of underground rock that holds water and can be tapped by wells.
  - *Example:* The **aquifer** is being drained faster than rain can refill it.

### potable water
- **IPA:** /ˌpəʊtəbl ˈwɔːtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Water that is safe to drink.
  - *Example:* The village had no **potable water** until the new well was drilled.

### desalination
- **IPA:** /diːˌsælɪˈneɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The removal of salt from seawater to make it drinkable.
  - *Example:* A **desalination** plant now supplies a fifth of the coast's water.

### air quality
- **IPA:** /ˈeə ˌkwɒləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How clean or polluted the air is.
  - *Example:* **Air quality** improved sharply after the coal plant closed.

### particulate
- **IPA:** /pəˈtɪkjələt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tiny solid particle in the air, harmful to breathe.
  - *Example:* Diesel engines produce fine **particulate** that reaches deep into the lungs.

### smog
- **IPA:** /smɒɡ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A mixture of smoke, fog, and pollution that hangs over a city.
  - *Example:* Heavy **smog** closed schools for two days.

### ozone layer
- **IPA:** /ˈəʊzəʊn ˌleɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A layer of gas high in the atmosphere that blocks harmful rays from the sun.
  - *Example:* Bans on certain chemicals helped the **ozone layer** recover.

### acid rain
- **IPA:** /ˌæsɪd ˈreɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Rain made acidic by pollution, which damages forests, lakes, and buildings.
  - *Example:* **Acid rain** stripped the leaves from trees across the hills.

### endangered species
- **IPA:** /ɪnˌdeɪndʒəd ˈspiːʃiːz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A type of animal or plant at serious risk of dying out.
  - *Example:* The wetland is a refuge for several **endangered species**.

### extinction
- **IPA:** /ɪkˈstɪŋkʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The dying out of a whole species.
  - *Example:* Hunting drove the bird close to **extinction**.

### protected area
- **IPA:** /prəˌtektɪd ˈeəriə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A region where development and activity are limited by law to protect nature.
  - *Example:* Fishing is banned within the **protected area**.

### nature reserve
- **IPA:** /ˈneɪtʃə rɪˌzɜːv/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An area of land managed to protect its plants, animals, and habitats.
  - *Example:* The old quarry was turned into a **nature reserve**.

### sustainable development
- **IPA:** /səˌsteɪnəbl dɪˈveləpmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Growth that meets present needs without harming the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
  - *Example:* The plan tries to balance jobs with **sustainable development**.

### circular economy
- **IPA:** /ˌsɜːkjələ ɪˈkɒnəmi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An economy in which products and materials are reused and recycled rather than thrown away.
  - *Example:* In a **circular economy**, the maker takes the product back at the end of its life.

### life-cycle assessment
- **IPA:** /ˈlaɪf saɪkl əˌsesmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A study of the total environmental impact of a product from raw material to disposal.
  - *Example:* A **life-cycle assessment** showed the glass bottle was worse than the carton.

### environmental impact assessment
- **IPA:** /ɪnˌvaɪrənmentl ˈɪmpækt əˌsesmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A required study of how a proposed project would affect the environment.
  - *Example:* The road scheme was delayed by its **environmental impact assessment**.

### adaptation
- **IPA:** /ˌædæpˈteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Changes made to cope with new conditions, especially a changing climate.
  - *Example:* Flood defences are a form of climate **adaptation**.

### resilience
- **IPA:** /rɪˈzɪliəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ability of a system or community to recover quickly from shocks.
  - *Example:* Diverse crops give the farm more **resilience** to bad weather.

### green procurement
- **IPA:** /ˌɡriːn prəˈkjʊəmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Buying goods and services chosen partly for their low environmental impact.
  - *Example:* **Green procurement** rules favour suppliers with recycled packaging.

### eco-label
- **IPA:** /ˈiːkəʊ ˌleɪbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A mark on a product showing it meets certain environmental standards.
  - *Example:* Shoppers look for the **eco-label** when choosing cleaning products.

### emissions trading
- **IPA:** /ɪˈmɪʃnz ˌtreɪdɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A system where companies buy and sell permits to release a limited total of greenhouse gas.
  - *Example:* Under **emissions trading**, cutting pollution lets a firm sell spare permits.

### sustainability report
- **IPA:** /səˌsteɪnəˈbɪləti rɪˌpɔːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A published account of a company's environmental and social performance.
  - *Example:* The **sustainability report** listed water use, waste, and emissions.

### resource depletion
- **IPA:** /rɪˈsɔːs dɪˌpliːʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The using up of natural resources faster than they can be replaced.
  - *Example:* **Resource depletion** has forced the industry to use recycled metal.

### overfishing
- **IPA:** /ˌəʊvəˈfɪʃɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Catching so many fish that stocks cannot recover.
  - *Example:* **Overfishing** collapsed the cod population within a decade.

### monoculture
- **IPA:** /ˈmɒnəʊkʌltʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The growing of a single crop over a large area, year after year.
  - *Example:* **Monoculture** leaves fields open to a single pest wiping out the harvest.

### permaculture
- **IPA:** /ˈpɜːməkʌltʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A way of farming and living designed to work with natural cycles and need few outside inputs.
  - *Example:* The smallholding is run on **permaculture** principles.

### sustainable
- **IPA:** /səˈsteɪnəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Able to continue over a long time without using up resources or causing harm.
  - *Example:* The company switched to **sustainable** timber for its packaging.

### eco-friendly
- **IPA:** /ˈiːkəʊ ˌfrendli/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Not harmful to the environment.
  - *Example:* The hotel promotes its **eco-friendly** cleaning and towel policy.

### greenhouse effect
- **IPA:** /ˈɡriːnhaʊs ɪˌfekt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The trapping of the sun's heat by gases in the atmosphere.
  - *Example:* Extra carbon dioxide strengthens the **greenhouse effect**.

### waste management
- **IPA:** /ˈweɪst ˌmænɪdʒmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The collection, treatment, and disposal of rubbish.
  - *Example:* The town hired a new **waste management** contractor.

### recyclable
- **IPA:** /riːˈsaɪkləbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Able to be processed and used again.
  - *Example:* All the packaging is now **recyclable** at the kerbside.

### environmental protection
- **IPA:** /ɪnˌvaɪrənmentl prəˈtekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Action taken to prevent damage to the natural world.
  - *Example:* A dedicated agency oversees **environmental protection**.

### carbon tax
- **IPA:** /ˈkɑːbən tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tax on fuels based on the amount of carbon dioxide they release.
  - *Example:* The **carbon tax** made coal more expensive than gas.

### clean energy
- **IPA:** /ˌkliːn ˈenədʒi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Energy that produces little or no pollution or greenhouse gas.
  - *Example:* The region attracts investment in **clean energy** projects.

### environmental footprint
- **IPA:** /ɪnˌvaɪrənmentl ˈfʊtprɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The total effect that a person or organisation has on the environment.
  - *Example:* Remote working shrank the company's **environmental footprint**.

### conservation area
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒnsəˈveɪʃn ˌeəriə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A place given legal protection because of its natural or historic value.
  - *Example:* New buildings in the **conservation area** must match the old style.
