# 36 — Personal Finance & Household Budget

### budgeting
- **IPA:** /ˈbʌdʒɪtɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The activity of planning how to spend and save your money.
  - *Example:* Careful **budgeting** let them save for a [deposit](../toeic-600/02-finance-and-banking.md#2-deposit) in two years.

### outgoings
- **IPA:** /ˈaʊtɡəʊɪŋz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The regular amounts of money a person or household spends.
  - *Example:* Rent is their biggest monthly **outgoing**.

### take-home pay
- **IPA:** /ˌteɪk həʊm ˈpeɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of wages left after tax and other deductions.
  - *Example:* A pay rise of 5% added little to her **take-home pay** after tax.

### payslip
- **IPA:** /ˈpeɪslɪp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document showing how much a worker has been paid and what was deducted.
  - *Example:* Check the **payslip** to see how much went to the [pension](../toeic-600/18-employee-benefits-and-insurance.md#7-pension).

### direct debit
- **IPA:** /dəˌrekt ˈdebɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An instruction letting a company take set or varying amounts from your account automatically.
  - *Example:* The gym fee is paid by **direct debit** on the first of the month.

### standing order
- **IPA:** /ˌstændɪŋ ˈɔːdə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An instruction to your bank to pay a fixed amount to someone regularly.
  - *Example:* She set up a **standing order** to move £200 to [savings](36-personal-finance-and-household-budget.md#savings) each payday.

### bank transfer
- **IPA:** /ˈbæŋk ˌtrænsfɜː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The electronic movement of money from one bank account to another.
  - *Example:* Pay the [deposit](../toeic-600/02-finance-and-banking.md#2-deposit) by **bank transfer**, not cash.

### overdraft fee
- **IPA:** /ˈəʊvədrɑːft fiː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A charge for spending more than the money in your account.
  - *Example:* A single **overdraft fee** wiped out the month's [savings](36-personal-finance-and-household-budget.md#savings).

### balance enquiry
- **IPA:** /ˈbæləns ɪnˌkwaɪəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A request to find out how much money is in an account.
  - *Example:* A quick **balance enquiry** at the machine showed he was overdrawn.

### savings account
- **IPA:** /ˈseɪvɪŋz əˌkaʊnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A bank account that pays interest and is meant for money you do not spend day to day.
  - *Example:* The **savings account** pays more interest if you never [withdraw](../toeic-600/02-finance-and-banking.md#3-withdraw).

### current account
- **IPA:** /ˈkʌrənt əˌkaʊnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A bank account used for everyday payments and receiving wages.
  - *Example:* Her salary goes into a **current account** and bills come straight out.

### joint account
- **IPA:** /ˌdʒɔɪnt əˈkaʊnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A bank account shared by two or more people.
  - *Example:* They opened a **joint account** for the household bills.

### emergency fund
- **IPA:** /ɪˈmɜːdʒənsi fʌnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money set aside to cover unexpected costs such as repairs or job loss.
  - *Example:* Advisers suggest an **emergency fund** of three months' [spending](35-shopping-and-consumer-life.md#spending).

### rainy-day fund
- **IPA:** /ˌreɪni deɪ ˈfʌnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money saved for a time when it may be needed.
  - *Example:* The car repair came out of the **rainy-day fund**.

### annual percentage rate
- **IPA:** /ˌænjuəl pəˈsentɪdʒ reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The yearly cost of a loan including interest and fees, shown as a percentage.
  - *Example:* The card's **annual percentage rate** jumps after the offer period.

### credit score
- **IPA:** /ˈkredɪt skɔː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A number that shows how likely a person is to repay borrowed money.
  - *Example:* Missing payments damaged his **credit score** for years.

### credit limit
- **IPA:** /ˈkredɪt ˌlɪmɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The most a person is allowed to borrow on a credit card or account.
  - *Example:* She asked the bank to lower her **credit limit** to control [spending](35-shopping-and-consumer-life.md#spending).

### minimum payment
- **IPA:** /ˌmɪnɪməm ˈpeɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The smallest amount you must pay on a debt each month to avoid a penalty.
  - *Example:* Paying only the **minimum payment** keeps the debt growing.

### balance transfer
- **IPA:** /ˈbæləns ˌtrænsfɜː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Moving debt from one credit card to another, usually to get a lower interest rate.
  - *Example:* A **balance transfer** gave him a year with no interest to pay it off.

### debt consolidation
- **IPA:** /ˈdet kənˌsɒlɪˈdeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Combining several debts into one loan, often with a single lower payment.
  - *Example:* **Debt consolidation** made the monthly total easier to manage.

### repayment plan
- **IPA:** /rɪˈpeɪmənt plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An agreed schedule for paying back money that is owed.
  - *Example:* The lender agreed a two-year **repayment plan**.

### default notice
- **IPA:** /dɪˈfɔːlt ˌnəʊtɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal warning that payments have been missed and action may follow.
  - *Example:* A **default notice** arrived after three missed instalments.

### bailiff
- **IPA:** /ˈbeɪlɪf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An officer with legal power to collect debts or remove goods.
  - *Example:* The threat of a **bailiff** visit finally prompted a phone call.

### insolvency
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈsɒlvənsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The state of being unable to pay your debts when they are due.
  - *Example:* Rising costs pushed the small business toward **insolvency**.

### frugal
- **IPA:** /ˈfruːɡl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Careful with money; spending only on what is needed.
  - *Example:* A **frugal** lifestyle let them retire early.

### thrifty
- **IPA:** /ˈθrɪfti/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Good at using money and resources without waste.
  - *Example:* Her **thrifty** habits meant nothing was ever thrown away.

### cut back
- **IPA:** /ˌkʌt ˈbæk/
- **Meaning 1 (phr v):** To reduce the amount you spend or use.
  - *Example:* They **cut back** on eating out to pay off the loan.

### tighten belt
- **IPA:** /ˌtaɪtn ˈbelt/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** To spend less money because there is less available.
  - *Example:* Everyone had to **tighten** their **belt** after the price rises.

### live within means
- **IPA:** /ˌlɪv wɪˈðɪn ˈmiːnz/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** To spend no more than the money you have.
  - *Example:* The course teaches young adults to **live within their means**.

### make ends meet
- **IPA:** /ˌmeɪk endz ˈmiːt/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** To have just enough money for your needs.
  - *Example:* On one salary, they struggle to **make ends meet**.

### council tax
- **IPA:** /ˈkaʊnsl tæks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A local tax on homes, used to pay for services such as rubbish collection.
  - *Example:* **Council tax** went up 4% this year.

### insurance premium
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈʃʊərəns ˌpriːmiəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The regular amount paid for an insurance policy.
  - *Example:* Fitting an alarm lowered the home **insurance premium**.

### standing charge
- **IPA:** /ˈstændɪŋ tʃɑːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fixed daily fee on a utility bill, charged before any usage.
  - *Example:* Even with the heating off, the **standing charge** still applied.

### tariff comparison
- **IPA:** /ˈtærɪf kəmˌpærɪsn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A check of the rates offered by different suppliers for the same service.
  - *Example:* A quick **tariff comparison** saved them £150 a year on energy.

### cashflow
- **IPA:** /ˈkæʃfləʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The movement of money into and out of a household or business over time.
  - *Example:* Paying the [insurance](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#9-insurance) yearly rather than monthly hurt their **cashflow**.

### windfall
- **IPA:** /ˈwɪndfɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sum of money received unexpectedly.
  - *Example:* They put the tax **windfall** straight into [savings](36-personal-finance-and-household-budget.md#savings).

### inheritance
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈherɪtəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money or property received from someone who has died.
  - *Example:* A small **inheritance** cleared the last of the [mortgage](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#5-mortgage).

### dependants
- **IPA:** /dɪˈpendənts/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** People, especially children, who rely on someone else for money and support.
  - *Example:* [Life cover](36-personal-finance-and-household-budget.md#life-cover) should reflect how many **dependants** you have.

### pension contribution
- **IPA:** /ˈpenʃn ˌkɒntrɪˈbjuːʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money paid into a pension each month by an employee or employer.
  - *Example:* Raising her **pension contribution** cut her tax bill.

### retirement fund
- **IPA:** /rɪˈtaɪəmənt fʌnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Savings built up to provide income after a person stops working.
  - *Example:* The market fall knocked 10% off his **retirement fund**.

### financial adviser
- **IPA:** /faɪˌnænʃl ədˈvaɪzə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A professional who helps people plan their savings, investments, and pensions.
  - *Example:* A **financial adviser** reviewed their [pensions](../toeic-600/18-employee-benefits-and-insurance.md#7-pension) and [insurance](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#9-insurance).

### will
- **IPA:** /wɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A legal document saying what should happen to your money and property after you die.
  - *Example:* They updated the **will** after the second child was born.

### estate
- **IPA:** /ɪˈsteɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** All the money and property a person owns, especially at death.
  - *Example:* The **estate** was divided equally between the three children.

### probate
- **IPA:** /ˈprəʊbeɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The legal process of proving a will and settling a dead person's affairs.
  - *Example:* **Probate** took eight months before the house could be sold.

### life cover
- **IPA:** /ˈlaɪf ˌkʌvə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Insurance that pays a sum to your family if you die.
  - *Example:* The [mortgage](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#5-mortgage) came with a [requirement](06-projects-and-deadlines.md#requirement) to hold **life cover**.

### income protection
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnkʌm prəˌtekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Insurance that pays a regular income if you cannot work because of illness or injury.
  - *Example:* **Income protection** replaced most of his salary during the [recovery](11-economics-and-markets.md#recovery).

### sinking fund
- **IPA:** /ˈsɪŋkɪŋ fʌnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money saved gradually toward a known future cost.
  - *Example:* A **sinking fund** for the boiler meant no shock when it failed.

### itemize
- **IPA:** /ˈaɪtəmaɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To list things separately, one by one.
  - *Example:* She **itemized** every [expense](../toeic-600/02-finance-and-banking.md#20-expense) to see where the money went.

### discretionary spending
- **IPA:** /dɪˌskreʃənri ˈspendɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money spent on things you want but do not need.
  - *Example:* When money is tight, **discretionary spending** is the first to go.

### fixed cost
- **IPA:** /ˌfɪkst ˈkɒst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A regular cost that stays the same each month, such as rent.
  - *Example:* Rent and [insurance](../toeic-600/17-health-and-medical.md#9-insurance) are **fixed costs** in the budget.

### variable cost
- **IPA:** /ˌveəriəbl ˈkɒst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A cost that changes from month to month, such as food or fuel.
  - *Example:* Groceries are the largest **variable cost** for the family.

### envelope method
- **IPA:** /ˈenvələʊp ˌmeθəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A budgeting system where cash for each spending category is kept in a separate envelope.
  - *Example:* The **envelope method** stopped him overspending on [takeaways](07-data-reports-and-analysis.md#takeaway).

### round-up saving
- **IPA:** /ˈraʊnd ʌp ˌseɪvɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A scheme that rounds each purchase up to the nearest unit and saves the difference.
  - *Example:* **Round-up saving** quietly built a £300 buffer over a year.

### financial cushion
- **IPA:** /faɪˌnænʃl ˈkʊʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reserve of money that protects you against sudden costs or loss of income.
  - *Example:* Three months' pay in the bank gave them a **financial cushion**.

### budget
- **IPA:** /ˈbʌdʒɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A plan of expected income and spending over a period.
  - *Example:* The monthly **budget** left £120 for [savings](36-personal-finance-and-household-budget.md#savings).
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To plan how much to spend on something.
  - *Example:* They **budgeted** £3,000 for the kitchen.

### savings
- **IPA:** /ˈseɪvɪŋz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money kept rather than spent, usually in an account.
  - *Example:* The holiday came out of their **savings**.

### personal loan
- **IPA:** /ˌpɜːsənl ˈləʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money borrowed from a bank for personal use, repaid with interest over a set term.
  - *Example:* They took a **personal loan** to spread the cost of the car.

### credit card
- **IPA:** /ˈkredɪt kɑːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A card that lets you buy now and pay the bank back later, with interest if not paid in full.
  - *Example:* He clears the **credit card** in full every month to avoid interest.

### overdraft
- **IPA:** /ˈəʊvədrɑːft/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An arrangement that lets you spend more than the money in your account, up to a limit.
  - *Example:* The account has a £500 interest-free **overdraft**.

### financial goal
- **IPA:** /faɪˌnænʃl ˈɡəʊl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A specific money target, such as saving a deposit or clearing a debt.
  - *Example:* Their main **financial goal** is to be mortgage-free by 55.

### money management
- **IPA:** /ˈmʌni ˌmænɪdʒmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The handling of income, spending, saving, and borrowing.
  - *Example:* A simple app improved his **money management** a lot.

### household expenses
- **IPA:** /ˈhaʊshəʊld ɪkˌspensɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The regular costs of running a home, such as food, bills, and rent.
  - *Example:* **Household expenses** rose faster than wages for three years.

### monthly budget
- **IPA:** /ˌmʌnθli ˈbʌdʒɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A plan of how much money will come in and go out each month.
  - *Example:* The **monthly budget** is reviewed together every payday.

### bank statement
- **IPA:** /ˈbæŋk ˌsteɪtmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A record from the bank listing all money in and out of an account over a period.
  - *Example:* Going through the **bank statement** revealed a [subscription](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#subscription) they had forgotten.

### savings goal
- **IPA:** /ˈseɪvɪŋz ɡəʊl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A specific amount someone is trying to save, often for a purpose.
  - *Example:* Her **savings goal** was £5,000 for a car.

### debt repayment
- **IPA:** /ˈdet rɪˌpeɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The paying back of borrowed money.
  - *Example:* Half of every pay rise goes toward **debt repayment**.

### spending limit
- **IPA:** /ˈspendɪŋ ˌlɪmɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A maximum amount set for spending in a period or category.
  - *Example:* They agreed a **spending limit** of £30 for gifts.

### bill payment
- **IPA:** /ˈbɪl ˌpeɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of paying a demand for money owed, such as for electricity or rent.
  - *Example:* Automatic **bill payment** means she never misses a [due date](06-projects-and-deadlines.md#due-date).

### financial planning
- **IPA:** /faɪˌnænʃl ˈplænɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of setting money goals and deciding how to reach them.
  - *Example:* Good **financial planning** in their thirties made retirement comfortable.

### pay cheque
- **IPA:** /ˈpeɪ tʃek/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The regular payment an employee receives for their work.
  - *Example:* Most of the **pay cheque** is gone by the middle of the month.

### direct deposit
- **IPA:** /dəˌrekt dɪˈpɒzɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The payment of wages or benefits straight into a bank account.
  - *Example:* Wages arrive by **direct deposit** on the last working day.

### cost-cutting
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒst ˌkʌtɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Reducing the amount of money spent.
  - *Example:* A round of **cost-cutting** started with the [streaming](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#streaming) [subscriptions](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#subscription).

### household finances
- **IPA:** /ˈhaʊshəʊld ˌfaɪnænsɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The money matters of a home: income, spending, savings, and debt.
  - *Example:* One [partner](04-teamwork-and-workplace-relationships.md#partner) manages the **household finances** for the whole family.

### budget plan
- **IPA:** /ˈbʌdʒɪt plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A written scheme showing how money will be shared among needs and goals.
  - *Example:* The **budget plan** put 50% to needs, 30% to wants, and 20% to [savings](36-personal-finance-and-household-budget.md#savings).

### everyday expenses
- **IPA:** /ˌevrideɪ ɪkˈspensɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The small, regular costs of daily life, such as food and transport.
  - *Example:* A [prepaid](26-telecommunications-and-networks.md#prepaid) card keeps **everyday expenses** separate from bills.
