### advertising
- **IPA:** /ˈædvətaɪzɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The activity of promoting products or services through paid messages.
  - *Example:* Half the launch budget went on **advertising**.

### creative
- **IPA:** /kriˈeɪtɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The images, words, and design of an advertisement.
  - *Example:* The **creative** was approved but the media plan was not.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A person who develops ideas and content in an agency.
  - *Example:* Two **creatives** worked on the campaign for a month.

### copywriter
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒpiraɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who writes the text for advertisements and marketing.
  - *Example:* The **copywriter** cut the slogan from nine words to three.

### art director
- **IPA:** /ˈɑːt dəˌrektə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The person responsible for the visual style of an advertisement or publication.
  - *Example:* The **art director** chose a bold colour and a single image.

### jingle
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒɪŋɡl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short, catchy tune used in an advertisement.
  - *Example:* People still remember the **jingle** decades later.

### storyboard
- **IPA:** /ˈstɔːribɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A series of drawings showing the planned shots of a film or advertisement.
  - *Example:* The client signed off the **storyboard** before filming began.

### spot
- **IPA:** /spɒt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A single broadcast of an advertisement on TV or radio.
  - *Example:* They bought a 30-second **spot** during the final.

### placement
- **IPA:** /ˈpleɪsmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The position where an advertisement appears, such as a page or time slot.
  - *Example:* A front-page **placement** cost three times the usual rate.

### media buy
- **IPA:** /ˈmiːdiə baɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The purchase of advertising space or time.
  - *Example:* The agency handled the **media buy** across TV, print, and online.

### media plan
- **IPA:** /ˈmiːdiə plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A schedule setting out where and when advertisements will run.
  - *Example:* The **media plan** front-loaded spending into the first two weeks.

### reach
- **IPA:** /riːtʃ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The number of different people who see or hear an advertisement.
  - *Example:* The campaign's **reach** was 4 million adults.

### frequency
- **IPA:** /ˈfriːkwənsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The average number of times each person sees an advertisement.
  - *Example:* A **frequency** of three or four is usually needed to be remembered.

### impression
- **IPA:** /ɪmˈpreʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A single instance of an advertisement being shown to someone online.
  - *Example:* The banner ran up 2 million **impressions** in a week.

### click-through rate
- **IPA:** /ˈklɪk θruː reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The percentage of people who click an online ad after seeing it.
  - *Example:* A stronger headline doubled the **click-through rate**.

### cost per click
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒst pə ˈklɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount an advertiser pays each time someone clicks an ad.
  - *Example:* **Cost per click** rose sharply as more brands bid on the keyword.

### cost per thousand
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒst pə ˈθaʊznd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The price of showing an advertisement to one thousand people.
  - *Example:* Print has a higher **cost per thousand** than social media.

### conversion
- **IPA:** /kənˈvɜːʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The point at which a viewer of an ad takes the desired action, such as buying.
  - *Example:* The landing page was redesigned to lift **conversion**.

### engagement
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈɡeɪdʒmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The level of interaction people have with content, such as likes, shares, and comments.
  - *Example:* Video posts get far higher **engagement** than plain text.

### targeting
- **IPA:** /ˈtɑːɡɪtɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The choosing of a specific group of people to show advertising to.
  - *Example:* Tighter **targeting** reduced waste and improved results.

### retargeting
- **IPA:** /ˌriːˈtɑːɡɪtɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Showing ads to people who have already visited a website or shown interest.
  - *Example:* **Retargeting** reminded shoppers of items left in their basket.

### segmentation
- **IPA:** /ˌseɡmenˈteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The division of a market into groups with similar needs or behaviour.
  - *Example:* **Segmentation** by age and income shaped three different messages.

### psychographic
- **IPA:** /ˌsaɪkəˈɡræfɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to people's attitudes, values, and lifestyles rather than just age or income.
  - *Example:* **Psychographic** data showed the buyers cared most about sustainability.

### persona
- **IPA:** /pəˈsəʊnə/

### brand awareness

  - *Example:* The sponsorship deal was chosen to build **brand awareness**.

### brand equity
- **IPA:** /ˈbrænd ˌekwəti/

### brand loyalty
- **IPA:** /ˈbrænd ˌlɔɪəlti/

### rebrand
- **IPA:** /ˌriːˈbrænd/

### positioning statement

### unique selling point
- **IPA:** /juˌniːk ˈselɪŋ pɔɪnt/

  - *Example:* The **unique selling point** is a lifetime repair guarantee.

### call to action
- **IPA:** /ˌkɔːl tu ˈækʃn/

### above the line
