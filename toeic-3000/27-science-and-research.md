# 27 — Science & Research

### research
- **IPA:** /rɪˈsɜːtʃ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Careful study to discover new facts or test ideas.
  - *Example:* Years of **research** went into the new material.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To study a subject in detail.
  - *Example:* The team **researched** how customers actually use the product.

### theory
- **IPA:** /ˈθɪəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A well-supported explanation of how or why something happens.
  - *Example:* The results fit the leading **theory** well.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** An idea or set of ideas meant to explain something, not yet proven.
  - *Example:* One **theory** is that the fault is caused by heat.

### experiment
- **IPA:** /ɪkˈsperɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A test carried out under controlled conditions to check a hypothesis.
  - *Example:* The **experiment** was repeated five times to be sure.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To try out new ideas or methods.
  - *Example:* The kitchen **experiments** with a new dish each week.

### trial
- **IPA:** /ˈtraɪəl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A test of how well something works before it is used widely.
  - *Example:* The drug is in the final **trial** before [approval](06-projects-and-deadlines.md#approval).

### procedure
- **IPA:** /prəˈsiːdʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An established, ordered way of doing something.
  - *Example:* Follow the safety **procedure** exactly when [handling](../toeic-600/11-logistics-shipping-and-warehousing.md#17-handling) the acid.

### study protocol
- **IPA:** /ˈstʌdi ˌprəʊtəkɒl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A detailed plan setting out exactly how a study must be carried out.
  - *Example:* Any change to the **study protocol** must be [approved](03-management-and-leadership.md#approve) first.

### control group
- **IPA:** /kənˈtrəʊl ɡruːp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A group in a study that does not receive the treatment, used for comparison.
  - *Example:* The **control group** was given a sugar pill instead of the drug.

### placebo
- **IPA:** /pləˈsiːbəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A harmless substance given in place of a real drug to test its true effect.
  - *Example:* Neither the [patients](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#patient) nor the doctors knew who got the **placebo**.

### sample size
- **IPA:** /ˈsɑːmpl saɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The number of people or items included in a study.
  - *Example:* A small **sample size** made the results hard to trust.

### replication
- **IPA:** /ˌreplɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The repeating of a study by others to see if the same results appear.
  - *Example:* The finding held up under **replication** by three other labs.

### peer review
- **IPA:** /ˌpɪə rɪˈvjuː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The checking of research by other experts before it is published.
  - *Example:* The paper spent six months in **peer review**.

### publication
- **IPA:** /ˌpʌblɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of making research available in print or online; a published work.
  - *Example:* **Publication** of the study drew wide press coverage.

### journal
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒɜːnl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A periodical containing academic articles on a particular subject.
  - *Example:* The results appeared in a leading medical **journal**.

### citation
- **IPA:** /saɪˈteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A reference to another piece of work, showing where an idea or fact came from.
  - *Example:* The paper has over a thousand **citations**.

### abstract
- **IPA:** /ˈæbstrækt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short summary of a research paper, placed at the beginning.
  - *Example:* Most readers judge a paper from its **abstract**.

### literature review
- **IPA:** /ˈlɪtrətʃə rɪˌvjuː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A survey of what has already been published on a topic.
  - *Example:* The **literature review** showed no one had studied this exact question.

### data collection
- **IPA:** /ˈdeɪtə kəˌlekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The gathering of information for a study.
  - *Example:* **Data collection** ran over two winters.

### observation
- **IPA:** /ˌɒbzəˈveɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of watching something carefully to gain information.
  - *Example:* The conclusion is based on direct **observation** in the field.

### measurement
- **IPA:** /ˈmeʒəmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act or result of finding the size, amount, or degree of something.
  - *Example:* Each **measurement** was taken twice and [averaged](07-data-reports-and-analysis.md#average).

### instrument
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnstrəmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tool or device used for scientific work or measurement.
  - *Example:* A more sensitive **instrument** picked up the faint signal.

### accuracy
- **IPA:** /ˈækjərəsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How close a measurement is to the true value.
  - *Example:* The scale's **accuracy** is checked against a known weight.

### precision
- **IPA:** /prɪˈsɪʒn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How consistent repeated measurements are with each other.
  - *Example:* The readings had high **precision** but were slightly off the true value.

### error margin
- **IPA:** /ˈerə ˌmɑːdʒɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The range within which the true value is expected to lie.
  - *Example:* The result was 5.2 grams, with an **error margin** of 0.1.

### significance
- **IPA:** /sɪɡˈnɪfɪkəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** In statistics, the likelihood that a result is real and not due to chance.
  - *Example:* The difference reached **significance** only in the larger sample.

### causation
- **IPA:** /kɔːˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The relationship in which one thing directly produces another.
  - *Example:* The study showed a link but could not prove **causation**.

### confounding factor
- **IPA:** /kənˈfaʊndɪŋ ˌfæktə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A hidden variable that affects the result and can lead to a false conclusion.
  - *Example:* Age was a **confounding factor** the first [analysis](27-science-and-research.md#analysis) missed.

### empirical
- **IPA:** /ɪmˈpɪrɪkl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Based on observation and experiment rather than theory alone.
  - *Example:* There is little **empirical** [support](04-teamwork-and-workplace-relationships.md#support) for the claim.

### quantitative
- **IPA:** /ˈkwɒntɪtətɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Based on numbers and measurable amounts.
  - *Example:* The **quantitative** data came from a survey of 2,000 users.

### qualitative
- **IPA:** /ˈkwɒlɪtətɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Based on descriptions and qualities rather than numbers.
  - *Example:* **Qualitative** [interviews](../toeic-600/04-human-resources-and-recruitment.md#6-interview) revealed why customers felt frustrated.

### cross-sectional
- **IPA:** /ˌkrɒs ˈsekʃənl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Studying many subjects at a single point in time.
  - *Example:* A **cross-sectional** survey compared households of different sizes.

### laboratory
- **IPA:** /ləˈbɒrətri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A room or building equipped for scientific experiments and tests.
  - *Example:* Samples are sent to an outside **laboratory** for [analysis](27-science-and-research.md#analysis).

### fieldwork
- **IPA:** /ˈfiːldwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Research or study done in a real setting rather than a lab.
  - *Example:* Months of **fieldwork** in the forest produced the data.

### specimen
- **IPA:** /ˈspesɪmən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sample of something taken for study or testing.
  - *Example:* Each **specimen** is labelled with the date and location.

### reagent
- **IPA:** /riˈeɪdʒənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A substance used in a chemical reaction to detect or produce another substance.
  - *Example:* The test **reagent** turns blue if sugar is present.

### compound
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒmpaʊnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A substance made of two or more elements chemically joined.
  - *Example:* The new **compound** dissolves easily in water.

### molecule
- **IPA:** /ˈmɒlɪkjuːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The smallest unit of a substance that keeps its chemical properties.
  - *Example:* A water **molecule** has two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.

### catalyst
- **IPA:** /ˈkætəlɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A substance that speeds up a chemical reaction without being used up.
  - *Example:* The **catalyst** cut the [reaction](27-science-and-research.md#reaction) time from hours to minutes.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** An event or person that causes change.
  - *Example:* The report was the **catalyst** for a full policy review.

### reaction
- **IPA:** /riˈækʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A process in which substances change into different substances.
  - *Example:* The **reaction** gives off heat and a small amount of gas.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A response to something that has happened.
  - *Example:* The **reaction** to the price rise was swift and angry.

### synthesis
- **IPA:** /ˈsɪnθəsɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The making of a substance by combining simpler ones.
  - *Example:* The **synthesis** of the drug takes twelve steps.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The combining of ideas or information into a whole.
  - *Example:* The report is a **synthesis** of thirty separate studies.

### analysis
- **IPA:** /əˈnæləsɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A detailed examination of something to understand or explain it.
  - *Example:* Chemical **analysis** [identified](10-problem-solving-and-decision-making.md#identify) the pollutant.

### spectrometer
- **IPA:** /spekˈtrɒmɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An instrument that measures the properties of light to identify substances.
  - *Example:* The **spectrometer** confirmed the metal was pure.

### microscope
- **IPA:** /ˈmaɪkrəskəʊp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An instrument that makes very small objects appear larger.
  - *Example:* Under the **microscope**, the crack was clearly visible.

### centrifuge
- **IPA:** /ˈsentrɪfjuːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A machine that spins samples fast to separate their parts by weight.
  - *Example:* The blood was placed in the **centrifuge** to separate the plasma.

### incubator
- **IPA:** /ˈɪŋkjubeɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that keeps samples or organisms at a controlled temperature.
  - *Example:* The cultures were left in the **incubator** overnight.

### sterile
- **IPA:** /ˈsteraɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Completely free from bacteria and other living organisms.
  - *Example:* All [equipment](../toeic-600/05-office-procedures-and-equipment.md#26-equipment) must be **sterile** before the sample is opened.

### discovery
- **IPA:** /dɪˈskʌvəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The finding of something not known before.
  - *Example:* The **discovery** of the gene changed how the disease is treated.

### innovation
- **IPA:** /ˌɪnəˈveɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A new method, idea, or product; the act of introducing one.
  - *Example:* The company spends heavily on **innovation**.

### patent application
- **IPA:** /ˈpeɪtnt ˌæplɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal request for a legal right to be the only user of an invention.
  - *Example:* The **patent application** for the sensor is still being examined.

### grant
- **IPA:** /ɡrɑːnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sum of money given for a particular purpose, especially research.
  - *Example:* A [government](31-government-and-public-services.md#government) **grant** funded the first three years of the work.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To officially give or allow something.
  - *Example:* The [council](31-government-and-public-services.md#council) **granted** permission for the trial.

### funding body
- **IPA:** /ˈfʌndɪŋ ˌbɒdi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An organisation that provides money for research.
  - *Example:* The **funding body** requires a [progress report](06-projects-and-deadlines.md#progress-report) every year.

### principal investigator
- **IPA:** /ˌprɪnsəpl ɪnˈvestɪɡeɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The lead scientist responsible for a research project.
  - *Example:* As **principal investigator**, she signs off every result.

### research fellow
- **IPA:** /rɪˈsɜːtʃ ˌfeləʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A researcher, often early in their career, employed to work on a project.
  - *Example:* Two **research fellows** ran the day-to-day [experiments](27-science-and-research.md#experiment).

### ethics committee
- **IPA:** /ˈeθɪks kəˌmɪti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A group that checks whether a study treats people and animals properly.
  - *Example:* The **ethics committee** asked for changes to the [consent](14-law-and-compliance.md#consent) form.

### informed consent
- **IPA:** /ɪnˌfɔːmd kənˈsent/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Agreement to take part in a study after being told the risks and details.
  - *Example:* Every [participant](../toeic-600/06-meetings-and-presentations.md#8-participant) signed a form giving **informed consent**.

### reproducibility
- **IPA:** /ˌriːprəˌdjuːsəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The degree to which a study's results can be obtained again by others.
  - *Example:* Poor **reproducibility** cast doubt on the original claim.

### meta-analysis
- **IPA:** /ˌmetə əˈnæləsɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A study that combines the results of many earlier studies.
  - *Example:* A **meta-analysis** of 40 trials confirmed the small [benefit](../toeic-600/18-employee-benefits-and-insurance.md#1-benefit).

### model
- **IPA:** /ˈmɒdl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A simplified description or computer version of a system, used to study or predict it.
  - *Example:* The [climate](34-weather-climate-and-natural-events.md#climate) **model** projects warmer, wetter winters.

### simulation
- **IPA:** /ˌsɪmjuˈleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A computer representation of a real process, used for testing or training.
  - *Example:* A **simulation** showed the bridge would sway too much in high wind.

### validation
- **IPA:** /ˌvælɪˈdeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of checking that a method or model gives correct, trustworthy results.
  - *Example:* The test needs external **validation** before it can be used on [patients](29-health-medicine-and-wellness.md#patient).

### hypothesis testing
- **IPA:** /haɪˈpɒθəsɪs ˌtestɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A statistical method for deciding whether data support a particular claim.
  - *Example:* **Hypothesis testing** showed the new layout really did help.

### null hypothesis
- **IPA:** /ˌnʌl haɪˈpɒθəsɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The default claim that there is no effect or no difference, which a test tries to reject.
  - *Example:* The data let them reject the **null hypothesis** with confidence.

### researcher
- **IPA:** /rɪˈsɜːtʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who carries out academic or scientific study.
  - *Example:* A team of **researchers** spent two years on the project.

### scientific method
- **IPA:** /ˌsaɪənˌtɪfɪk ˈmeθəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The systematic process of observing, forming a hypothesis, testing, and drawing conclusions.
  - *Example:* [Students](30-education-and-training.md#student) learn the **scientific method** through simple [classroom](30-education-and-training.md#classroom) trials.

### research paper
- **IPA:** /rɪˈsɜːtʃ ˌpeɪpə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A written report of original research, usually published in a journal.
  - *Example:* The **research paper** ran to thirty pages with an [appendix](07-data-reports-and-analysis.md#appendix).

### lab technician
- **IPA:** /ˈlæb tekˌnɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who prepares equipment and carries out routine tests in a laboratory.
  - *Example:* A **lab technician** logged and stored every sample.

### field study
- **IPA:** /ˈfiːld ˌstʌdi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Research carried out in a natural or real-world setting.
  - *Example:* A **field study** of ten farms measured the effect of the new feed.

### empirical evidence
- **IPA:** /ɪmˌpɪrɪkl ˈevɪdəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Information gained from direct observation or experiment.
  - *Example:* There is now strong **empirical evidence** that the method works.

### research grant
- **IPA:** /rɪˈsɜːtʃ ɡrɑːnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money awarded to fund a specific research project.
  - *Example:* The lab won a five-year **research grant** to study soil health.

### replicate
- **IPA:** /ˈreplɪkeɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To repeat a study or experiment to check its results.
  - *Example:* Another group failed to **replicate** the original finding.

### test tube
- **IPA:** /ˈtest tjuːb/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small glass tube used to hold samples in a laboratory.
  - *Example:* Each **test tube** held a slightly different concentration.

### hypothesise
- **IPA:** /haɪˈpɒθəsaɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To suggest an explanation as a starting point for testing.
  - *Example:* The team **hypothesised** that heat was causing the cracks.

### experiment design
- **IPA:** /ɪkˈsperɪmənt dɪˌzaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The plan for how an experiment is set up, including groups, controls, and measures.
  - *Example:* A flaw in the **experiment design** meant the results were useless.

### research question
- **IPA:** /rɪˈsɜːtʃ ˌkwestʃən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The specific question that a study is designed to answer.
  - *Example:* The **research question** was whether shorter shifts reduce errors.

### control variable
- **IPA:** /kənˈtrəʊl ˌveəriəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A factor kept constant in an experiment so it cannot affect the result.
  - *Example:* Room [temperature](34-weather-climate-and-natural-events.md#temperature) was a **control variable** throughout the trial.

### research findings
- **IPA:** /rɪˈsɜːtʃ ˌfaɪndɪŋz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The results and conclusions produced by a study.
  - *Example:* The **research findings** were shared with the industry before [publication](27-science-and-research.md#publication).
