# 34 — Weather, Climate & Natural Events

### meteorologist
- **IPA:** /ˌmiːtiəˈrɒlədʒɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A scientist who studies and predicts the weather.
  - *Example:* A **meteorologist** explained why the storm changed course.

### barometer
- **IPA:** /bəˈrɒmɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An instrument that measures air pressure to help predict weather.
  - *Example:* A falling **barometer** usually means rain is on the way.

### atmospheric pressure
- **IPA:** /ˌætməsˌferɪk ˈpreʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The weight of the air pressing down on the earth's surface.
  - *Example:* Low **atmospheric pressure** brought a spell of wet weather.

### humidity
- **IPA:** /hjuːˈmɪdəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of water vapour in the air.
  - *Example:* High **humidity** made the heat feel much worse.

### precipitation
- **IPA:** /prɪˌsɪpɪˈteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Water that falls from the sky as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.
  - *Example:* Total **precipitation** for the month was twice the [average](07-data-reports-and-analysis.md#average).

### drizzle
- **IPA:** /ˈdrɪzl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Light rain in very fine drops.
  - *Example:* A cold **drizzle** fell all morning.

### downpour
- **IPA:** /ˈdaʊnpɔː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A heavy fall of rain, often sudden.
  - *Example:* A brief **downpour** flooded the underpass.

### cloudburst
- **IPA:** /ˈklaʊdbɜːst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sudden, very heavy rainstorm.
  - *Example:* A **cloudburst** dropped 40 millimetres of rain in an hour.

### hailstorm
- **IPA:** /ˈheɪlstɔːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A storm in which balls of ice fall from the sky.
  - *Example:* A **hailstorm** stripped the leaves off the vines.

### sleet
- **IPA:** /sliːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A mix of rain and partly melted snow.
  - *Example:* Morning **sleet** turned the roads to slush.

### frost
- **IPA:** /frɒst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A thin layer of ice that forms on surfaces when the temperature drops below freezing.
  - *Example:* A late **frost** damaged the fruit blossom.

### blizzard
- **IPA:** /ˈblɪzəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A severe snowstorm with strong winds.
  - *Example:* The **blizzard** closed the mountain roads for two days.

### snowdrift
- **IPA:** /ˈsnəʊdrɪft/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A deep bank of snow piled up by the wind.
  - *Example:* A **snowdrift** blocked the farm track completely.

### thaw
- **IPA:** /θɔː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period of warmer weather when snow and ice melt.
  - *Example:* The spring **thaw** raised the river level sharply.

### heatwave
- **IPA:** /ˈhiːtweɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A long period of unusually hot weather.
  - *Example:* The **heatwave** pushed electricity demand to a record.

### cold snap
- **IPA:** /ˈkəʊld snæp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short period of very cold weather.
  - *Example:* A **cold snap** in May caught the growers by surprise.

### dry spell
- **IPA:** /ˈdraɪ spel/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period of little or no rain.
  - *Example:* A three-week **dry spell** left the reservoirs low.

### monsoon
- **IPA:** /mɒnˈsuːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A seasonal wind that brings heavy rain to parts of Asia.
  - *Example:* The **monsoon** arrived a week late this year.

### gale
- **IPA:** /ɡeɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A very strong wind.
  - *Example:* A **gale** brought down trees across the region.

### squall
- **IPA:** /skwɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sudden violent gust of wind, often with rain or snow.
  - *Example:* A **squall** capsized two small boats in the bay.

### gust
- **IPA:** /ɡʌst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sudden strong rush of wind.
  - *Example:* A **gust** ripped the [sign off](03-management-and-leadership.md#sign-off) the wall.

### crosswind
- **IPA:** /ˈkrɒswɪnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A wind blowing across the direction of travel.
  - *Example:* A strong **crosswind** made landing difficult.

### front
- **IPA:** /frʌnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The boundary between two masses of air of different temperature.
  - *Example:* A weather **front** moving in from the west will bring rain by evening.

### cold front
- **IPA:** /ˈkəʊld frʌnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The leading edge of a mass of cold air, often bringing showers and clearer skies.
  - *Example:* A **cold front** dropped [temperatures](34-weather-climate-and-natural-events.md#temperature) by ten degrees overnight.

### warm front
- **IPA:** /ˈwɔːm frʌnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The leading edge of a mass of warm air, usually bringing steady rain then milder weather.
  - *Example:* A slow-moving **warm front** kept the skies grey for days.

### high pressure
- **IPA:** /ˌhaɪ ˈpreʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An area of heavier air that usually brings settled, dry weather.
  - *Example:* **High pressure** [sitting](23-hospitality-and-tourism-industry.md#sitting) over the country meant a week of sunshine.

### low pressure
- **IPA:** /ˌləʊ ˈpreʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An area of lighter air that usually brings clouds, wind, and rain.
  - *Example:* Deep **low pressure** in the Atlantic drove the storms ashore.

### overcast
- **IPA:** /ˌəʊvəˈkɑːst/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Completely covered with cloud.
  - *Example:* The sky stayed **overcast** all day, with no rain.

### visibility
- **IPA:** /ˌvɪzəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The distance you can see clearly, depending on weather conditions.
  - *Example:* Fog cut **visibility** to less than 50 metres.

### fog bank
- **IPA:** /ˈfɒɡ bæŋk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A thick mass of fog, especially one seen from a distance.
  - *Example:* A **fog bank** rolled in off the sea and swallowed the [harbour](21-transportation-and-shipping.md#harbour).

### mist
- **IPA:** /mɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A thin cloud of tiny water drops near the ground, less thick than fog.
  - *Example:* Morning **mist** cleared by nine o'clock.

### dew point
- **IPA:** /ˈdjuː pɔɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The temperature at which air becomes saturated and water begins to condense.
  - *Example:* A high **dew point** made the night feel sticky.

### wind chill
- **IPA:** /ˈwɪnd tʃɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How cold the air feels because of the wind, colder than the actual temperature.
  - *Example:* The **wind chill** made it feel like minus fifteen.

### ultraviolet index
- **IPA:** /ˌʌltrəˈvaɪələt ˌɪndeks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A scale showing how strong the sun's harmful rays are on a given day.
  - *Example:* A high **ultraviolet index** was [forecast](07-data-reports-and-analysis.md#forecast), so sun cream was advised.

### storm surge
- **IPA:** /ˈstɔːm sɜːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A rise in sea level pushed ashore by a storm, causing coastal flooding.
  - *Example:* The **storm surge** put the seafront road under a metre of water.

### tidal wave
- **IPA:** /ˈtaɪdl weɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A very large ocean wave, often caused by an earthquake or storm.
  - *Example:* A **tidal wave** swept boats onto the promenade.

### flooding
- **IPA:** /ˈflʌdɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The covering of normally dry land with water.
  - *Example:* Heavy rain caused **flooding** in low-lying streets.

### flash flood
- **IPA:** /ˈflæʃ flʌd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sudden flood that rises very quickly, often in a dry area after intense rain.
  - *Example:* A **flash flood** swept a car off the desert road.

### landslide
- **IPA:** /ˈlændslaɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A mass of earth and rock sliding down a slope.
  - *Example:* A **landslide** blocked the railway for a week.

### mudslide
- **IPA:** /ˈmʌdslaɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A flow of soft wet earth moving quickly down a hillside.
  - *Example:* Days of rain [triggered](10-problem-solving-and-decision-making.md#trigger) a **mudslide** above the village.

### avalanche
- **IPA:** /ˈævəlɑːnʃ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large mass of snow and ice sliding suddenly down a mountain.
  - *Example:* An **avalanche** closed the ski area and cut off two huts.

### earthquake
- **IPA:** /ˈɜːθkweɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sudden violent shaking of the ground caused by movement in the earth's crust.
  - *Example:* The **earthquake** damaged hundreds of older buildings.

### tremor
- **IPA:** /ˈtremə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small earthquake or shaking movement.
  - *Example:* A minor **tremor** rattled windows across the city.

### aftershock
- **IPA:** /ˈɑːftəʃɒk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A smaller earthquake that follows a larger one.
  - *Example:* Rescue work was slowed by repeated **aftershocks**.

### epicentre
- **IPA:** /ˈepɪsentə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The point on the surface directly above where an earthquake begins.
  - *Example:* The **epicentre** was 20 kilometres [offshore](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#offshore).

### magnitude
- **IPA:** /ˈmæɡnɪtjuːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A measure of the strength of an earthquake.
  - *Example:* The quake had a **magnitude** of 6.4.

### fault line
- **IPA:** /ˈfɔːlt laɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A crack in the earth's crust along which earthquakes tend to happen.
  - *Example:* The city sits close to a major **fault line**.

### volcanic eruption
- **IPA:** /vɒlˌkænɪk ɪˈrʌpʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The bursting out of lava, ash, and gas from a volcano.
  - *Example:* A **volcanic eruption** grounded flights across the region.

### ash cloud
- **IPA:** /ˈæʃ klaʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A cloud of fine volcanic particles carried through the air.
  - *Example:* The **ash cloud** drifted for hundreds of kilometres.

### lava flow
- **IPA:** /ˈlɑːvə fləʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A stream of molten rock moving away from a volcano.
  - *Example:* The **lava flow** reached the edge of the town within days.

### tsunami
- **IPA:** /tsuːˈnɑːmi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A series of huge sea waves caused by an undersea earthquake or landslide.
  - *Example:* A warning system now gives coastal towns time to react to a **tsunami**.

### cyclone
- **IPA:** /ˈsaɪkləʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large, violent storm with winds spinning around a calm centre.
  - *Example:* The **cyclone** flattened crops across the delta.

### hurricane
- **IPA:** /ˈhʌrɪkən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A very powerful tropical storm, especially in the Atlantic.
  - *Example:* The **hurricane** was [downgraded](11-economics-and-markets.md#downgrade) before it reached land.

### typhoon
- **IPA:** /taɪˈfuːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tropical storm of great strength in the western Pacific.
  - *Example:* Ports closed as the **typhoon** approached the coast.

### tornado
- **IPA:** /tɔːˈneɪdəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A violently spinning column of air that reaches the ground.
  - *Example:* A **tornado** tore the roof from the grain store.

### waterspout
- **IPA:** /ˈwɔːtəspaʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A spinning column of air and water that forms over the sea.
  - *Example:* Sailors filmed a **waterspout** off the headland.

### evacuation
- **IPA:** /ɪˌvækjuˈeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The organised movement of people away from a dangerous area.
  - *Example:* The **evacuation** of the coastal town began at dawn.

### emergency shelter
- **IPA:** /ɪˈmɜːdʒənsi ˌʃeltə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A safe place set up to house people forced from their homes by a disaster.
  - *Example:* The school gym was used as an **emergency shelter** for 200 families.

### relief effort
- **IPA:** /rɪˈliːf ˌefət/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Organised work to provide food, shelter, and aid after a disaster.
  - *Example:* The **relief effort** brought in [supplies](../toeic-600/05-office-procedures-and-equipment.md#7-supplies) by helicopter.

### weather warning
- **IPA:** /ˈweðə ˌwɔːnɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official message telling people that dangerous weather is expected.
  - *Example:* A **weather warning** for high winds was issued for the coast.

### severe weather alert
- **IPA:** /sɪˌvɪə ˈweðər əˌlɜːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An urgent notice that extreme and dangerous weather is likely soon.
  - *Example:* A **severe weather alert** closed schools across the county.

### seasonal outlook
- **IPA:** /ˈsiːzənl ˌaʊtlʊk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A general forecast of what a coming season's weather is likely to be.
  - *Example:* The **seasonal outlook** points to a drier-than-usual autumn.

### climate pattern
- **IPA:** /ˈklaɪmət ˌpætn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A regular, large-scale behaviour of the climate over months or years.
  - *Example:* A shifting **climate pattern** has made the dry season longer.

### El Niño
- **IPA:** /el ˈniːnjəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A warming of the Pacific Ocean that changes weather around the world.
  - *Example:* **El Niño** brought heavy rain to the west coast and [drought](20-agriculture-and-food-supply.md#drought) elsewhere.

### jet stream
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒet striːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fast, narrow band of wind high in the atmosphere that steers weather systems.
  - *Example:* A dip in the **jet stream** pulled cold air far to the south.

### weather forecast
- **IPA:** /ˈweðə ˌfɔːkɑːst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A statement of what the weather is expected to be.
  - *Example:* The **weather forecast** promised a dry weekend.

### temperature
- **IPA:** /ˈtemprətʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How hot or cold something is, measured on a scale.
  - *Example:* The **temperature** dropped below [freezing](20-agriculture-and-food-supply.md#freezing) after dark.

### rainfall
- **IPA:** /ˈreɪnfɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of rain that falls in a place over a period.
  - *Example:* Annual **rainfall** has fallen steadily over the last decade.

### storm
- **IPA:** /stɔːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Very bad weather with strong wind and usually rain, snow, or thunder.
  - *Example:* The **storm** knocked out power to thousands of homes.

### flood
- **IPA:** /flʌd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large amount of water covering land that is usually dry.
  - *Example:* The **flood** reached the first floor of riverside houses.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To cover or fill with water.
  - *Example:* The river burst its banks and **flooded** the fields.

### natural disaster
- **IPA:** /ˌnætʃrəl dɪˈzɑːstə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sudden event in nature, such as a flood or earthquake, that causes great damage.
  - *Example:* The region has a fund to rebuild after a **natural disaster**.

### climate
- **IPA:** /ˈklaɪmət/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The usual weather conditions of an area over many years.
  - *Example:* The mild **climate** makes the coast popular with retirees.

### weather conditions
- **IPA:** /ˈweðə kənˌdɪʃnz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The state of the weather at a particular time and place.
  - *Example:* Poor **weather conditions** delayed the flight by two hours.

### wind speed
- **IPA:** /ˈwɪnd spiːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How fast the wind is blowing.
  - *Example:* **Wind speed** reached 120 kilometres an hour on the ridge.

### disaster relief
- **IPA:** /dɪˈzɑːstə rɪˌliːf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money, supplies, and help sent to an area hit by a disaster.
  - *Example:* International **disaster relief** arrived within 48 hours of the quake.
