# 19 — Energy & Utilities

### utility
- **IPA:** /juːˈtɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that supplies a basic public service such as water, gas, or electricity.
  - *Example:* The **utility** raised its prices for the third time in a year.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The quality of being useful.
  - *Example:* The tool has little **utility** outside the lab.

### grid
- **IPA:** /ɡrɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The network of cables that carries electricity across a region or country.
  - *Example:* Power from the wind farm feeds straight into the **grid**.

### power plant
- **IPA:** /ˈpaʊə plɑːnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large facility where electricity is generated.
  - *Example:* The gas-fired **power plant** supplies half a million homes.

### generator
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒenəreɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A machine that turns mechanical energy into electricity.
  - *Example:* A diesel **generator** kept the hospital running during the cut.

### turbine
- **IPA:** /ˈtɜːbaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A machine with blades turned by water, steam, gas, or wind to produce power.
  - *Example:* Steam from the boiler spins the **turbine**.

### transformer
- **IPA:** /trænsˈfɔːmə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that raises or lowers the voltage of an electricity supply.
  - *Example:* A failed **transformer** cut power to the whole street.

### substation
- **IPA:** /ˈsʌbsteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A local site where high-voltage electricity is stepped down for distribution.
  - *Example:* The **substation** was upgraded to handle the new housing estate.

### transmission
- **IPA:** /trænzˈmɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The movement of electricity at high voltage over long distances.
  - *Example:* Some energy is always lost during **transmission**.

### voltage
- **IPA:** /ˈvəʊltɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The force that pushes an electric current through a circuit, measured in volts.
  - *Example:* The equipment runs on a **voltage** of 240.

### current
- **IPA:** /ˈkʌrənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A flow of electricity through a wire or circuit.
  - *Example:* A safety switch cuts the **current** if a fault is detected.

### wattage
- **IPA:** /ˈwɒtɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of electrical power something uses or produces, measured in watts.
  - *Example:* Lower-**wattage** bulbs cut the lighting bill.

### kilowatt-hour
- **IPA:** /ˌkɪləwɒt ˈaʊə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A unit of energy equal to using one kilowatt for one hour, used for billing.
  - *Example:* The tariff charges 28 cents per **kilowatt-hour**.

### meter
- **IPA:** /ˈmiːtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that measures how much of a utility is used.
  - *Example:* The **meter** is read every three months.

### meter reading
- **IPA:** /ˈmiːtə ˌriːdɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The figure shown on a utility meter, recorded for billing.
  - *Example:* Submit a **meter reading** to avoid an estimated bill.

### off-peak
- **IPA:** /ˌɒf ˈpiːk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to times of low demand, when prices are usually lower.
  - *Example:* The washing machine runs on cheaper **off-peak** electricity at night.

### peak demand
- **IPA:** /ˌpiːk dɪˈmɑːnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The highest level of use of a utility during a period.
  - *Example:* **Peak demand** occurs on cold winter evenings.

### load
- **IPA:** /ləʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of power being drawn from a system at a given time.
  - *Example:* Air conditioning adds a heavy **load** in summer.

### outage
- **IPA:** /ˈaʊtɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period when a supply of electricity or another service is not available.
  - *Example:* A planned **outage** on Sunday will allow repairs to the line.

### blackout
- **IPA:** /ˈblækaʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A complete loss of electrical power in an area.
  - *Example:* The storm caused a **blackout** across three districts.

### brownout
- **IPA:** /ˈbraʊnaʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A drop in voltage that dims lights and can damage equipment, short of a full blackout.
  - *Example:* Repeated **brownouts** shortened the life of the motors.

### backup generator
- **IPA:** /ˈbækʌp ˌdʒenəreɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A generator that starts automatically when the main supply fails.
  - *Example:* The data centre has two **backup generators** and stored fuel for a week.

### uninterruptible supply
- **IPA:** /ˌʌnɪntəˈrʌptəbl səˌplaɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A battery system that keeps power flowing for a short time during a failure.
  - *Example:* An **uninterruptible supply** keeps the servers alive until the generator starts.

### fossil fuel
- **IPA:** /ˌfɒsl ˈfjuːəl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fuel such as coal, oil, or gas formed from the remains of living things long ago.
  - *Example:* The country is cutting its reliance on **fossil fuel**.

### coal
- **IPA:** /kəʊl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A hard black rock burned as fuel, especially in power stations.
  - *Example:* The last **coal** station is due to close in 2027.

### natural gas
- **IPA:** /ˌnætʃrəl ˈɡæs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A gas found underground, used for heating and generating electricity.
  - *Example:* **Natural gas** prices spiked after the pipeline was shut.

### crude oil
- **IPA:** /ˌkruːd ˈɔɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Unrefined petroleum as it comes out of the ground.
  - *Example:* **Crude oil** is shipped to the coast for refining.

### refinery
- **IPA:** /rɪˈfaɪnəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A plant where crude oil is processed into fuels and other products.
  - *Example:* A fire at the **refinery** cut national fuel supplies.

### pipeline
- **IPA:** /ˈpaɪplaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A long line of pipes for carrying oil, gas, or water over a distance.
  - *Example:* The new **pipeline** links the gas field to the port.

### drilling
- **IPA:** /ˈdrɪlɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of making deep holes in the ground to reach oil, gas, or water.
  - *Example:* **Drilling** for gas began after years of surveys.

### extraction
- **IPA:** /ɪkˈstrækʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of removing a natural resource from the ground.
  - *Example:* The cost of **extraction** rises as the easy reserves run out.

### reserves
- **IPA:** /rɪˈzɜːvz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A supply of a natural resource that is known to exist and can be used.
  - *Example:* The field holds enough gas **reserves** for 20 years.

### renewable
- **IPA:** /rɪˈnjuːəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of energy) Coming from a source that is not used up, such as sun, wind, or water.
  - *Example:* Half the region's power is now **renewable**.

### solar panel
- **IPA:** /ˈsəʊlə ˌpænl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A flat device that converts sunlight into electricity or heat.
  - *Example:* **Solar panels** on the roof cover most of the office's daytime use.

### photovoltaic
- **IPA:** /ˌfəʊtəʊvɒlˈteɪɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to the direct conversion of light into electricity.
  - *Example:* The **photovoltaic** cells still work on cloudy days, just less well.

### wind farm
- **IPA:** /ˈwɪnd fɑːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A group of wind turbines used to generate electricity.
  - *Example:* The offshore **wind farm** powers 300,000 homes.

### hydroelectric
- **IPA:** /ˌhaɪdrəʊɪˈlektrɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to electricity made using the force of moving water.
  - *Example:* The dam runs a large **hydroelectric** station.

### geothermal
- **IPA:** /ˌdʒiːəʊˈθɜːml/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to heat from inside the earth, used for power or heating.
  - *Example:* **Geothermal** heat warms the whole town's water.

### biomass
- **IPA:** /ˈbaɪəʊmæs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Plant or animal material burned as fuel to produce energy.
  - *Example:* The plant switched from coal to wood-pellet **biomass**.

### nuclear reactor
- **IPA:** /ˌnjuːkliə riˈæktə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device in which nuclear reactions are controlled to produce energy.
  - *Example:* The **nuclear reactor** was shut down for refuelling.

### fuel rod
- **IPA:** /ˈfjuːəl rɒd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sealed tube of nuclear fuel used inside a reactor.
  - *Example:* Spent **fuel rods** are stored underwater for years.

### energy efficiency
- **IPA:** /ˈenədʒi ɪˌfɪʃnsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The practice of using less energy to do the same work.
  - *Example:* Better **energy efficiency** cut the factory's power bill by 30%.

### smart meter
- **IPA:** /ˈsmɑːt ˌmiːtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A meter that sends usage readings to the supplier automatically.
  - *Example:* A **smart meter** shows how much each appliance costs to run.

### feed-in tariff
- **IPA:** /ˈfiːd ɪn ˌtærɪf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A payment to households or firms for electricity they generate and send to the grid.
  - *Example:* The **feed-in tariff** made the rooftop panels pay for themselves.

### net metering
- **IPA:** /ˌnet ˈmiːtərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A billing system that offsets the power you export against the power you import.
  - *Example:* Under **net metering**, the summer surplus covers winter use.

### base load
- **IPA:** /ˈbeɪs ləʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The minimum level of demand on a grid over a period, met by steady sources.
  - *Example:* Nuclear plants are well suited to supplying **base load**.

### peak load
- **IPA:** /ˈpiːk ləʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The maximum level of demand on a grid, met by fast-starting plants.
  - *Example:* Gas turbines are switched on to cover the evening **peak load**.

### energy audit
- **IPA:** /ˈenədʒi ˌɔːdɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A survey of how a building or business uses energy and where it could save.
  - *Example:* The **energy audit** recommended sealing draughts and adding timers.

### wastage
- **IPA:** /ˈweɪstɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of something lost through careless or inefficient use.
  - *Example:* Leaving lights on overnight is pure **wastage**.

### standby power
- **IPA:** /ˈstændbaɪ ˌpaʊə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The electricity used by devices that are switched off but still plugged in.
  - *Example:* **Standby power** can add 5% to a home's electricity bill.

### thermal
- **IPA:** /ˈθɜːml/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to heat.
  - *Example:* **Thermal** imaging showed where heat was escaping the roof.

### cooling tower
- **IPA:** /ˈkuːlɪŋ ˌtaʊə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tall structure that removes waste heat from a power station by cooling water.
  - *Example:* Steam rose from the **cooling towers** at dawn.

### boiler
- **IPA:** /ˈbɔɪlə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A container in which water is heated to produce hot water or steam.
  - *Example:* The building's **boiler** was replaced with a more efficient model.

### heat exchanger
- **IPA:** /ˈhiːt ɪksˌtʃeɪndʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that transfers heat from one fluid to another without mixing them.
  - *Example:* A **heat exchanger** recovers warmth from the outgoing air.

### district heating
- **IPA:** /ˈdɪstrɪkt ˌhiːtɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A system that supplies heat to many buildings from one central source.
  - *Example:* The new estate is warmed by **district heating** from a waste plant.

### off-grid
- **IPA:** /ˌɒf ˈɡrɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Not connected to the main electricity network.
  - *Example:* The remote lodge is **off-grid**, running on solar and a generator.

### microgrid
- **IPA:** /ˈmaɪkrəʊɡrɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small local energy network that can run on its own or with the main grid.
  - *Example:* The campus **microgrid** kept power on during the regional outage.

### storage battery
- **IPA:** /ˈstɔːrɪdʒ ˌbætəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large battery that stores electricity for use when demand or generation changes.
  - *Example:* A **storage battery** soaks up midday solar power for the evening.

### demand response
- **IPA:** /dɪˈmɑːnd rɪˌspɒns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A scheme where users cut power use at busy times in return for lower bills.
  - *Example:* Under **demand response**, the plant pauses some machines during peaks.

### utility bill
- **IPA:** /juːˈtɪləti bɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A statement of charges for a service such as electricity, gas, or water.
  - *Example:* The **utility bill** doubled after the cold spell.

### connection charge
- **IPA:** /kəˈnekʃn tʃɑːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fixed fee for being connected to a utility network, separate from usage.
  - *Example:* The **connection charge** is billed even in months with no use.

### supply contract
- **IPA:** /səˈplaɪ ˌkɒntrækt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An agreement setting the price and terms for delivering a utility over a period.
  - *Example:* The firm locked in a two-year **supply contract** before prices rose.

### national grid
- **IPA:** /ˌnæʃnəl ˈɡrɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The country-wide network that carries electricity from generators to users.
  - *Example:* Surplus wind power is sent into the **national grid**.

### power line
- **IPA:** /ˈpaʊə laɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A cable that carries electricity, especially one supported on poles or towers.
  - *Example:* A fallen tree brought down the **power line**.

### kilowatt
- **IPA:** /ˈkɪləwɒt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A unit of power equal to one thousand watts.
  - *Example:* The heater draws two **kilowatts** at full setting.

### megawatt
- **IPA:** /ˈmeɡəwɒt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A unit of power equal to one million watts, used for power stations.
  - *Example:* The new solar farm has a capacity of 50 **megawatts**.

### energy provider
- **IPA:** /ˈenədʒi prəˌvaɪdə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that sells gas or electricity to customers.
  - *Example:* Switching **energy provider** saved the household $200 a year.

### energy mix
- **IPA:** /ˈenədʒi mɪks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The combination of different sources used to supply a country's or company's energy.
  - *Example:* Renewables now make up 40% of the national **energy mix**.

### grid connection
- **IPA:** /ˈɡrɪd kəˌnekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The physical and contractual link between a generator or user and the grid.
  - *Example:* The wind farm waited two years for a **grid connection**.

### power output
- **IPA:** /ˈpaʊər ˌaʊtpʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of electrical power a source produces.
  - *Example:* The turbine's **power output** falls when the wind drops.

### power cut
- **IPA:** /ˈpaʊə kʌt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A temporary loss of electricity supply.
  - *Example:* A brief **power cut** reset every clock in the building.

### electricity meter
- **IPA:** /ɪlekˈtrɪsəti ˌmiːtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A device that records how much electricity a property uses.
  - *Example:* The **electricity meter** is in a box beside the front door.

### gas supply
- **IPA:** /ˈɡæs səˌplaɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The provision of gas to a building or area through pipes.
  - *Example:* The **gas supply** was shut off while the leak was fixed.

### power surge
- **IPA:** /ˈpaʊə sɜːdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sudden sharp increase in electrical current that can damage equipment.
  - *Example:* A **power surge** during the storm destroyed two computers.

### energy consumption
- **IPA:** /ˈenədʒi kənˌsʌmpʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The total amount of energy used by a person, building, or country.
  - *Example:* New lighting cut the store's **energy consumption** by a fifth.

### load shedding
- **IPA:** /ˈləʊd ˌʃedɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The deliberate switching off of power to some areas to prevent the whole grid failing.
  - *Example:* Rolling **load shedding** left each district without power for two hours.
