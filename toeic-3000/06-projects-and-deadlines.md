# 06 — Projects & Deadlines

### project
- **IPA:** /ˈprɒdʒekt/ (n) · /prəˈdʒekt/ (v)
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A planned piece of work with a clear aim, done over a set period.
  - *Example:* The website **project** runs from March to July.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To estimate or forecast a future figure based on current data.
  - *Example:* We **project** sales of 40,000 units next year.
- **Meaning 3 (v):** To make light or an image fall on a surface.
  - *Example:* She **projected** the chart onto the wall.

### task
- **IPA:** /tɑːsk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A piece of work that must be done.
  - *Example:* Break the job into small **tasks** with named owners.

### subtask
- **IPA:** /ˈsʌbtɑːsk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A smaller task that is part of a larger one.
  - *Example:* Each **subtask** should take no more than a day.

### deliverable
- **IPA:** /dɪˈlɪvərəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A specific item or result that a project must produce.
  - *Example:* The first **deliverable** is a working prototype.

### phase
- **IPA:** /feɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A distinct stage in a process or project.
  - *Example:* Testing begins in the second **phase**.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To introduce or remove something gradually (*phase in / phase out*).
  - *Example:* The old software will be **phased** out by June.

### stage
- **IPA:** /steɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A point or period in a process of development.
  - *Example:* The design is at an early **stage**.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To organise and carry out an event or action.
  - *Example:* The union **staged** a one-day walkout.

### sprint
- **IPA:** /sprɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short, fixed period in which a team completes a set amount of work.
  - *Example:* The feature was finished in a two-week **sprint**.

### iteration
- **IPA:** /ˌɪtəˈreɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** One version or cycle in a repeated process of improvement.
  - *Example:* The third **iteration** of the layout finally tested well.

### scope
- **IPA:** /skəʊp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The range of work, subjects, or activities that something deals with.
  - *Example:* Translation is outside the **scope** of this contract.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The opportunity or freedom to do or develop something.
  - *Example:* There is **scope** to cut costs in packaging.

### scope creep
- **IPA:** /ˈskəʊp kriːp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The gradual, uncontrolled growth of a project's requirements.
  - *Example:* Weekly extra requests caused serious **scope creep**.

### requirement
- **IPA:** /rɪˈkwaɪəmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Something that is needed or demanded.
  - *Example:* The client listed twelve **requirements** for the app.

### kickoff
- **IPA:** /ˈkɪkɒf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The first meeting or event that starts a project.
  - *Example:* Everyone attends the **kickoff** to agree goals and roles.

### Gantt chart
- **IPA:** /ˈɡænt tʃɑːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A bar chart showing project tasks against time.
  - *Example:* The **Gantt chart** shows testing overlapping with training.

### critical path
- **IPA:** /ˌkrɪtɪkl ˈpɑːθ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The sequence of tasks that determines the shortest possible project length.
  - *Example:* Any delay on the **critical path** delays the whole launch.

### dependency
- **IPA:** /dɪˈpendənsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A task or item that must be completed before another can start.
  - *Example:* Design is a **dependency** for both coding and testing.

### bottleneck
- **IPA:** /ˈbɒtlnek/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A point where progress slows because of limited capacity.
  - *Example:* Approvals from one manager are the main **bottleneck**.

### blocker
- **IPA:** /ˈblɒkə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Something that stops work from continuing until it is resolved.
  - *Example:* Raise any **blocker** at the daily [stand-up](04-teamwork-and-workplace-relationships.md#stand-up).

### constraint
- **IPA:** /kənˈstreɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A limitation or restriction on what can be done.
  - *Example:* Budget is the tightest **constraint** on this project.

### resource
- **IPA:** /rɪˈzɔːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A supply of money, staff, materials, or skills that can be used.
  - *Example:* We lack the **resource** to run two pilots at once.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To provide what is needed for a task.
  - *Example:* The project was never properly **resourced**.

### workload
- **IPA:** /ˈwɜːkləʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of work a person or team must do.
  - *Example:* Adding this project doubles her **workload**.

### capacity
- **IPA:** /kəˈpæsəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of work that a person or team is able to do.
  - *Example:* The team has no spare **capacity** until next month.

### allocation
- **IPA:** /ˌæləˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of assigning resources or shares for a purpose.
  - *Example:* The staff **allocation** gives two people to testing.

### assignment
- **IPA:** /əˈsaɪnmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A task or job given to someone.
  - *Example:* His first **assignment** was to map the current process.

### handover
- **IPA:** /ˈhændəʊvə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of passing responsibility, work, or information to someone else.
  - *Example:* Write a **handover** note before you go on leave.

### sign-off
- **IPA:** /ˈsaɪn ɒf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Formal approval that a piece of work is complete and acceptable.
  - *Example:* We need client **sign-off** before the next phase.

### due date
- **IPA:** /ˈdjuː deɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The date by which something must be finished or paid.
  - *Example:* The **due date** for the draft is Friday.

### buffer
- **IPA:** /ˈbʌfə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Extra time or resources kept in reserve to absorb delays.
  - *Example:* Build a two-day **buffer** into the schedule.

### slack
- **IPA:** /slæk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Spare time in a schedule during which a task could be delayed without harm.
  - *Example:* There is some **slack** before the printing stage.

### overrun
- **IPA:** /ˈəʊvərʌn/ (n) · /ˌəʊvəˈrʌn/ (v)
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An amount by which time or cost goes beyond the plan.
  - *Example:* The budget **overrun** reached 20%.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To continue beyond an expected time or limit.
  - *Example:* The meeting **overran** by half an hour.

### delay
- **IPA:** /dɪˈleɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period of time by which something is late.
  - *Example:* A parts shortage caused a three-week **delay**.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To make something happen later than planned.
  - *Example:* Bad weather **delayed** the site work.

### slippage
- **IPA:** /ˈslɪpɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A failure to meet a target date, causing the schedule to move back.
  - *Example:* Small **slippage** at each stage added up to a month.

### on track
- **IPA:** /ˌɒn ˈtræk/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** Progressing as planned and likely to meet the goal.
  - *Example:* The project is **on track** for a June finish.

### behind schedule
- **IPA:** /bɪˌhaɪnd ˈʃedjuːl/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** Later than the planned timing.
  - *Example:* Testing is a week **behind schedule**.

### ahead of schedule
- **IPA:** /əˌhed əv ˈʃedjuːl/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** Earlier than the planned timing.
  - *Example:* The build finished **ahead of schedule** and under budget.

### progress
- **IPA:** /ˈprəʊɡres/ (n) · /prəˈɡres/ (v)
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Forward movement toward a goal.
  - *Example:* We made good **progress** on the backlog this week.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To move forward or develop over time.
  - *Example:* The talks are **progressing** slowly.

### status update
- **IPA:** /ˈsteɪtəs ˌʌpdeɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short report on the current state of a task or project.
  - *Example:* Send a weekly **status update** to the [sponsor](#sponsor).

### checkpoint
- **IPA:** /ˈtʃekpɔɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A planned point at which progress is reviewed before continuing.
  - *Example:* The next **checkpoint** decides whether to go live.

### review
- **IPA:** /rɪˈvjuː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal examination of work or performance.
  - *Example:* The design passed its safety **review**.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To examine something again in order to judge or improve it.
  - *Example:* Let's **review** the plan after the pilot.

### walkthrough
- **IPA:** /ˈwɔːkθruː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A step-by-step demonstration or explanation of how something works.
  - *Example:* She gave the client a **walkthrough** of the new system.

### approval
- **IPA:** /əˈpruːvl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Official agreement that allows something to happen.
  - *Example:* Purchases over $1,000 need written **approval**.

### escalation
- **IPA:** /ˌeskəˈleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of passing a problem to a more senior person for a decision.
  - *Example:* There is a clear **escalation** path for missed deadlines.

### risk register
- **IPA:** /ˈrɪsk ˌredʒɪstə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document listing possible risks, their likelihood, and planned responses.
  - *Example:* Supplier failure is the top item on the **risk register**.

### mitigation
- **IPA:** /ˌmɪtɪˈɡeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Action taken to reduce the seriousness or likelihood of a problem.
  - *Example:* As **mitigation**, we lined up a second supplier.

### rework
- **IPA:** /ˈriːwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Work that has to be done again because it was not right the first time.
  - *Example:* Unclear specs led to hours of **rework**.

### backlog
- **IPA:** /ˈbæklɒɡ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A quantity of work that has built up and still needs to be done.
  - *Example:* The support **backlog** grew during the holidays.

### expedite
- **IPA:** /ˈekspədaɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To make a process happen more quickly.
  - *Example:* We paid a fee to **expedite** the permit.

### wrap up
- **IPA:** /ˌræp ˈʌp/

  - *Example:* Let's **wrap up** the remaining tasks by Thursday.

### close out
- **IPA:** /ˌkləʊz ˈaʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (phr v):** To formally finish a project, settling all open items.
  - *Example:* Finance can't **close out** the project until every invoice is paid.

### post-mortem
- **IPA:** /ˌpəʊst ˈmɔːtəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A review after a project or event to analyse what happened and why.
  - *Example:* The **post-mortem** found that the estimate was too optimistic.

### lessons learned
- **IPA:** /ˌlesnz ˈlɜːnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Knowledge gained from experience that can improve future work.
  - *Example:* The **lessons learned** were shared with every project team.

### deliver
- **IPA:** /dɪˈlɪvə/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To produce or provide something that was promised.

### execute

### track

### monitor

  - *Example:* Each desk has two **monitors**.

### work package
- **IPA:** /ˈwɜːk ˌpækɪdʒ/

### project charter

  - *Example:* The **project charter** names the sponsor and the budget.

### sponsor

  - *Example:* The **sponsor** approved extra funds after the review.

### scope document
- **IPA:** /ˈskəʊp ˌdɒkjumənt/

### punch list

### project manager

  - *Example:* The **project manager** chases every open action.

### project plan

### work breakdown

### progress report

  - *Example:* The monthly **progress report** goes to the whole [steering committee](02-company-organization-and-departments.md#steering-committee).

### workstream
- **IPA:** /ˈwɜːkstriːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** One of several parallel areas of activity within a larger programme.
  - *Example:* The training **workstream** runs alongside the technical build.

### project lifecycle

### project scope

### change request

  - *Example:* Each **change request** is reviewed before it is accepted.
