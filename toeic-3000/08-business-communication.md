### briefing
- **IPA:** /ˈbriːfɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A meeting or document giving people the information they need for a task.
  - *Example:* The morning **briefing** covers safety and the day's targets.

### bulletin
- **IPA:** /ˈbʊlətɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short official statement or news report.
  - *Example:* A staff **bulletin** goes out every Friday afternoon.

### circular
- **IPA:** /ˈsɜːkjələ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A printed notice or letter sent to a large number of people.
  - *Example:* Head office sent a **circular** about the new expenses rules.

### notice
- **IPA:** /ˈnəʊtɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A written announcement displayed or given to inform people.
  - *Example:* A **notice** on the door explained the closure.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** Advance warning that something will happen.
  - *Example:* Tenants must give one month's **notice**.
- **Meaning 3 (v):** To become aware of something.
  - *Example:* Did you **notice** the change to the schedule?

### announcement
- **IPA:** /əˈnaʊnsmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A public or formal statement giving news or information.
  - *Example:* The **announcement** of the merger came after markets closed.

### disclosure
- **IPA:** /dɪsˈkləʊʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of making information known, especially information that was secret.
  - *Example:* Full **disclosure** of fees is required by law.

### statement
- **IPA:** /ˈsteɪtmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal written or spoken account of facts or views.
  - *Example:* The company issued a **statement** denying the reports.

### press release
- **IPA:** /ˈpres rɪˌliːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official statement given to journalists for publication.
  - *Example:* The **press release** announced 200 new jobs.

### brochure
- **IPA:** /ˈbrəʊʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small booklet with pictures and information about a product or service.
  - *Example:* Take a **brochure** from the stand near reception.

### leaflet
- **IPA:** /ˈliːflət/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A single printed sheet giving information or advertising.
  - *Example:* A **leaflet** about the recall was sent to every customer.

### pamphlet
- **IPA:** /ˈpæmflət/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small, thin book with a paper cover, giving information on one subject.
  - *Example:* The safety **pamphlet** explains what to do in a fire.

### prospectus
- **IPA:** /prəˈspektəs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal document describing a company or course to attract investors or students.
  - *Example:* The share **prospectus** sets out the risks in detail.

### newsletter
- **IPA:** /ˈnjuːzletə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A regular printed or emailed report sent to members or customers.
  - *Example:* The monthly **newsletter** features one customer story.

### digest
- **IPA:** /ˈdaɪdʒest/ (n) · /daɪˈdʒest/ (v)
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short summary of longer pieces of writing or news.
  - *Example:* A weekly **digest** collects the main industry headlines.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To think about information until you fully understand it.
  - *Example:* Give the team a day to **digest** the report.

### enclosure
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈkləʊʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Something placed inside an envelope or package along with a letter.
  - *Example:* The letter listed three **enclosures**, but only two arrived.

### bcc
- **IPA:** /ˌbiː siː ˈsiː/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To send a copy of an email to someone without other recipients seeing their address.
  - *Example:* **bcc** the whole list so addresses stay private.

### reply-all
- **IPA:** /rɪˌplaɪ ˈɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (v/n):** To send a reply to everyone who received the original email.
  - *Example:* Please don't **reply-all** just to say thanks.

### reminder
- **IPA:** /rɪˈmaɪndə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A message that helps someone remember to do something.
  - *Example:* A **reminder** goes out two days before the deadline.

### confirmation
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒnfəˈmeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A statement or document that says something is definitely true or arranged.
  - *Example:* You will get email **confirmation** once the order ships.

### notification
- **IPA:** /ˌnəʊtɪfɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official message telling someone that something has happened or will happen.
  - *Example:* Staff received **notification** of the office move by letter.

### disclaimer
- **IPA:** /dɪsˈkleɪmə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A statement that limits legal responsibility or denies a connection.
  - *Example:* The email carries a standard confidentiality **disclaimer**.

### footer
- **IPA:** /ˈfʊtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Text that appears at the bottom of every page or email.
  - *Example:* The contact details are in the **footer**.

### salutation
- **IPA:** /ˌsæljuˈteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The greeting at the start of a letter or email, such as "Dear Ms Lee".
  - *Example:* Use a formal **salutation** for a first contact.

### tone
- **IPA:** /təʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The general attitude or feeling expressed in a piece of writing or speech.
  - *Example:* The reply's **tone** was polite but firm.

### register
- **IPA:** /ˈredʒɪstə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The level of formality in language, chosen to suit the situation.
  - *Example:* A report to the board needs a more formal **register** than a team chat.

### jargon
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒɑːɡən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Special words used by a profession that others find hard to understand.
  - *Example:* Cut the **jargon** so customers can follow the letter.

### buzzword
- **IPA:** /ˈbʌzwɜːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fashionable word or phrase, often vague, common in business talk.
  - *Example:* The email was full of **buzzwords** but said very little.

### phrasing
- **IPA:** /ˈfreɪzɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The particular way words are chosen and put together.
  - *Example:* Careful **phrasing** kept the apology from sounding defensive.

### wording
- **IPA:** /ˈwɜːdɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The specific words used in a piece of writing.
  - *Example:* Legal changed the **wording** of one sentence in the contract.

### concise
- **IPA:** /kənˈsaɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Giving a lot of information clearly in few words.
  - *Example:* Keep the update **concise**: three bullet points at most.

### verbose
- **IPA:** /vɜːˈbəʊs/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Using more words than needed.
  - *Example:* The **verbose** introduction buried the main request.

### ambiguous
- **IPA:** /æmˈbɪɡjuəs/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Having more than one possible meaning; unclear.
  - *Example:* "Soon" is too **ambiguous**; give a date.

### clarity
- **IPA:** /ˈklærəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The quality of being clear and easy to understand.
  - *Example:* The rewrite gained **clarity** by using shorter sentences.

### brevity
- **IPA:** /ˈbrevəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The quality of using few words; shortness.
  - *Example:* Readers will thank you for **brevity**.

### courteous
- **IPA:** /ˈkɜːtiəs/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Polite and respectful.
  - *Example:* Even a firm refusal can be **courteous**.

### tactful
- **IPA:** /ˈtæktfl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Careful not to say or do anything that would upset someone.
  - *Example:* A **tactful** note reminded him without blaming him.

### diplomatic
- **IPA:** /ˌdɪpləˈmætɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Skilled at dealing with people in a sensitive, tactful way.
  - *Example:* She gave a **diplomatic** answer that upset no one.

### persuasive
- **IPA:** /pəˈsweɪsɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Good at making people agree or believe something.
  - *Example:* The proposal made a **persuasive** case for more staff.

### coherent
- **IPA:** /kəʊˈhɪərənt/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Clear, logical, and well organised.
  - *Example:* The argument was **coherent** and easy to follow.

### legible
- **IPA:** /ˈledʒəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Clear enough to be read.
  - *Example:* Please write in **legible** capitals on the form.

### proofread
- **IPA:** /ˈpruːfriːd/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To read a text carefully to find and correct mistakes.
  - *Example:* Always **proofread** an email to a client before sending.

### redraft
- **IPA:** /ˌriːˈdrɑːft/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To write a new, improved version of a document.
  - *Example:* She **redrafted** the notice to make it friendlier.

### paraphrase
- **IPA:** /ˈpærəfreɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To express someone's meaning using different words.
  - *Example:* He **paraphrased** the policy so the team could act on it.

### quote
- **IPA:** /kwəʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To repeat words exactly as someone said or wrote them.
  - *Example:* The article **quotes** the CEO directly.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A price stated for a job before it is done.
  - *Example:* We got a **quote** of £900 for the repair.

### cite
- **IPA:** /saɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To mention something as an example or as support for an argument.
  - *Example:* The report **cites** three studies on remote work.

### verbatim
- **IPA:** /vɜːˈbeɪtɪm/
- **Meaning 1 (adv/adj):** Using exactly the same words; word for word.
  - *Example:* The minutes record the motion **verbatim**.

### off the record
- **IPA:** /ˌɒf ðə ˈrekɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** Said in private and not to be reported or quoted.
  - *Example:* **Off the record**, she admitted the launch was too early.

### on the record
- **IPA:** /ˌɒn ðə ˈrekɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (phr):** Said openly and able to be quoted or reported.
  - *Example:* The spokesperson would not comment **on the record**.

### talking points
- **IPA:** /ˈtɔːkɪŋ pɔɪnts/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short list of the main points a speaker wants to make.
  - *Example:* Managers were sent **talking points** for the staff meeting.

### key message
- **IPA:** /ˌkiː ˈmesɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The single most important idea you want the audience to remember.
  - *Example:* The **key message** is that service quality will not change.

### elevator pitch
- **IPA:** /ˈelɪveɪtə pɪtʃ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A very short description of an idea that can be given in under a minute.
  - *Example:* Practise your **elevator pitch** before the investor day.

### town hall

  - *Example:* The quarterly **town hall** is streamed to all sites.

### all-hands
- **IPA:** /ˌɔːl ˈhændz/

### readout

### circulate

### disseminate

### convey

### briefing note

### bullet point

### formatting

  - *Example:* He left a **recap note** so the absent members were up to date.

### write-up

  - *Example:* The trade-show **write-up** goes to the whole sales team.
