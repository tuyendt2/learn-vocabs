# 32 — Media, News & Publishing

> Cross-links jump to another entry in the 3000-word set. **Ctrl/Cmd + click** to follow.

### journalism
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒɜːnəlɪzəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The work of gathering and reporting news for the public.
  - *Example:* The award recognised [outstanding](../toeic-600/09-sales-and-purchasing.md#24-outstanding) investigative **journalism**.

### reporter
- **IPA:** /rɪˈpɔːtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who gathers and writes or broadcasts news.
  - *Example:* A **reporter** was sent to cover the factory closure.

### correspondent
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒrəˈspɒndənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A journalist who reports on a particular place or subject.
  - *Example:* The paper's [economics](11-economics-and-markets.md#economics) **correspondent** [analysed](10-problem-solving-and-decision-making.md#analyse) the budget.

### editor
- **IPA:** /ˈedɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who decides the content of a publication and prepares it for release.
  - *Example:* The **editor** cut the [article](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#article) by a third before printing.

### sub-editor
- **IPA:** /ˈsʌb ˌedɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who checks and corrects text and writes headlines before publication.
  - *Example:* A **sub-editor** spotted the wrong date in the first paragraph.

### columnist
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒləmnɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A writer who contributes a regular opinion piece to a publication.
  - *Example:* Her weekly **columnist** slot covers workplace trends.

### stringer
- **IPA:** /ˈstrɪŋə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A part-time or local reporter who supplies stories to a news organisation.
  - *Example:* A **stringer** in the region filed the first report of the storm.

### byline
- **IPA:** /ˈbaɪlaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The line naming the writer of an article.
  - *Example:* The story ran without a **byline** to protect the [reporter](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#reporter).

### dateline
- **IPA:** /ˈdeɪtlaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A line at the start of a report giving the place and date it was written.
  - *Example:* The **dateline** read "Geneva, Tuesday".

### headline
- **IPA:** /ˈhedlaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The title of a news article, printed in large type.
  - *Example:* The **headline** overstated what the study actually found.

### lead
- **IPA:** /liːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The opening sentence or paragraph of a news story, giving the main point.
  - *Example:* The **lead** must answer who, what, where, and when.

### standfirst
- **IPA:** /ˈstændfɜːst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short introductory paragraph between the headline and the article.
  - *Example:* The **standfirst** summed up the piece in two sentences.

### caption
- **IPA:** /ˈkæpʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short text under a photo explaining what it shows.
  - *Example:* The **caption** named everyone in the picture.

### masthead
- **IPA:** /ˈmɑːsthed/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The name of a newspaper or magazine printed at the top of the front page.
  - *Example:* The **masthead** has not changed in eighty years.

### broadsheet
- **IPA:** /ˈbrɔːdʃiːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large-format newspaper, traditionally seen as more serious.
  - *Example:* The story led every **broadsheet** the next morning.

### tabloid
- **IPA:** /ˈtæblɔɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small-format newspaper with short articles and many pictures, often sensational.
  - *Example:* The **tabloid** ran the rumour as fact.

### periodical
- **IPA:** /ˌpɪəriˈɒdɪkl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A magazine or journal published at regular intervals.
  - *Example:* The [library](30-education-and-training.md#library) subscribes to forty trade **periodicals**.

### quarterly
- **IPA:** /ˈkwɔːtəli/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A publication that comes out four times a year.
  - *Example:* The [research](27-science-and-research.md#research) **quarterly** carried the full study.

### subscription
- **IPA:** /səbˈskrɪpʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An arrangement to receive a publication or service regularly for payment.
  - *Example:* Digital **subscriptions** now bring in more than print sales.

### newsstand
- **IPA:** /ˈnjuːzstænd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A stall or shop that sells newspapers and magazines.
  - *Example:* The [magazine](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#magazine) sold out at every **newsstand** in the city.

### wire service
- **IPA:** /ˈwaɪə ˌsɜːvɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An organisation that supplies news reports to many outlets at once.
  - *Example:* The **wire service** flashed the result within seconds.

### news agency
- **IPA:** /ˈnjuːz ˌeɪdʒənsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that collects news and sells it to newspapers and broadcasters.
  - *Example:* A **news agency** photographer captured the key moment.

### press pass
- **IPA:** /ˈpres pɑːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official card allowing a journalist access to an event or restricted area.
  - *Example:* [Reporters](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#reporter) needed a **press pass** to enter the courtroom.

### exclusive
- **IPA:** /ɪkˈskluːsɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A story reported by only one news outlet.
  - *Example:* The paper ran the [interview](../toeic-600/04-human-resources-and-recruitment.md#6-interview) as an **exclusive**.

### scoop
- **IPA:** /skuːp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An important news story reported before any competitor.
  - *Example:* The merger leak was a real **scoop** for the young [reporter](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#reporter).

### breaking news
- **IPA:** /ˌbreɪkɪŋ ˈnjuːz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** News that is happening now and reported immediately.
  - *Example:* The channel cut to **breaking news** of the [evacuation](34-weather-climate-and-natural-events.md#evacuation).

### feature
- **IPA:** /ˈfiːtʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A longer article that explores a subject in depth rather than reporting an event.
  - *Example:* The weekend **feature** followed one family for a year.

### op-ed
- **IPA:** /ˌɒp ˈed/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An opinion article written by someone who is not on the newspaper's staff.
  - *Example:* A former [minister](31-government-and-public-services.md#minister) wrote an **op-ed** defending the policy.

### editorial
- **IPA:** /ˌedɪˈtɔːriəl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An article giving the newspaper's own opinion on an issue.
  - *Example:* The **editorial** called for an independent [inquiry](../toeic-600/07-correspondence-and-email.md#5-inquiry).

### retraction
- **IPA:** /rɪˈtrækʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A public statement withdrawing something previously reported as untrue.
  - *Example:* The [magazine](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#magazine) printed a full **retraction** and an apology.

### press correction
- **IPA:** /ˈpres kəˌrekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A published note putting right an error in an earlier report.
  - *Example:* A small **press correction** appeared on page two the next day.

### fact-check
- **IPA:** /ˈfækt tʃek/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To verify the accuracy of claims before or after publication.
  - *Example:* Every figure in the piece was **fact-checked** against the source.

### source
- **IPA:** /sɔːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person or document that provides information for a story.
  - *Example:* Two independent **sources** confirmed the account.

### attribution
- **IPA:** /ˌætrɪˈbjuːʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Stating who provided a piece of information or made a statement.
  - *Example:* The quote was published with full **attribution**.

### libel
- **IPA:** /ˈlaɪbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The publishing of a false written statement that damages someone's reputation.
  - *Example:* The company sued the website for **libel**.

### defamation
- **IPA:** /ˌdefəˈmeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of harming someone's reputation with false statements.
  - *Example:* He dropped the **defamation** claim after a printed apology.

### censorship
- **IPA:** /ˈsensəʃɪp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The suppression of information or opinion by an authority.
  - *Example:* Wartime **censorship** kept troop movements out of the papers.

### gag order
- **IPA:** /ˈɡæɡ ˌɔːdə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A court order forbidding public discussion of a case.
  - *Example:* A **gag order** stopped the media naming the company.

### freedom of the press
- **IPA:** /ˌfriːdəm əv ðə ˈpres/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The right of news organisations to report without government control.
  - *Example:* The law was criticised as a threat to **freedom of the press**.

### paywall
- **IPA:** /ˈpeɪwɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A system that blocks online content until the reader pays or subscribes.
  - *Example:* The investigation sat behind a **paywall** for a week before being opened up.

### clickbait
- **IPA:** /ˈklɪkbeɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Content with a misleading or exaggerated headline designed to attract clicks.
  - *Example:* Readers grew tired of the site's **clickbait** [headlines](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#headline).

### aggregator
- **IPA:** /ˈæɡrɪɡeɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A website or app that collects news stories from many sources in one place.
  - *Example:* Most of the traffic came through a news **aggregator**.

### syndication
- **IPA:** /ˌsɪndɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The selling of an article, column, or programme to many outlets.
  - *Example:* The cartoon earns most of its money from **syndication**.

### podcast
- **IPA:** /ˈpɒdkɑːst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A recorded audio programme made available online for download or streaming.
  - *Example:* The paper launched a daily news **podcast**.

### streaming
- **IPA:** /ˈstriːmɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Watching or listening to media over the internet in real time, without downloading it.
  - *Example:* **Streaming** now accounts for most video [viewing](../toeic-600/19-real-estate-and-property.md#22-viewing).

### broadcast
- **IPA:** /ˈbrɔːdkɑːst/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To send out a programme by radio or television.
  - *Example:* The debate was **broadcast** live on three channels.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A radio or television programme.
  - *Example:* The morning **broadcast** reaches two million listeners.

### broadcast transmission
- **IPA:** /ˈbrɔːdkɑːst trænzˌmɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The sending out of a radio or television programme signal.
  - *Example:* A fault stopped the **broadcast transmission** for twenty minutes.

### anchor
- **IPA:** /ˈæŋkə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The main presenter of a television or radio news programme.
  - *Example:* The evening news **anchor** has been with the [network](../toeic-600/13-technology-and-computers.md#5-network) for years.

### newsroom
- **IPA:** /ˈnjuːzruːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The office where journalists work to produce the news.
  - *Example:* The **newsroom** was cut from 80 staff to 30.

### deadline pressure
- **IPA:** /ˈdedlaɪn ˌpreʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The stress of having to finish work by a fixed time.
  - *Example:* Under **deadline pressure**, small errors slip through.

### print run
- **IPA:** /ˈprɪnt rʌn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The number of copies of a publication printed at one time.
  - *Example:* The first **print run** of the book was 10,000 copies.

### proof
- **IPA:** /pruːf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A trial print of a text used to check for errors before final printing.
  - *Example:* The author found three mistakes in the **proof**.

### galley
- **IPA:** /ˈɡæli/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An early printed version of a text, used for correction and review.
  - *Example:* Reviewers were sent **galley** copies months before [publication](27-science-and-research.md#publication).

### typesetting
- **IPA:** /ˈtaɪpsetɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The arranging of text into the form it will take on the printed page.
  - *Example:* **Typesetting** the dictionary took a full year.

### layout
- **IPA:** /ˈleɪaʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The way text and images are arranged on a page.
  - *Example:* A cleaner **layout** made the [magazine](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#magazine) easier to read.

### imprint
- **IPA:** /ˈɪmprɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A brand name under which a publisher issues certain books.
  - *Example:* The novel appeared under the publisher's crime **imprint**.

### publisher
- **IPA:** /ˈpʌblɪʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person or company that produces and sells books, papers, or magazines.
  - *Example:* Three **publishers** bid for the rights to the memoir.

### manuscript
- **IPA:** /ˈmænjuskrɪpt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An author's text before it is published.
  - *Example:* The **manuscript** was accepted after two rounds of edits.

### royalties
- **IPA:** /ˈrɔɪəltiz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Payments made to an author or artist based on sales of their work.
  - *Example:* She earns **royalties** on every copy sold.

### copyright clearance
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒpiraɪt ˌklɪərəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Permission obtained to use text, images, or music owned by someone else.
  - *Example:* **Copyright clearance** for the photos delayed the book by a month.

### ghostwriter
- **IPA:** /ˈɡəʊstraɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A writer who produces a book or article credited to someone else.
  - *Example:* The autobiography was written by a **ghostwriter**.

### serialization
- **IPA:** /ˌsɪəriəlaɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The publishing of a book in parts over several issues of a newspaper or magazine.
  - *Example:* [Newspaper](32-media-news-and-publishing.md#newspaper) **serialization** brought the book to a huge [audience](33-arts-culture-and-entertainment.md#audience).

### circulation figures
- **IPA:** /ˌsɜːkjəˈleɪʃn ˌfɪɡəz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The numbers showing how many copies of a publication are sold.
  - *Example:* Falling **circulation figures** forced the paper to go weekly.

### journalist
- **IPA:** /ˈdʒɜːnəlɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who writes or reports news for a living.
  - *Example:* A [freelance](01-career-and-professional-development.md#freelance) **journalist** broke the story.

### news story
- **IPA:** /ˈnjuːz ˌstɔːri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A report of a recent event, published or broadcast.
  - *Example:* The **news story** was picked up around the world.

### front page
- **IPA:** /ˌfrʌnt ˈpeɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The first page of a newspaper, carrying the most important stories.
  - *Example:* The scandal was on the **front page** for a week.

### newspaper
- **IPA:** /ˈnjuːzpeɪpə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A printed publication issued daily or weekly with news, features, and advertising.
  - *Example:* The local **newspaper** covered the [council](31-government-and-public-services.md#council) [meeting](../toeic-600/06-meetings-and-presentations.md#1-meeting) in full.

### article
- **IPA:** /ˈɑːtɪkl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A piece of writing on a particular subject in a newspaper, magazine, or website.
  - *Example:* The **article** quoted three industry experts.

### edition
- **IPA:** /ɪˈdɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A particular version of a publication issued at one time.
  - *Example:* The story made the late **edition** but not the early one.

### publishing house
- **IPA:** /ˈpʌblɪʃɪŋ haʊs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that produces books.
  - *Example:* A small **publishing house** took a chance on the debut novel.

### freelance journalist
- **IPA:** /ˌfriːlɑːns ˈdʒɜːnəlɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A journalist who sells work to different outlets rather than being employed by one.
  - *Example:* As a **freelance journalist**, she pitches stories to several papers.

### news report
- **IPA:** /ˈnjuːz rɪˌpɔːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An account of a recent event given in the media.
  - *Example:* Early **news reports** turned out to be inaccurate.

### news desk
- **IPA:** /ˈnjuːz desk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The section of a newsroom that decides which stories to cover and assigns reporters.
  - *Example:* Call the **news desk** if you have a tip.

### magazine
- **IPA:** /ˌmæɡəˈziːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A publication with articles and pictures, issued weekly or monthly.
  - *Example:* The trade **magazine** goes to every member of the association.

### press briefing
- **IPA:** /ˈpres ˌbriːfɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A meeting where an organisation gives journalists information and takes questions.
  - *Example:* The daily **press briefing** was moved online.
