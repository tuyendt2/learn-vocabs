# 11 — Economics & Markets

### economy
- **IPA:** /ɪˈkɒnəmi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The system by which a country produces, distributes, and uses goods and money.
  - *Example:* The national **economy** grew by 2% last year.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** Careful use of money or resources to avoid waste.
  - *Example:* For the sake of **economy**, they cut back on travel.

### economics
- **IPA:** /ˌiːkəˈnɒmɪks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The study of how money, industry, and trade are organised.
  - *Example:* She has a degree in **economics**.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The financial aspects of something.
  - *Example:* The **economics** of the project only work at large scale.

### macroeconomic
- **IPA:** /ˌmækrəʊˌiːkəˈnɒmɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to the economy as a whole, such as growth, inflation, and employment.
  - *Example:* **Macroeconomic** conditions favour borrowers this year.

### microeconomic
- **IPA:** /ˌmaɪkrəʊˌiːkəˈnɒmɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to the behaviour of individual firms, households, and markets.
  - *Example:* At the **microeconomic** level, each store sets its own prices.

### gross domestic product
- **IPA:** /ˌɡrəʊs dəˌmestɪk ˈprɒdʌkt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The total value of goods and services produced in a country in a year.
  - *Example:* Exports make up a third of the country's **gross domestic product**.

### growth
- **IPA:** /ɡrəʊθ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An increase in size, value, or amount.
  - *Example:* The company reported strong **growth** in overseas sales.

### recession
- **IPA:** /rɪˈseʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period when the economy shrinks and trade and industry decline.
  - *Example:* Demand for luxury goods fell sharply during the **recession**.

### downturn
- **IPA:** /ˈdaʊntɜːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A fall in economic or business activity.
  - *Example:* The **downturn** forced the firm to delay hiring.

### slump
- **IPA:** /slʌmp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sudden, severe fall in prices, sales, or activity.
  - *Example:* A **slump** in orders left the factory half idle.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To fall suddenly and by a large amount.
  - *Example:* Share prices **slumped** after the profit warning.

### recovery
- **IPA:** /rɪˈkʌvəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A return to a normal or stronger condition after a decline.
  - *Example:* A slow **recovery** began in the second half of the year.

### upturn
- **IPA:** /ˈʌptɜːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An improvement or rise in economic or business activity.
  - *Example:* Retailers reported an **upturn** in spring sales.

### boom
- **IPA:** /buːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period of rapid economic growth and prosperity.
  - *Example:* The housing **boom** pushed prices to record levels.

### stagnation
- **IPA:** /stæɡˈneɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period with little or no growth or activity.
  - *Example:* Years of **stagnation** left wages barely rising.

### inflation
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈfleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A general rise in prices and fall in the purchasing power of money.
  - *Example:* **Inflation** reached 6%, its highest level in a decade.

### deflation
- **IPA:** /dɪˈfleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A general fall in prices across an economy.
  - *Example:* **Deflation** can make people delay purchases, hoping for lower prices.

### purchasing power
- **IPA:** /ˈpɜːtʃəsɪŋ ˌpaʊə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of goods or services that a unit of money can buy.
  - *Example:* Rising prices cut households' **purchasing power**.

### cost of living
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒst əv ˈlɪvɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of money needed to pay for basic needs such as food and housing.
  - *Example:* The **cost of living** is much higher in the capital.

### wage
- **IPA:** /weɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A regular payment made to an employee, usually by the hour or week.
  - *Example:* The minimum **wage** rises again in April.

### income
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnkʌm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money received from work, investments, or business.
  - *Example:* Rental **income** covers most of the mortgage.

### disposable income
- **IPA:** /dɪˌspəʊzəbl ˈɪnkʌm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The money left to spend or save after tax and essential costs.
  - *Example:* Higher fuel bills squeezed **disposable income**.

### unemployment
- **IPA:** /ˌʌnɪmˈplɔɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The state of not having a job; the number of people without work.
  - *Example:* **Unemployment** fell to its lowest level in five years.

### labour market
- **IPA:** /ˈleɪbə ˌmɑːkɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The supply of and demand for workers.
  - *Example:* A tight **labour market** made skilled staff hard to find.

### productivity
- **IPA:** /ˌprɒdʌkˈtɪvəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The rate at which goods or services are produced, per worker or per hour.
  - *Example:* New machines raised **productivity** by a fifth.

### supply
- **IPA:** /səˈplaɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of a good or service available to buyers.
  - *Example:* A poor harvest cut the **supply** of wheat.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To provide something that is needed.
  - *Example:* Three firms **supply** us with packaging.

### demand
- **IPA:** /dɪˈmɑːnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The desire and ability of buyers to purchase a good or service.
  - *Example:* **Demand** for electric bikes rose sharply.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To ask for something firmly.
  - *Example:* Customers **demanded** refunds after the fault was found.

### equilibrium
- **IPA:** /ˌiːkwɪˈlɪbriəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A state of balance, especially between supply and demand.
  - *Example:* Prices settle where the market reaches **equilibrium**.

### shortage
- **IPA:** /ˈʃɔːtɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A situation in which there is not enough of something.
  - *Example:* A chip **shortage** delayed car production worldwide.

### scarcity
- **IPA:** /ˈskeəsəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A lack of something; a situation where demand is greater than supply.
  - *Example:* Water **scarcity** raised the cost of farming in the region.

### commodity
- **IPA:** /kəˈmɒdəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A raw material or basic good that is traded, such as oil, metal, or grain.
  - *Example:* **Commodity** prices swing with global demand.

### consumption
- **IPA:** /kənˈsʌmpʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The using up of goods, services, or resources.
  - *Example:* Household **consumption** drives most of the economy.

### trade
- **IPA:** /treɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The buying and selling of goods and services, especially between countries.
  - *Example:* **Trade** between the two nations doubled in a decade.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To buy and sell goods or services.
  - *Example:* The firm **trades** mainly in farm machinery.

### export
- **IPA:** /ˈekspɔːt/ (n) · /ɪkˈspɔːt/ (v)
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A good or service sold to another country.
  - *Example:* Wine is one of the country's biggest **exports**.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To send goods to another country for sale.
  - *Example:* They **export** to more than 40 markets.

### import
- **IPA:** /ˈɪmpɔːt/ (n) · /ɪmˈpɔːt/ (v)
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A good or service brought in from another country.
  - *Example:* Cheap **imports** undercut local producers.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To bring goods into a country from abroad.
  - *Example:* We **import** most of our raw cotton.

### tariff
- **IPA:** /ˈtærɪf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tax on goods coming into or leaving a country.
  - *Example:* A new **tariff** on steel raised costs for builders.

### quota
- **IPA:** /ˈkwəʊtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official limit on the amount of something allowed.
  - *Example:* An import **quota** caps how many cars can enter each year.

### embargo
- **IPA:** /ɪmˈbɑːɡəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official ban on trade with a particular country.
  - *Example:* The **embargo** cut off the firm's largest market overnight.

### trade deficit
- **IPA:** /ˈtreɪd ˌdefɪsɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount by which a country's imports exceed its exports.
  - *Example:* A weaker currency helped shrink the **trade deficit**.

### trade surplus
- **IPA:** /ˈtreɪd ˌsɜːpləs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount by which a country's exports exceed its imports.
  - *Example:* Strong car exports gave the country a large **trade surplus**.

### balance of payments
- **IPA:** /ˌbæləns əv ˈpeɪmənts/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A record of all money flowing into and out of a country.
  - *Example:* Tourism income improved the **balance of payments**.

### exchange rate
- **IPA:** /ɪksˈtʃeɪndʒ reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The value of one currency expressed in terms of another.
  - *Example:* A falling **exchange rate** made imported parts dearer.

### devaluation
- **IPA:** /ˌdiːvæljuˈeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official reduction in the value of a currency against others.
  - *Example:* **Devaluation** boosted exports but raised the price of fuel.

### appreciation
- **IPA:** /əˌpriːʃiˈeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A rise in the value of a currency or an asset over time.
  - *Example:* Currency **appreciation** hurt the country's exporters.

### monetary policy
- **IPA:** /ˈmʌnɪtri ˌpɒləsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A central bank's actions to control the money supply and interest rates.
  - *Example:* Tighter **monetary policy** is meant to slow inflation.

### fiscal policy
- **IPA:** /ˈfɪskl ˌpɒləsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A government's decisions on taxation and public spending.
  - *Example:* **Fiscal policy** loosened as the government raised spending on roads.

### central bank
- **IPA:** /ˌsentrəl ˈbæŋk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The national institution that manages a country's currency and interest rates.
  - *Example:* The **central bank** raised its base rate by half a point.

### interest rate
- **IPA:** /ˈɪntrəst reɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The percentage charged for borrowing money or paid on savings.
  - *Example:* Higher **interest rates** cooled the housing market.

### stimulus
- **IPA:** /ˈstɪmjələs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Government action, such as extra spending or tax cuts, to encourage economic growth.
  - *Example:* A **stimulus** package aimed to protect jobs during the downturn.

### austerity
- **IPA:** /ɔːˈsterəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A policy of reduced public spending to lower government debt.
  - *Example:* Years of **austerity** cut budgets for local services.

### subsidy
- **IPA:** /ˈsʌbsədi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Money given by a government to support an industry or keep prices low.
  - *Example:* A fuel **subsidy** kept transport costs down.

### bailout
- **IPA:** /ˈbeɪlaʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Financial help given to a failing company or economy to save it.
  - *Example:* The airline received a government **bailout** to avoid collapse.

### monopoly
- **IPA:** /məˈnɒpəli/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Complete control of a good or service by one company, with no competition.
  - *Example:* The rail operator had a **monopoly** on the route.

### oligopoly
- **IPA:** /ˌɒlɪˈɡɒpəli/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A market controlled by a small number of large companies.
  - *Example:* Three firms form an **oligopoly** in mobile networks.

### competition
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒmpəˈtɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Rivalry between companies trying to win customers.
  - *Example:* Strong **competition** kept prices low for shoppers.

### deregulation
- **IPA:** /ˌdiːreɡjuˈleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The removal of government rules controlling an industry.
  - *Example:* **Deregulation** of the energy market let new suppliers enter.

### privatization
- **IPA:** /ˌpraɪvətaɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The transfer of a business or service from government to private ownership.
  - *Example:* The **privatization** of the water company was controversial.

### nationalization
- **IPA:** /ˌnæʃnəlaɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The transfer of a private business into government ownership.
  - *Example:* **Nationalization** of the failing bank protected savers.

### emerging market
- **IPA:** /ɪˌmɜːdʒɪŋ ˈmɑːkɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A developing economy that is growing fast and becoming more advanced.
  - *Example:* The firm sees its fastest growth in **emerging markets**.

### developed economy
- **IPA:** /dɪˌveləpt ɪˈkɒnəmi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A country with high income, advanced industry, and stable institutions.
  - *Example:* Demand in **developed economies** is flat but reliable.

### current account
- **IPA:** /ˌkʌrənt əˈkaʊnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The part of the balance of payments covering trade in goods, services, and income.
  - *Example:* The **current account** moved into surplus as exports rose.

### sentiment
- **IPA:** /ˈsentɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The general mood or attitude of consumers or investors.
  - *Example:* Business **sentiment** improved after the rate cut.

### volatility
- **IPA:** /ˌvɒləˈtɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The tendency of prices or markets to change quickly and unpredictably.
  - *Example:* Currency **volatility** makes long-term pricing hard.

### speculation
- **IPA:** /ˌspekjuˈleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Buying assets in the hope of quick profit from price changes, with high risk.
  - *Example:* **Speculation** drove property prices well above their real value.

### bubble
- **IPA:** /ˈbʌbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A situation where prices rise far above real value before falling sharply.
  - *Example:* Analysts warned that the tech **bubble** would burst.

### per capita
- **IPA:** /pə ˈkæpɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (adj/adv):** For each person; calculated by dividing a total by the population.
  - *Example:* **Per capita** income is often used to compare living standards.

### downgrade
- **IPA:** /ˌdaʊnˈɡreɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To lower an official rating or estimate.
  - *Example:* Analysts **downgraded** the stock after weak results.

### output gap
- **IPA:** /ˈaʊtpʊt ɡæp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The difference between what an economy produces and what it could produce at full capacity.
  - *Example:* A large **output gap** points to spare capacity and low inflation.

### consumer confidence
- **IPA:** /kənˌsjuːmə ˈkɒnfɪdəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** How optimistic households feel about the economy and their finances.
  - *Example:* **Consumer confidence** dropped as energy bills rose.

### purchasing manager index
- **IPA:** /ˈpɜːtʃəsɪŋ ˌmænɪdʒər ˌɪndeks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A monthly survey-based measure of activity in manufacturing or services.
  - *Example:* A **purchasing manager index** above 50 signals expansion.

### market forces
- **IPA:** /ˈmɑːkɪt ˌfɔːsɪz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The effects of supply and demand that set prices without government control.
  - *Example:* Rents in the area are driven purely by **market forces**.

### free market
- **IPA:** /ˌfriː ˈmɑːkɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An economic system in which prices are set by open competition, not the state.
  - *Example:* Supporters of the **free market** opposed the price caps.

### boom and bust
- **IPA:** /ˌbuːm ən ˈbʌst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A repeated cycle of rapid growth followed by sharp decline.
  - *Example:* The construction sector is prone to **boom and bust**.

### real terms
- **IPA:** /ˌrɪəl ˈtɜːmz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Values adjusted to remove the effect of inflation, allowing fair comparison over time.
  - *Example:* In **real terms**, wages were lower than ten years earlier.

### protectionism
- **IPA:** /prəˈtekʃənɪzəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The policy of shielding domestic industries from foreign competition with tariffs or quotas.
  - *Example:* Rising **protectionism** made it harder to sell into that market.

### free trade
- **IPA:** /ˌfriː ˈtreɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** International trade without tariffs, quotas, or other restrictions.
  - *Example:* The **free trade** agreement removed duties on most goods.

### economic cycle
- **IPA:** /ˌiːkəˈnɒmɪk ˈsaɪkl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The regular pattern of growth and decline that an economy passes through.
  - *Example:* The business plans for a full **economic cycle**, not just good years.
