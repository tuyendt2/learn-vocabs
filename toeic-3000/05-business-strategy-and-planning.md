### strategy
- **IPA:** /ˈstrætədʒi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A carefully planned set of actions to achieve a long-term aim.
  - *Example:* The five-year **strategy** focuses on online sales.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The skill of planning and directing large operations.
  - *Example:* She is strong on **strategy** but leaves the detail to others.

### tactic
- **IPA:** /ˈtæktɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A specific action or method used to achieve a short-term goal.
  - *Example:* Free samples were a **tactic** to win back lost customers.

### goal
- **IPA:** /ɡəʊl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An aim or result that a person or organisation works toward.
  - *Example:* The **goal** for this quarter is to cut returns by 10%.

### target
- **IPA:** /ˈtɑːɡɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A specific level or result that is aimed for.
  - *Example:* The team beat its sales **target** by a wide margin.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To direct effort or resources at a particular group or aim.
  - *Example:* The campaign **targets** small business owners.

### roadmap
- **IPA:** /ˈrəʊdmæp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A plan showing the steps and timing needed to reach a goal.
  - *Example:* The product **roadmap** runs to the end of next year.

### blueprint
- **IPA:** /ˈbluːprɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A detailed plan of how something should be done.
  - *Example:* The report is a **blueprint** for expanding into new regions.

### framework
- **IPA:** /ˈfreɪmwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A basic structure of ideas or rules used to plan or judge something.
  - *Example:* Decisions are made within a clear **framework** of company values.

### priority
- **IPA:** /praɪˈɒrəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Something treated as more important than other things.
  - *Example:* Safety is the first **priority** on every site.

### focus
- **IPA:** /ˈfəʊkəs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The main area of attention or activity.
  - *Example:* This year's **focus** is customer retention.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To give attention and effort to one thing.
  - *Example:* We should **focus** on the products that actually make money.

### direction
- **IPA:** /dəˈrekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The general course or path an organisation is taking.
  - *Example:* Staff wanted clearer **direction** from the new leadership.

### mission
- **IPA:** /ˈmɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The central purpose or most important aim of an organisation.
  - *Example:* Affordable healthcare is the charity's **mission**.

### timeline
- **IPA:** /ˈtaɪmlaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A plan showing when the stages of a project will happen.
  - *Example:* The **timeline** shows launch in September.

### rollout
- **IPA:** /ˈrəʊlaʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The introduction of a new product, system, or plan, often in stages.
  - *Example:* The **rollout** starts with three pilot stores.

### execution
- **IPA:** /ˌeksɪˈkjuːʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The carrying out of a plan or task.
  - *Example:* The idea was sound, but the **execution** was rushed.

### implementation
- **IPA:** /ˌɪmplɪmenˈteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of putting a decision or plan into effect.
  - *Example:* **Implementation** of the new system took three months.

### alignment
- **IPA:** /əˈlaɪnmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Agreement between different plans, teams, or goals so they support each other.
  - *Example:* There is now full **alignment** between sales and production targets.

### positioning
- **IPA:** /pəˈzɪʃənɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The way a company presents its product relative to competitors in the market.
  - *Example:* The **positioning** is premium quality at a mid-range price.

### differentiation
- **IPA:** /ˌdɪfəˌrenʃiˈeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of making a product clearly different from those of competitors.
  - *Example:* Fast repairs are the company's main point of **differentiation**.

### competitive edge
- **IPA:** /kəmˌpetətɪv ˈedʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An advantage that lets a company do better than its rivals.
  - *Example:* Local warehousing gives them a **competitive edge** on delivery.

### value proposition
- **IPA:** /ˈvæljuː ˌprɒpəzɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The reason a customer should choose a product; the benefit it promises.
  - *Example:* The **value proposition** is simple: same quality, half the price.

### core competency
- **IPA:** /ˌkɔː ˈkɒmpɪtənsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A central strength or ability that gives a company its advantage.
  - *Example:* Precision engineering is the firm's **core competency**.

### capability
- **IPA:** /ˌkeɪpəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The power or ability to do something.
  - *Example:* The plant lacks the **capability** to make the larger units.

### resource allocation
- **IPA:** /rɪˈsɔːs ˌæləˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The way money, staff, and equipment are divided among activities.
  - *Example:* Poor **resource allocation** left the key project understaffed.

### contingency
- **IPA:** /kənˈtɪndʒənsi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A plan or provision for something that might happen in the future.
  - *Example:* The budget includes a **contingency** for price rises.

### scenario
- **IPA:** /səˈnɑːriəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A description of a possible future situation, used for planning.
  - *Example:* Planners modelled a worst-case **scenario** of a six-month downturn.

### outlook
- **IPA:** /ˈaʊtlʊk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The likely future situation, based on current information.
  - *Example:* The **outlook** for the sector is cautiously positive.

### trajectory
- **IPA:** /trəˈdʒektəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The path of development or change that something is following.
  - *Example:* Sales are on an upward **trajectory** after two flat years.

### growth plan
- **IPA:** /ˈɡrəʊθ plæn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A plan setting out how a business will get bigger.
  - *Example:* The **growth plan** targets three new countries by 2028.

### expansion
- **IPA:** /ɪkˈspænʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of becoming larger in size, number, or scope.
  - *Example:* Rapid **expansion** put a strain on the supply chain.

### diversification
- **IPA:** /daɪˌvɜːsɪfɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of increasing the range of products, services, or markets.
  - *Example:* **Diversification** into services reduced the firm's reliance on hardware.

### consolidation
- **IPA:** /kənˌsɒlɪˈdeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of combining several things into a single, stronger whole.
  - *Example:* The year was about **consolidation** rather than new ventures.

### penetration
- **IPA:** /ˌpenɪˈtreɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The extent to which a product has been sold within a market.
  - *Example:* Market **penetration** in rural areas is still low.

### market entry
- **IPA:** /ˈmɑːkɪt ˌentri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of starting to sell in a new market.
  - *Example:* A local partner made **market entry** in Japan much faster.

### first-mover
- **IPA:** /ˌfɜːst ˈmuːvə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that is first to sell a new type of product, gaining an early advantage.
  - *Example:* As a **first-mover**, it set the standard others had to follow.

### disruptor
- **IPA:** /dɪsˈrʌptə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company whose new approach greatly changes an existing market.
  - *Example:* The app became a **disruptor** in the taxi business.

### incumbent
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈkʌmbənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An established company that already holds a strong position in a market.
  - *Example:* Small rivals struggle against the two **incumbents**.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** The person currently holding an office or post.
  - *Example:* The **incumbent** was re-elected as chair.

### headwind
- **IPA:** /ˈhedwɪnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A force or condition that makes progress harder.
  - *Example:* Rising fuel costs are a serious **headwind** for the airline.

### tailwind
- **IPA:** /ˈteɪlwɪnd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A condition that helps progress or growth.
  - *Example:* A weak currency was a **tailwind** for exporters.

### economies of scale
- **IPA:** /ɪˌkɒnəmiz əv ˈskeɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Cost savings gained by producing goods in large quantities.
  - *Example:* Merging the two plants brought **economies of scale**.

### break-even
- **IPA:** /ˌbreɪk ˈiːvn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The point at which income equals costs, with no profit or loss.
  - *Example:* The new store reached **break-even** in eight months.

### key result
- **IPA:** /ˌkiː rɪˈzʌlt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A measurable outcome used to track progress toward an objective.
  - *Example:* One **key result** is a customer rating above 4.5.

### performance indicator
- **IPA:** /pəˈfɔːməns ˌɪndɪkeɪtə/

  - *Example:* On-time delivery is our main **performance indicator**.

### review cycle
- **IPA:** /rɪˈvjuː ˌsaɪkl/

  - *Example:* The plan is checked against results each quarterly **review cycle**.

### pivot

  - *Example:* The startup **pivoted** from selling software to selling data.
- **Meaning 2 (n):** A significant change of strategy.
  - *Example:* The **pivot** to subscriptions saved the business.

### iterate

### wind down

### long-term

### mid-term
- **IPA:** /ˌmɪd ˈtɜːm/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to a period between the near future and the distant future.
  - *Example:* The **mid-term** plan covers the next two to three years.

### top-down
- **IPA:** /ˌtɒp ˈdaʊn/

### bottom-up
- **IPA:** /ˌbɒtəm ˈʌp/

### stakeholder mapping

### gap analysis

### planning horizon

### roadmapping
