# 20 — Agriculture & Food Supply

### agriculture
- **IPA:** /ˈæɡrɪkʌltʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The practice of farming, including growing crops and raising animals.
  - *Example:* **Agriculture** employs a third of the region's [workforce](../toeic-600/04-human-resources-and-recruitment.md#21-workforce).

### farming
- **IPA:** /ˈfɑːmɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The business or activity of running a farm.
  - *Example:* Dairy **farming** is the main use of land in the valley.

### cultivation
- **IPA:** /ˌkʌltɪˈveɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The preparing and use of land for growing crops.
  - *Example:* The hillsides were cleared for the **cultivation** of coffee.

### arable
- **IPA:** /ˈærəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of land) Suitable for or used for growing crops.
  - *Example:* Only a fifth of the country's land is **arable**.

### pasture
- **IPA:** /ˈpɑːstʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Grassland used for feeding grazing animals.
  - *Example:* The cattle were moved to fresh **pasture**.

### livestock
- **IPA:** /ˈlaɪvstɒk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Animals kept on a farm, such as cattle, sheep, and pigs.
  - *Example:* Feed prices squeezed **livestock** farmers hard.

### poultry
- **IPA:** /ˈpəʊltri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Birds such as chickens and turkeys kept for eggs and meat.
  - *Example:* A disease outbreak forced a cull of the **poultry**.

### dairy
- **IPA:** /ˈdeəri/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Relating to milk and products made from it.
  - *Example:* The **dairy** herd is milked twice a day.

### herd
- **IPA:** /hɜːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large group of animals of one kind kept together.
  - *Example:* The farmer expanded the **herd** to 200 cows.

### flock
- **IPA:** /flɒk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A group of birds or sheep kept together.
  - *Example:* The **flock** was sheared in early summer.

### crop
- **IPA:** /krɒp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A plant grown in large amounts for food or sale.
  - *Example:* Wheat is the main **crop** on this farm.

### harvest
- **IPA:** /ˈhɑːvɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The gathering of ripe crops, or the crops gathered.
  - *Example:* Heavy rain ruined part of the **harvest**.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To gather a crop.
  - *Example:* The grapes are **harvested** by hand in September.

### sowing
- **IPA:** /ˈsəʊɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of planting seeds in the ground.
  - *Example:* Spring **sowing** was delayed by wet fields.

### planting
- **IPA:** /ˈplɑːntɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The act of putting plants or seeds in the ground to grow.
  - *Example:* **Planting** of the new [orchard](20-agriculture-and-food-supply.md#orchard) took a full week.

### irrigation
- **IPA:** /ˌɪrɪˈɡeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The supply of water to land or crops through channels or pipes.
  - *Example:* Drip **irrigation** cut water use by half.

### fertilizer
- **IPA:** /ˈfɜːtəlaɪzə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A substance added to soil to help plants grow.
  - *Example:* The price of **fertilizer** rose with the cost of gas.

### pesticide
- **IPA:** /ˈpestɪsaɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A chemical used to kill insects and other pests that damage crops.
  - *Example:* Tests found no **pesticide** residue on the sample.

### herbicide
- **IPA:** /ˈhɜːbɪsaɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A chemical used to kill unwanted plants, especially weeds.
  - *Example:* **Herbicide** was sprayed between the rows before [planting](20-agriculture-and-food-supply.md#planting).

### tillage
- **IPA:** /ˈtɪlɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The preparation of land for growing crops by ploughing and digging.
  - *Example:* Reduced **tillage** helps keep moisture in the soil.

### ploughing
- **IPA:** /ˈplaʊɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The turning over of soil with a plough before planting.
  - *Example:* Autumn **ploughing** breaks up the heavy ground.

### rotation
- **IPA:** /rəʊˈteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The practice of growing different crops in a field in a planned order.
  - *Example:* Crop **rotation** keeps the soil healthy and reduces pests.

### fallow
- **IPA:** /ˈfæləʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of land) Left unplanted for a period to recover its fertility.
  - *Example:* One field is left **fallow** each year.

### greenhouse
- **IPA:** /ˈɡriːnhaʊs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A glass or plastic building for growing plants in controlled conditions.
  - *Example:* Tomatoes are grown in the **greenhouse** all year round.

### horticulture
- **IPA:** /ˈhɔːtɪkʌltʃə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The science and business of growing fruit, vegetables, and flowers.
  - *Example:* She studied **horticulture** before taking over the nursery.

### orchard
- **IPA:** /ˈɔːtʃəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A piece of land planted with fruit trees.
  - *Example:* The apple **orchard** yields around 40 tonnes a year.

### vineyard
- **IPA:** /ˈvɪnjəd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A piece of land where grapes are grown for wine.
  - *Example:* Frost in May damaged half the **vineyard**.

### silo
- **IPA:** /ˈsaɪləʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tall tower or pit for storing grain or animal feed.
  - *Example:* The grain is dried and blown into the **silo**.

### granary
- **IPA:** /ˈɡrænəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A building for storing threshed grain.
  - *Example:* The old stone **granary** was converted into offices.

### feedstock
- **IPA:** /ˈfiːdstɒk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Raw material supplied to a machine or processing plant.
  - *Example:* Maize is the main **feedstock** for the ethanol plant.

### fodder
- **IPA:** /ˈfɒdə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Food, such as hay or straw, for cattle and other farm animals.
  - *Example:* A dry summer left little **fodder** for the winter.

### grazing
- **IPA:** /ˈɡreɪzɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Land where animals feed on growing grass; the act of feeding on it.
  - *Example:* The upland **grazing** is used only in summer.

### free-range
- **IPA:** /ˌfriː ˈreɪndʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** (of animals) Kept in natural conditions with freedom to move, not in cages.
  - *Example:* **Free-range** eggs sell at a higher price.

### organic
- **IPA:** /ɔːˈɡænɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Produced without artificial chemicals such as pesticides or fertilizers.
  - *Example:* The farm went fully **organic** three years ago.

### agronomy
- **IPA:** /əˈɡrɒnəmi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The science of soil management and crop production.
  - *Example:* An **agronomy** adviser recommended a different seed variety.

### soil
- **IPA:** /sɔɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The top layer of earth in which plants grow.
  - *Example:* The **soil** here is sandy and drains quickly.

### topsoil
- **IPA:** /ˈtɒpsɔɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The upper, most fertile layer of soil.
  - *Example:* Heavy [machinery](../toeic-600/10-manufacturing-and-production.md#12-machinery) had compacted the **topsoil**.

### erosion
- **IPA:** /ɪˈrəʊʒn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The gradual wearing away of soil or rock by wind or water.
  - *Example:* [Planting](20-agriculture-and-food-supply.md#planting) hedges slowed the **erosion** of the hillside fields.

### drought
- **IPA:** /draʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A long period with little or no rain.
  - *Example:* Two years of **drought** cut the region's grain output.

### blight
- **IPA:** /blaɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A plant disease that quickly kills or damages crops.
  - *Example:* Potato **blight** spread fast in the warm, wet weather.

### pest
- **IPA:** /pest/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An insect or animal that damages crops, food, or livestock.
  - *Example:* Beetles are the worst **pest** in the bean fields.

### infestation
- **IPA:** /ˌɪnfeˈsteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The presence of a large number of pests in a place.
  - *Example:* An **infestation** of mice got into the grain store.

### cooperative
- **IPA:** /kəʊˈɒpərətɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A business owned and run jointly by its members, who share the profits.
  - *Example:* Local farmers sell their milk through a **cooperative**.

### smallholder
- **IPA:** /ˈsmɔːlhəʊldə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A farmer who works a small piece of land.
  - *Example:* Most coffee is grown by **smallholders** with a few hectares each.

### agribusiness
- **IPA:** /ˈæɡrɪbɪznəs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Large-scale farming and the industries connected with it.
  - *Example:* **Agribusiness** now controls most of the country's grain trade.

### cold chain
- **IPA:** /ˈkəʊld tʃeɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An unbroken series of refrigerated steps that keeps perishable goods cold from farm to shop.
  - *Example:* A break in the **cold chain** spoiled a whole [container](21-transportation-and-shipping.md#container) of fish.

### shelf life
- **IPA:** /ˈʃelf laɪf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The length of time a product stays usable or safe to sell.
  - *Example:* The new packaging doubled the bread's **shelf life**.

### processing plant
- **IPA:** /ˈprəʊsesɪŋ plɑːnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A factory where raw food is cleaned, cut, cooked, or packed.
  - *Example:* The tomatoes go straight from the field to the **processing plant**.

### packing house
- **IPA:** /ˈpækɪŋ haʊs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A building where fruit and vegetables are sorted, graded, and packed.
  - *Example:* Fruit is chilled at the **packing house** within an hour of picking.

### distributor
- **IPA:** /dɪˈstrɪbjətə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that buys goods and supplies them to shops or other businesses.
  - *Example:* A national **distributor** [delivers](06-projects-and-deadlines.md#deliver) the produce to supermarkets.

### wholesaler
- **IPA:** /ˈhəʊlseɪlə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A business that buys goods in bulk and sells them on to retailers.
  - *Example:* Restaurants buy their vegetables from a market **wholesaler**.

### commodity price
- **IPA:** /kəˈmɒdəti praɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The market price of a basic traded good such as grain, sugar, or coffee.
  - *Example:* A poor [harvest](20-agriculture-and-food-supply.md#harvest) abroad pushed the **commodity price** up sharply.

### futures contract
- **IPA:** /ˈfjuːtʃəz ˌkɒntrækt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An agreement to buy or sell a crop at a fixed price on a future date.
  - *Example:* The farmer used a **futures contract** to lock in this year's wheat price.

### food security
- **IPA:** /ˈfuːd sɪˌkjʊərəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The state of having reliable access to enough safe, affordable food.
  - *Example:* [Droughts](20-agriculture-and-food-supply.md#drought) abroad raised concerns about national **food security**.

### traceability
- **IPA:** /ˌtreɪsəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ability to track a food product back through every stage of its supply.
  - *Example:* [Barcodes](16-retail-and-store-operations.md#barcode) give full **traceability** from the field to the shelf.

### food certification
- **IPA:** /ˈfuːd ˌsɜːtɪfɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Official approval that a food product meets a defined standard, such as organic or fair trade.
  - *Example:* **Food certification** lets the farm sell into [premium](../toeic-600/18-employee-benefits-and-insurance.md#2-premium) markets.

### labelling
- **IPA:** /ˈleɪblɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The information printed on a product's packaging, such as contents and origin.
  - *Example:* New rules require clearer **labelling** of [allergens](38-food-cooking-and-dining-out.md#allergen).

### contamination
- **IPA:** /kənˌtæmɪˈneɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The presence of harmful or unwanted substances in food, water, or another material.
  - *Example:* **Contamination** with bacteria led to a national recall.

### recall
- **IPA:** /rɪˈkɔːl/ (v) · /ˈriːkɔːl/ (n)
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official request for customers to return a product because of a fault or danger.
  - *Example:* The **recall** covered every jar with a certain batch code.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To officially ask for a product to be returned.
  - *Example:* The company **recalled** the sauce after tests found glass fragments.

### spoilage
- **IPA:** /ˈspɔɪlɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of food going bad, or the amount lost this way.
  - *Example:* Better cold storage cut **spoilage** in the [warehouse](../toeic-600/11-logistics-shipping-and-warehousing.md#15-warehouse).

### preservation
- **IPA:** /ˌprezəˈveɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The treatment of food to stop it decaying.
  - *Example:* Salting is one of the oldest methods of **preservation**.

### refrigeration
- **IPA:** /rɪˌfrɪdʒəˈreɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The keeping of food cold to slow the growth of bacteria.
  - *Example:* The truck lost **refrigeration** for six hours on the motorway.

### freezing
- **IPA:** /ˈfriːzɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The preserving of food by keeping it below 0°C.
  - *Example:* **Freezing** the peas within hours of picking locks in the flavour.

### canning
- **IPA:** /ˈkænɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The preserving of food by sealing it in metal cans.
  - *Example:* The **canning** line runs 24 hours a day during the tomato season.

### pasteurization
- **IPA:** /ˌpɑːstʃəraɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The heating of a food or drink to kill harmful bacteria.
  - *Example:* **Pasteurization** makes the milk safe and extends its [shelf life](20-agriculture-and-food-supply.md#shelf-life).

### seasonal produce
- **IPA:** /ˌsiːzənl ˈprɒdjuːs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Fruit and vegetables available naturally only at certain times of year.
  - *Example:* The menu changes with the **seasonal produce**.

### farm-to-table
- **IPA:** /ˌfɑːm tə ˈteɪbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Describing food that goes directly from the farm to the consumer with few steps between.
  - *Example:* The restaurant [promotes](../toeic-600/08-marketing-and-advertising.md#7-promote) a **farm-to-table** approach with local [suppliers](../toeic-600/09-sales-and-purchasing.md#2-supplier).

### yield per hectare
- **IPA:** /ˌjiːld pər ˈhekteə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of crop produced from one hectare of land.
  - *Example:* Better seed raised the **yield per hectare** by 15%.

### mechanization
- **IPA:** /ˌmekənaɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The replacement of manual work with machines.
  - *Example:* **Mechanization** cut the number of workers needed at [harvest](20-agriculture-and-food-supply.md#harvest).

### farmer
- **IPA:** /ˈfɑːmə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who owns or manages a farm.
  - *Example:* Local **farmers** met to discuss the drop in milk prices.

### farmland
- **IPA:** /ˈfɑːmlænd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Land used or suitable for farming.
  - *Example:* Housing has spread onto what was once **farmland**.

### grain
- **IPA:** /ɡreɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The small hard seeds of cereal crops such as wheat, rice, and maize.
  - *Example:* The port handles millions of tonnes of **grain** a year.

### cash crop
- **IPA:** /ˈkæʃ krɒp/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A crop grown to be sold for money rather than used by the grower.
  - *Example:* Cotton is the region's main **cash crop**.

### growing season
- **IPA:** /ˈɡrəʊɪŋ ˌsiːzn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The part of the year when conditions allow crops to grow.
  - *Example:* A longer **growing season** let them fit in a second crop.

### crop yield
- **IPA:** /ˈkrɒp jiːld/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount of a crop produced from a given area of land.
  - *Example:* Fertiliser and [irrigation](20-agriculture-and-food-supply.md#irrigation) together doubled the **crop yield**.

### combine harvester
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒmbaɪn ˈhɑːvɪstə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large machine that cuts, threshes, and cleans grain in one pass.
  - *Example:* One **combine harvester** can clear a field in a few hours.
