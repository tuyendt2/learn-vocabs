# 07 — Data, Reports & Analysis

### data
- **IPA:** /ˈdeɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Facts and figures collected for reference or analysis.
  - *Example:* The **data** show a clear rise in weekend orders.

### dataset
- **IPA:** /ˈdeɪtəset/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A collection of related data treated as a single unit.
  - *Example:* We merged two **datasets** to compare regions.

### figure
- **IPA:** /ˈfɪɡə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A number representing a particular amount or value.
  - *Example:* The sales **figures** for March were disappointing.

### statistic
- **IPA:** /stəˈtɪstɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A single number that represents a fact about a large set of data.
  - *Example:* One **statistic** stood out: 70% of returns came from one product.

### metric
- **IPA:** /ˈmetrɪk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A standard measure used to track performance or progress.
  - *Example:* Response time is the **metric** the support team lives by.

### indicator
- **IPA:** /ˈɪndɪkeɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A sign or measurement that shows the state or level of something.
  - *Example:* Repeat orders are a good **indicator** of customer satisfaction.

### variable
- **IPA:** /ˈveəriəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A factor that can change and may affect a result.
  - *Example:* Price is only one **variable** in the demand model.
- **Meaning 2 (adj):** Likely to change; not constant.
  - *Example:* Demand is highly **variable** through the year.

### sample
- **IPA:** /ˈsɑːmpl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A small part of a group, chosen to represent the whole.
  - *Example:* The survey used a **sample** of 500 customers.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To take and study part of something.
  - *Example:* We **sampled** one box in every hundred.

### survey
- **IPA:** /ˈsɜːveɪ/ (n) · /səˈveɪ/ (v)
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A set of questions used to gather opinions or facts from many people.
  - *Example:* The staff **survey** goes out every autumn.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To ask a group of people questions to collect information.
  - *Example:* We **surveyed** users after the redesign.

### questionnaire
- **IPA:** /ˌkwestʃəˈneə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A printed or online set of questions for gathering data.
  - *Example:* The **questionnaire** takes about five minutes to complete.

### respondent
- **IPA:** /rɪˈspɒndənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who answers a survey or questionnaire.
  - *Example:* Most **respondents** wanted longer opening hours.

### poll
- **IPA:** /pəʊl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A survey of opinion on a specific question.
  - *Example:* A quick **poll** showed the team preferred the earlier start.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To ask people for their opinion in a survey.
  - *Example:* We **polled** staff on the new canteen menu.

### census
- **IPA:** /ˈsensəs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official count or survey of a whole population.
  - *Example:* The **census** data help us plan store locations.

### trend
- **IPA:** /trend/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A general direction of change over time.
  - *Example:* The **trend** in complaints has been downward all year.

### pattern
- **IPA:** /ˈpætn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A regular, repeated way in which something happens.
  - *Example:* There is a clear weekly **pattern** in website traffic.

### correlation
- **IPA:** /ˌkɒrəˈleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A connection between two things in which they change together.
  - *Example:* There is a strong **correlation** between training and output.

### deviation
- **IPA:** /ˌdiːviˈeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount by which a value differs from a standard or average.
  - *Example:* A large **deviation** from the forecast triggers a review.

### anomaly
- **IPA:** /əˈnɒməli/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Something that differs from what is normal or expected.
  - *Example:* The spike in June was an **anomaly** caused by a one-off order.

### outlier
- **IPA:** /ˈaʊtlaɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A value that is much higher or lower than the rest of the data.
  - *Example:* We removed one **outlier** before calculating the average.

### average
- **IPA:** /ˈævərɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A single value found by adding amounts and dividing by how many there are.
  - *Example:* The **average** wait time fell to four minutes.
- **Meaning 2 (adj):** Typical; of the usual level or standard.
  - *Example:* An **average** day brings about 300 visitors.

### mean
- **IPA:** /miːn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The mathematical average of a set of numbers.
  - *Example:* The **mean** score was 72 out of 100.

### median
- **IPA:** /ˈmiːdiən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The middle value in a list of numbers arranged in order.
  - *Example:* The **median** salary is lower than the mean because of a few high earners.

### margin of error
- **IPA:** /ˌmɑːdʒɪn əv ˈerə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The amount by which a measured or reported result may differ from the true value.
  - *Example:* The **margin of error** on the poll is three percentage points.

### percentage
- **IPA:** /pəˈsentɪdʒ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An amount expressed as a part of a hundred.
  - *Example:* A high **percentage** of orders now come from the app.

### ratio
- **IPA:** /ˈreɪʃiəʊ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A relationship between two amounts, showing how many times one contains the other.
  - *Example:* The staff-to-customer **ratio** is one to twenty.

### proportion
- **IPA:** /prəˈpɔːʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A part or share of a whole.
  - *Example:* A large **proportion** of the budget goes on rent.

### breakdown
- **IPA:** /ˈbreɪkdaʊn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A division of totals into separate parts for analysis.
  - *Example:* The report gives a **breakdown** of costs by department.

### segment
- **IPA:** /ˈseɡmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A part of a larger group, market, or set of data.
  - *Example:* The over-50s **segment** is growing fastest.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To divide something into parts.
  - *Example:* We **segment** customers by how often they buy.

### category
- **IPA:** /ˈkætəɡəri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A group of things that share the same features.
  - *Example:* Complaints fall into three main **categories**.

### baseline
- **IPA:** /ˈbeɪslaɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A starting measurement used for later comparison.
  - *Example:* We set a **baseline** before the process change.

### threshold
- **IPA:** /ˈθreʃhəʊld/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A level or point at which something starts to happen or take effect.
  - *Example:* If errors pass the **threshold**, the line stops automatically.

### forecast
- **IPA:** /ˈfɔːkɑːst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A statement of what is likely to happen, based on current data.
  - *Example:* The demand **forecast** was revised down after the survey.

### projection
- **IPA:** /prəˈdʒekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An estimate of a future amount based on present trends.
  - *Example:* The five-year **projection** assumes steady growth.

### estimate
- **IPA:** /ˈestɪmət/ (n) · /ˈestɪmeɪt/ (v)
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An approximate calculation of a value.
  - *Example:* Our best **estimate** is 8,000 units a month.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To calculate something roughly.
  - *Example:* Analysts **estimate** the market at $2 billion.

### extrapolate
- **IPA:** /ɪkˈstræpəleɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To estimate unknown values by extending a known trend.
  - *Example:* If we **extrapolate** current growth, we hit capacity by 2027.

### interpolate
- **IPA:** /ɪnˈtɜːpəleɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To estimate a value that falls between two known values.
  - *Example:* We **interpolated** the missing month from the ones on either side.

### aggregate
- **IPA:** /ˈæɡrɪɡət/ (adj/n) · /ˈæɡrɪɡeɪt/ (v)
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Formed by adding several amounts together; total.
  - *Example:* **Aggregate** demand across all stores was flat.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To combine separate items into a whole.
  - *Example:* The tool **aggregates** feedback from every channel.

### collate
- **IPA:** /kəˈleɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To collect information from different sources and arrange it in order.
  - *Example:* She **collated** the figures from all branches into a single report.

### tabulate
- **IPA:** /ˈtæbjuleɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To arrange data in rows and columns.
  - *Example:* The results were **tabulated** by region and month.

### chart
- **IPA:** /tʃɑːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A diagram that presents information visually.
  - *Example:* The **chart** makes the seasonal dip obvious.
- **Meaning 2 (v):** To record or follow progress over time.
  - *Example:* The report **charts** five years of steady decline.

### graph
- **IPA:** /ɡrɑːf/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A drawing that shows the relationship between two sets of numbers.
  - *Example:* The **graph** plots revenue against advertising spend.

### bar chart
- **IPA:** /ˈbɑː tʃɑːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A chart that uses rectangular bars to compare quantities.
  - *Example:* A **bar chart** compares sales across the four regions.

### pie chart
- **IPA:** /ˈpaɪ tʃɑːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A circular chart divided into slices to show proportions.
  - *Example:* The **pie chart** shows how the budget is split.

### spreadsheet
- **IPA:** /ˈspredʃiːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A computer file that organises numbers and formulas in a grid.
  - *Example:* All the raw numbers live in one **spreadsheet**.

### pivot table
- **IPA:** /ˈpɪvət ˌteɪbl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tool that summarises and reorganises data in a spreadsheet.
  - *Example:* A **pivot table** quickly showed sales by month and product.

### visualization
- **IPA:** /ˌvɪʒuəlaɪˈzeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A visual representation of data, such as a chart or map.
  - *Example:* A simple **visualization** made the trend clear to the board.

### dashboard
- **IPA:** /ˈdæʃbɔːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A screen showing key measures and charts at a glance.
  - *Example:* The sales **dashboard** updates every hour.

### summary
- **IPA:** /ˈsʌməri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short statement of the main points of something.
  - *Example:* Put a one-page **summary** at the front of the report.

### executive summary
- **IPA:** /ɪɡˌzekjətɪv ˈsʌməri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A short section at the start of a report giving its key findings and recommendations.
  - *Example:* Most directors read only the **executive summary**.

### findings
- **IPA:** /ˈfaɪndɪŋz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The information or conclusions reached by a study or investigation.
  - *Example:* The **findings** support a move to weekly deliveries.

### insight
- **IPA:** /ˈɪnsaɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A clear, deep understanding of a situation gained from data or experience.
  - *Example:* The main **insight** was that price, not choice, drove the switch.

### takeaway
- **IPA:** /ˈteɪkəweɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A key point or lesson to remember from information presented.
  - *Example:* The **takeaway** is that our busiest hour is understaffed.

### implication
- **IPA:** /ˌɪmplɪˈkeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A likely effect or consequence of something.
  - *Example:* The data have clear **implications** for staffing.

### methodology
- **IPA:** /ˌmeθəˈdɒlədʒi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The set of methods and principles used to carry out a study.
  - *Example:* An appendix explains the survey **methodology**.

### assumption
- **IPA:** /əˈsʌmpʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Something accepted as true without proof, used as a basis for reasoning.
  - *Example:* The forecast rests on an **assumption** of 3% price growth.

### limitation
- **IPA:** /ˌlɪmɪˈteɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A weakness or restriction that reduces how far results can be trusted.
  - *Example:* A key **limitation** was the small sample size.

### bias
- **IPA:** /ˈbaɪəs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An error caused by favouring one outcome or group over others.
  - *Example:* Asking only loyal customers introduced **bias**.

### reliable
- **IPA:** /rɪˈlaɪəbl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Giving the same result each time; able to be trusted.
  - *Example:* The meter gives **reliable** readings once it is warmed up.

### valid
- **IPA:** /ˈvælɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Based on sound reasoning or evidence; logically correct.
  - *Example:* The comparison is only **valid** if the two groups are similar.

### accurate
- **IPA:** /ˈækjərət/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Correct in all details; free from error.
  - *Example:* Double-check that the totals are **accurate** before sending.

### precise
- **IPA:** /prɪˈsaɪs/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Exact and clearly stated; measured to a fine degree.
  - *Example:* We need a **precise** figure, not a rough range.

### raw data
- **IPA:** /ˌrɔː ˈdeɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Information that has been collected but not yet processed or analysed.
  - *Example:* The **raw data** is stored before any cleaning is done.

### quarterly report
- **IPA:** /ˈkwɔːtəli rɪˌpɔːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A report produced every three months on performance or results.
  - *Example:* The **quarterly report** goes to shareholders in May.

### annual report
- **IPA:** /ˌænjuəl rɪˈpɔːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A yearly document summarising a company's activities and finances.
  - *Example:* The **annual report** includes the audited accounts.

### white paper
- **IPA:** /ˈwaɪt ˌpeɪpə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An authoritative report that explains an issue and proposes a solution.
  - *Example:* The **white paper** argues for a shift to electric vans.

### case study
- **IPA:** /ˈkeɪs ˌstʌdi/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A detailed examination of one example to draw wider lessons.
  - *Example:* The **case study** describes how one store cut waste by half.

### appendix
- **IPA:** /əˈpendɪks/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A section at the end of a document with extra detail or supporting material.
  - *Example:* The full tables are in **Appendix** B.

### footnote
- **IPA:** /ˈfʊtnəʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A note at the bottom of a page giving extra information or a source.
  - *Example:* A **footnote** explains how the index was calculated.

### cross-reference
- **IPA:** /ˌkrɒs ˈrefrəns/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A note directing the reader to related information elsewhere.
  - *Example:* Each figure has a **cross-reference** to its source table.

### year-on-year
- **IPA:** /ˌjɪər ɒn ˈjɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (adj/adv):** Compared with the same period in the previous year.
  - *Example:* Sales were up 6% **year-on-year**.

### quarter-on-quarter
- **IPA:** /ˌkwɔːtər ɒn ˈkwɔːtə/
- **Meaning 1 (adj/adv):** Compared with the immediately preceding three-month period.
  - *Example:* Costs fell slightly **quarter-on-quarter**.

### comparative
- **IPA:** /kəmˈpærətɪv/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Involving comparison between two or more things.
  - *Example:* The **comparative** table sets our prices against three rivals.

### longitudinal
- **IPA:** /ˌlɒndʒɪˈtjuːdɪnl/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Following the same subjects over a long period of time.
  - *Example:* A **longitudinal** study tracked the same 200 customers for five years.

### data point
- **IPA:** /ˈdeɪtə pɔɪnt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A single item or value within a set of data.
  - *Example:* One weak **data point** is not enough to change the plan.

### sampling
- **IPA:** /ˈsɑːmplɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The process of selecting a representative part of a group for study.
  - *Example:* Random **sampling** avoids favouring any one type of customer.
