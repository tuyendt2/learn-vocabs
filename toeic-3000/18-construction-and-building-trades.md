# 18 — Construction & Building Trades

### contractor
- **IPA:** /kənˈtræktə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person or company hired to carry out building work.
  - *Example:* The **contractor** promised to finish the roof before winter.

### subcontractor
- **IPA:** /ˌsʌbkənˈtræktə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A firm hired by the main contractor to do part of a job.
  - *Example:* The electrical work went to a specialist **subcontractor**.

### developer
- **IPA:** /dɪˈveləpə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A company that buys land and builds on it to sell or rent.
  - *Example:* The **developer** plans 300 homes on the old factory site.

### architect
- **IPA:** /ˈɑːkɪtekt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A person who designs buildings and oversees their construction.
  - *Example:* The **architect** revised the plans to add more daylight.

### surveyor
- **IPA:** /səˈveɪə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A professional who measures land and buildings and assesses their condition or value.
  - *Example:* The **surveyor** found damp in the basement wall.

### site foreman
- **IPA:** /ˌsaɪt ˈfɔːmən/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The person in charge of a group of workers on a building site.
  - *Example:* Report to the **site foreman** when you arrive.

### tradesperson
- **IPA:** /ˈtreɪdzpɜːsn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A skilled manual worker such as a plumber, electrician, or carpenter.
  - *Example:* Every **tradesperson** on site must show a valid safety card.

### labourer
- **IPA:** /ˈleɪbərə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A worker who does hard physical work that requires little special training.
  - *Example:* Two **labourers** cleared the rubble by hand.

### scaffolding
- **IPA:** /ˈskæfəldɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A temporary framework of poles and boards for workers to stand on.
  - *Example:* **Scaffolding** went up around the tower for the repairs.

### formwork
- **IPA:** /ˈfɔːmwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Temporary moulds into which wet concrete is poured until it sets.
  - *Example:* The **formwork** was removed once the slab had cured.

### foundation
- **IPA:** /faʊnˈdeɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The solid underground base on which a building is built.
  - *Example:* Heavy rain delayed the pouring of the **foundation**.

### footing
- **IPA:** /ˈfʊtɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The widened base of a foundation that spreads the load into the ground.
  - *Example:* Wider **footings** were needed because of the soft soil.

### slab
- **IPA:** /slæb/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large, flat, thick piece of concrete, used for floors or foundations.
  - *Example:* The ground-floor **slab** was poured in one day.

### girder
- **IPA:** /ˈɡɜːdə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large beam, usually of steel, that supports a structure.
  - *Example:* A crane lifted each steel **girder** into place.

### beam
- **IPA:** /biːm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A long, strong horizontal bar of wood or metal that supports weight.
  - *Example:* The old oak **beam** still carries the upper floor.

### joist
- **IPA:** /dʒɔɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** One of a series of parallel beams that support a floor or ceiling.
  - *Example:* The floorboards are nailed across the **joists**.

### rafter
- **IPA:** /ˈrɑːftə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** One of the sloping beams that form the framework of a roof.
  - *Example:* New **rafters** were fitted before the tiles went on.

### truss
- **IPA:** /trʌs/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A framework of beams forming a rigid structure, often used to support a roof.
  - *Example:* The roof **trusses** arrived ready-made and were craned into place.

### load-bearing wall
- **IPA:** /ˈləʊd ˌbeərɪŋ wɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A wall that carries the weight of the structure above it.
  - *Example:* You cannot remove a **load-bearing wall** without a supporting beam.

### partition
- **IPA:** /pɑːˈtɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A light interior wall that divides a space but carries no structural load.
  - *Example:* A glass **partition** split the office into two meeting rooms.

### façade
- **IPA:** /fəˈsɑːd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The front, outward-facing wall of a building.
  - *Example:* The historic **façade** was kept while the inside was rebuilt.

### cladding
- **IPA:** /ˈklædɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A protective or decorative outer covering on a building.
  - *Example:* The timber **cladding** needs re-oiling every few years.

### insulation
- **IPA:** /ˌɪnsjuˈleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Material used to stop heat, cold, or sound passing through a wall or roof.
  - *Example:* Extra roof **insulation** cut the heating bill by a quarter.

### rebar
- **IPA:** /ˈriːbɑː/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Steel bars placed inside concrete to strengthen it.
  - *Example:* The **rebar** was tied in a grid before the pour.

### concrete
- **IPA:** /ˈkɒŋkriːt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A hard building material made from cement, sand, stone, and water.
  - *Example:* The **concrete** must cure for a week before loading.

### mortar
- **IPA:** /ˈmɔːtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A paste of cement and sand used to bind bricks or stones together.
  - *Example:* The bricklayer mixed a fresh batch of **mortar**.

### masonry
- **IPA:** /ˈmeɪsənri/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Structure or work built from stone, brick, or concrete blocks.
  - *Example:* Cracks in the **masonry** were repaired before repainting.

### brickwork
- **IPA:** /ˈbrɪkwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The bricks in a wall or building, considered as a whole.
  - *Example:* The **brickwork** on the chimney needs repointing.

### plastering
- **IPA:** /ˈplɑːstərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The work of covering walls and ceilings with a smooth layer of plaster.
  - *Example:* **Plastering** the whole flat took two days to dry.

### rendering
- **IPA:** /ˈrendərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A coat of cement or plaster applied to an outside wall.
  - *Example:* The **rendering** was cracked and letting in water.

### drywall
- **IPA:** /ˈdraɪwɔːl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Board made of plaster between paper layers, used to build interior walls.
  - *Example:* The new partitions are simple **drywall** on a metal frame.

### framing
- **IPA:** /ˈfreɪmɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The wooden or metal skeleton that gives a building its shape.
  - *Example:* Once the **framing** is up, the building takes form quickly.

### roofing
- **IPA:** /ˈruːfɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The material used to cover a roof, or the work of covering it.
  - *Example:* The **roofing** contractor quoted for slate or metal.

### guttering
- **IPA:** /ˈɡʌtərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The channels along the edge of a roof that carry rainwater away.
  - *Example:* Blocked **guttering** caused water to run down the wall.

### glazing
- **IPA:** /ˈɡleɪzɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The glass in windows and doors, or the fitting of it.
  - *Example:* Double **glazing** cut the noise from the road.

### plumbing
- **IPA:** /ˈplʌmɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The system of pipes and fittings that carry water in a building.
  - *Example:* The old **plumbing** was replaced with plastic pipe.

### wiring
- **IPA:** /ˈwaɪərɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The system of electrical wires in a building.
  - *Example:* The **wiring** was rerouted to add more sockets.

### ductwork
- **IPA:** /ˈdʌktwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The system of channels that carry air for heating, cooling, or ventilation.
  - *Example:* The **ductwork** runs above the suspended ceiling.

### HVAC
- **IPA:** /ˈeɪtʃvæk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Heating, ventilation, and air conditioning: the system that controls indoor air.
  - *Example:* The **HVAC** system is serviced twice a year.

### excavation
- **IPA:** /ˌekskəˈveɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The digging out of earth to prepare a site for building.
  - *Example:* **Excavation** for the basement uncovered an old drain.

### groundwork
- **IPA:** /ˈɡraʊndwɜːk/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The preparation of the ground and foundations before a building goes up.
  - *Example:* **Groundwork** took longer than expected on the sloping site.

### backfill
- **IPA:** /ˈbækfɪl/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Material used to refill a hole or trench after work is done.
  - *Example:* The trench was closed with compacted **backfill**.

### demolition
- **IPA:** /ˌdeməˈlɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The deliberate destruction of a building.
  - *Example:* **Demolition** of the old wing cleared space for the extension.

### retrofit
- **IPA:** /ˈretrəʊfɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (v):** To add new features or systems to an existing building.
  - *Example:* The office was **retrofitted** with solar panels and better insulation.

### refurbishment
- **IPA:** /rɪˈfɜːbɪʃmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The renovation and redecoration of a building to bring it up to date.
  - *Example:* The hotel closed for a six-month **refurbishment**.

### snagging
- **IPA:** /ˈsnæɡɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The checking and fixing of small faults after building work is finished.
  - *Example:* **Snagging** turned up a stuck window and a cracked tile.

### working drawing
- **IPA:** /ˌwɜːkɪŋ ˈdrɔːɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A detailed scale drawing used on site to build from.
  - *Example:* The **working drawing** shows the pipe runs behind the walls.

### permit
- **IPA:** /ˈpɜːmɪt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official document giving permission to carry out work.
  - *Example:* No digging can start without a **permit** from the council.

### planning permission
- **IPA:** /ˈplænɪŋ pəˌmɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Official approval from a local authority to build or change a property.
  - *Example:* They waited eight months for **planning permission**.

### zoning
- **IPA:** /ˈzəʊnɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Rules that set out how land in an area may be used.
  - *Example:* **Zoning** on that street does not allow warehouses.

### building code
- **IPA:** /ˈbɪldɪŋ kəʊd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A set of legal rules for how buildings must be designed and constructed.
  - *Example:* The staircase was rebuilt to meet the **building code**.

### building inspection
- **IPA:** /ˈbɪldɪŋ ɪnˌspekʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An official visit to check that construction work meets the building code.
  - *Example:* The wiring passed **building inspection** on the first visit.

### site survey
- **IPA:** /ˈsaɪt ˌsɜːveɪ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** An examination and measurement of a piece of land before building.
  - *Example:* The **site survey** showed the ground would need extra drainage.

### bill of quantities
- **IPA:** /ˌbɪl əv ˈkwɒntətiz/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A document listing all the materials and labour needed for a building project, used for pricing.
  - *Example:* Contractors priced the job from the **bill of quantities**.

### tender bid
- **IPA:** /ˈtendə bɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A formal written offer to carry out building work for a stated price.
  - *Example:* Five firms submitted a **tender bid** for the school building.

### milestone payment
- **IPA:** /ˈmaɪlstəʊn ˌpeɪmənt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A payment made when a defined stage of a project is completed.
  - *Example:* The next **milestone payment** is due when the roof is watertight.

### retention money
- **IPA:** /rɪˈtenʃn ˌmʌni/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A portion of payment held back until faults are fixed after completion.
  - *Example:* The client kept 5% **retention money** for six months.

### defect liability
- **IPA:** /ˈdiːfekt laɪˌəˈbɪləti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A period after completion during which the contractor must fix faults for free.
  - *Example:* The **defect liability** period runs for twelve months from handover.

### site handover
- **IPA:** /ˌsaɪt ˈhændəʊvə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The point at which a finished building is formally passed to the client.
  - *Example:* Keys and manuals were given to the owner at **site handover**.

### commissioning
- **IPA:** /kəˈmɪʃənɪŋ/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The testing and setting up of a building's systems before they are used.
  - *Example:* **Commissioning** of the heating system took a week.

### practical completion
- **IPA:** /ˌpræktɪkl kəmˈpliːʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The point at which a building is finished enough for the client to use it, apart from minor faults.
  - *Example:* **Practical completion** was certified with a short list of items still to fix.

### health and safety
- **IPA:** /ˌhelθ ən ˈseɪfti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** Laws and practices to protect workers and the public from harm on site.
  - *Example:* A **health and safety** officer visits the site each week.

### hard hat
- **IPA:** /ˌhɑːd ˈhæt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A protective helmet worn on building sites.
  - *Example:* **Hard hats** must be worn beyond this point.

### hoist
- **IPA:** /hɔɪst/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A machine for lifting heavy loads or people up a building.
  - *Example:* Materials go to the upper floors on the **hoist**.

### crane
- **IPA:** /kreɪn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A tall machine with a long arm for lifting and moving heavy objects.
  - *Example:* A tower **crane** dominated the skyline for a year.

### excavator
- **IPA:** /ˈekskəveɪtə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A large machine with a bucket arm, used for digging.
  - *Example:* The **excavator** cleared the trench in an afternoon.

### bulldozer
- **IPA:** /ˈbʊldəʊzə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A powerful tracked vehicle with a broad blade for pushing earth.
  - *Example:* A **bulldozer** levelled the ground for the car park.

### prefabricated
- **IPA:** /ˌpriːˈfæbrɪkeɪtɪd/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Made in sections in a factory and assembled on site.
  - *Example:* **Prefabricated** wall panels cut the build time in half.

### modular
- **IPA:** /ˈmɒdjələ/
- **Meaning 1 (adj):** Built from standard units that can be combined in different ways.
  - *Example:* The school was built from **modular** classrooms craned into place.

### sustainable build
- **IPA:** /səˌsteɪnəbl ˈbɪld/

  - *Example:* The **sustainable build** used recycled steel and rainwater harvesting.

### load calculation
- **IPA:** /ˈləʊd ˌkælkjuˈleɪʃn/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A calculation of the weight and forces a structure must safely carry.
  - *Example:* The **load calculation** decided the size of every beam.

### structural integrity
- **IPA:** /ˌstrʌktʃərəl ɪnˈteɡrəti/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The ability of a structure to carry its loads without failing.
  - *Example:* Engineers confirmed the **structural integrity** of the old bridge.

### site manager
- **IPA:** /ˈsaɪt ˌmænɪdʒə/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The person responsible for running a construction site day to day.
  - *Example:* The **site manager** coordinates deliveries and the trades.

### snag item
- **IPA:** /ˈsnæɡ ˌaɪtəm/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** A single small defect on a list of things to fix in finished building work.
  - *Example:* The biggest **snag item** was a door that would not close properly.

### fit-out
- **IPA:** /ˈfɪt aʊt/
- **Meaning 1 (n):** The work of making the inside of a building ready for use, after the shell is built.
  - *Example:* The office **fit-out** included flooring, lighting, and desks.
